# iGaming Solutions: A Complete Guide to B2B Services for Operators

On launch day a player expects two things to work at once: the deposit and the game. Behind that moment sit several suppliers, including a payment gateway, an account system and a game studio such as [BGaming](https://bgaming.com/). B2B solutions for iGaming supply those pieces, and the deployment model you choose decides who keeps the connections working after launch.

## What Are iGaming Solutions?

A registered player is only the start. That player still needs a funded wallet and a game that loads when they press play. iGaming solutions are the software, infrastructure and operational services that connect registration, deposits and gameplay into one working environment for an online casino business or sportsbook.

**B2B services for iGaming cover far more than the platform itself.** Operators assemble a combination of technology and operational solutions around their own requirements, which usually includes:

- Platform development and player account management
- Game content and payment processing
- Compliance tooling and customer relationship management
- Analytics, reporting and hosting

An end-to-end iGaming platform coordinates these responsibilities in one place. The coordination can come from one broad supplier, from a set of specialists connected through APIs, or from both at once. The scope of the package sets the remaining work: **whatever it does not cover stays with the operator's own team**, from licensing to day-to-day player support.

## Why Operators Rely on Specialist B2B Providers

Every player session creates work the player never sees:

- Server capacity
- Game updates
- Payment reconciliation
- Regulatory reporting

In Great Britain, the [Gambling Commission](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/statistics-and-research/publication/industry-statistics-annual-report-financial-year-april-2024-to-march-2025) reported in November 2025 that licensed remote casino, betting and bingo generated £7.8 billion in gross gambling yield between April 2024 and March 2025, up 13.1% year on year. **That figure describes one regulated market.** It is separate from the worldwide iGaming B2B software market, which measures what operators spend on suppliers rather than what players wager.

**Specialist providers spread that work across many clients.** A platform vendor maintains servers, applies software updates and keeps integrations current once, and every operator on the platform benefits. The operator configures existing capabilities and adds integrated services instead of funding each build from scratch.

Outsourcing in this way frees budget and staff for the product and the players. It also brings flexibility: an operator can add a sportsbook, switch a payment provider or enter a new jurisdiction by connecting a maintained service rather than by scheduling a development project. In return the operator depends on the supplier's roadmap and support, which later sections examine.

## B2B vs B2C iGaming Software

Running a casino and supplying its software are different jobs with different customers. The simplest picture is a chain: a supplier serves an operator, and the operator serves players.

### Who the Software Serves

B2B iGaming providers build and license infrastructure to operators:

- Platforms
- Game content
- Payment connections
- Compliance tooling

**Their customer is a gambling business**, and their product is usually multi-tenant, with each client's data, configuration and branding kept separate on a shared core.

B2C iGaming software faces the player. It runs direct-to-consumer gaming sites and apps, handles registration, marketing, promotions and support, and carries the licensing and regulatory responsibility for the gambling activity. **Most B2C operators run on software bought from the B2B side of the gaming services industry**, so the two models are layered rather than competing.

### How Each Side Earns

The business models are linked but distinct. **Suppliers earn through licence fees, setup charges or a share of the operator's revenue.** Operators earn from the gambling activity itself and pay for player acquisition, retention and customer service.

A consumer-facing casino can buy its technology on a software-as-a-service basis, paying as it launches, operates and scales rather than funding a full build up front. Typical use cases follow the same split:

- B2B: platform licensing, game aggregation, payment orchestration, compliance tooling
- B2C: branded casino and sportsbook sites, mobile apps, loyalty programmes

## Types of B2B iGaming Solutions

Launch plans depend on how much technology your team owns. Operators configure an existing platform, connect chosen components or build their own. Each choice sets how much B2B infrastructure a supplier holds, how customisable the digital gambling experience is, and which ongoing charges the business model carries. The deployment models below cover the iGaming stack options for anyone planning to start an online casino.

### White Label Solutions

A white-label platform is a ready-made casino that a provider has already built, licensed and tested. The operator adds branding, chooses games and payment methods, and sets the configuration. Implementation shifts from development to setup, so technical work and business preparation such as marketing, banking and staffing can run in parallel.

**The shared platform core is both the advantage and the limit.** Because many operators run on the same code, the provider maintains and improves it for everyone, and launches can happen within weeks. For the same reason, changes to core functionality are rarely possible; the operator works within the features the provider offers.

Before signing, assess three things alongside the reduced initial development:

- Ongoing charges, usually a monthly fee plus a revenue share
- Dependency on the provider for updates, uptime and new integrations
- Licensing responsibilities in the proposed package

Licensing varies by package in white-label and turnkey solutions. A white-label arrangement can use a ready-made licensed product under the provider's licence. A turnkey arrangement can run the bundled technology under the operator's own licence. Under both, the [online casino launch steps](https://bgaming.com/articles/5-exact-steps-to-create-an-online-casino-website) from licensing to marketing stay with the operator. **White label fits a standard launch where speed and predictable budget outweigh product differentiation.**

### Bespoke Solutions

Some operators need product behaviour that no shared platform offers: a custom wallet model, an unusual bonus engine, a front end built around a specific brand. Bespoke platform development gives that control over features, interfaces and architecture, because the operator's team or a contracted developer writes the software.

**The delivery team owns planning, engineering and quality assurance during the build, then continuing maintenance after launch.** Bugs, security patches, scaling and regulatory changes become the operator's work to schedule and fund.

**Implementation time follows the scope.** A narrow product with few integrations can move quickly, while a multi-brand platform with a sportsbook and several jurisdictions takes far longer and costs more at each step. The sequence usually runs:

1. Requirements and architecture
2. Design and development
3. Integration with games, payments and compliance services
4. Testing and regulatory preparation

Owning the platform does not mean building everything inside it. External content remains compatible with custom ownership, and BGaming supports direct integration with custom-built platforms alongside its platform-powered options. Bespoke suits operators with a specific product vision, the budget to fund it and the engineering capacity to maintain it for years.

### Modular Solutions

Suppose an operator wants to add a new game studio to a live casino. On a modular platform that is a single integration: the new supplier connects through an API while accounts, payments and the rest of the stack stay as they are. The same mechanism lets the operator replace a payment provider or a CRM without rebuilding the platform.

**Modular architecture means choosing components separately and connecting them through APIs.** Games, payments, identity checks, bonuses and reporting each come from the supplier that fits the market best, and each can expand or be swapped on its own. The operator keeps control over the user experience, since the front end is its own.

That flexibility carries a management cost. Shared account and transaction data flow across several suppliers, so someone must own:

- Integration planning and testing for each new component
- Monitoring of balances, bets and payments across systems
- Support ownership when a fault spans two vendors

**Delivery therefore requires planning, testing and monitoring, and operators usually need an in-house integration team.** The guide to [casino API integration](https://bgaming.com/articles/api-integration-casino-games-what-do-you-need-to-know) explains how those connections work in practice. Modular suits iGaming operators who want selective expansion or replacement over time and can sustain the technical oversight involved.

### Player Account Management (PAM)

A player's deposit limit, wallet balance and self-exclusion status must follow the same account whether the player logs in from a phone or a desktop. **PAM is the system that keeps identity, wallet and session consistent from registration through authentication to account support.**

In practice a PAM handles:

- Registration and player profiles
- Wallets, balances and transaction history
- Sessions and authentication
- KYC and anti-money laundering workflows
- Multi-brand support and responsible gaming controls

**Centralised records are what make controls enforceable.** When every bet, deposit and bonus touches the same account record, staff can apply rules consistently rather than reconciling across systems. Evaluate a PAM on whether its back office gives support, compliance and finance teams secure, role-based access to that record, and whether it logs who changed what.

Configurable capabilities to look for include identity checks, risk rules, brand management, deposit limits and self-exclusion. Account access is also where fraud and takeover attempts concentrate, so pair PAM evaluation with [player-account security](https://bgaming.com/articles/online-gambling-security-the-best-tips-for-gaming-operators) measures such as multi-factor authentication, encryption and activity monitoring.

### Payment Systems

The payment method a player chooses sets waiting time and minimum deposit. [Betfair's guidance](https://support.betfair.com/app/answers/detail/10271-instant-bank-transfers--faqs/) for GBP and EUR accounts shows the gap. Standard bank deposits and withdrawals take one to five days. A first Pay by Bank deposit can take up to 90 minutes, and later deposits and withdrawals are immediate. Minimum deposits differ too: £20 or €20 against £5 or €5.

**Method selection therefore sets funding friction and withdrawal expectations before marketing starts.** Comparing advertised features is not enough; operators need the published processing conditions for each method in each market.

A payment gateway connects the platform to multiple payment service providers and routes each transaction by geography, currency and risk. The rest of the payment journey includes:

- Authentication of the payer, including card authentication where required
- Monitoring for fraud and unusual patterns
- Reconciliation of every transaction against the player's wallet

**Regional preferences decide the method mix.** Cards dominate some markets, instant bank transfers others, and e-wallets or cryptocurrencies elsewhere. ### Sportsbook Software

Adding sports betting changes the operating rhythm of a casino. A slot's maths is fixed; a football match is not. A player who backs a pre-match selection may see the price move several times once play starts, and each change alters the operator's exposure. **Trading and exposure management become continuous tasks rather than periodic reviews.**

**A sportsbook solution supplies the pieces that make that manageable:**

- Odds feeds across pre-match and in-play markets
- Bet types from singles to accumulators and bet builders
- Live trading tools and virtual sports
- Risk management with limits, liability monitoring and bet acceptance rules

Operators usually add sportsbook software when their market expects it or when cross-selling between casino and sports justifies the cost. Deployment comes in two forms. API integration gives full front-end control but more implementation work; an embedded iFrame launches faster with the supplier's interface. Either way the sportsbook must share accounts, wallets and reporting with the rest of the platform.

When comparing suppliers, check regional sports coverage, real-time odds quality, wallet integration, multi-currency support, scaling under peak event traffic and which deployment options are offered.

### CRM, Business Intelligence and Compliance Tools

A campaign decision needs two things: a measurable result and a player base eligible to receive it. Customer relationship management supplies the second. A CRM segments players by behaviour, preferences and deposit history, so an operator can target a specific group, run the campaign and watch what happens.

Business intelligence supplies the first. **BI combines game, payment and marketing data to show whether a campaign paid off**, measured through gross gaming revenue, net revenue after bonuses and costs, retention and bonus return on investment. Those reports shape which games to promote and which segments to leave alone. BGaming's piece on [game analytics](https://bgaming.com/articles/data-driven-era-of-igaming-4-ways-to-use-game-analytics-for-enhanced-player-experience) shows how player-behaviour data feeds personalised marketing and fraud detection.

Compliance tools make sure eligibility holds. They connect onboarding checks, payment monitoring and campaign eligibility to the rules of each target market and to player-protection controls:

- KYC and AML verification at onboarding and at deposit thresholds
- Sanctions and politically exposed person screening
- Responsible gaming controls that exclude restricted players from promotions
- Audit-ready records for regulators

**Together these systems support long-term growth by making retention measurable, promotions accountable and regulatory compliance demonstrable.**

## How to Choose the Right iGaming Solutions Provider

Supplier promises become useful only when matched to testable operating requirements. Write one requirements brief, then compare B2B iGaming providers on two things: demonstrated operational fit and enforceable commercial responsibilities.

### Test Market Fit and Technical Ownership

Shortlist providers against the brief: target markets and licences, expected volumes, customisation needs and launch scope. **Ask each for references from operators of similar size in similar jurisdictions, and call them.**

**Then test instead of listening.** Run a representative workflow in a sandbox, such as a registration, a deposit, a bonus and a withdrawal, and inspect what sits behind it:

- API documentation and versioning practice
- Security certifications and incident history
- Disaster recovery and peak-load evidence
- Who owns support when a fault involves a third-party integration

### Compare Charges, Support and Exit Terms

Minimum monthly charges can begin before the casino goes live. A 2023 platform agreement between HR Entertainment and [Pragmatic Solutions](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1947210/000175392623001609/g083517_ex10-13.htm), filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, shows the shape such contracts take:

- €40,000 setup fee and €20,000 migration fee
- Monthly royalties of 2% of the first €1 million GGR and 1.5% above, where GGR is wagers less winnings
- A €15,000 monthly minimum starting at go-live or six months after commencement, whichever comes first
- Priority-one incident response within 15 minutes with updates every 30 minutes, backed by service credits of 5% to 15% of the monthly revenue share, subject to exclusions and an annual cap
- On termination, a customer-data transfer plan and up to six months of transition services on request

**Compare total cost, fee escalation, service-credit exclusions and transition duties, not the headline royalty rate.** Long-term partnership potential shows in the exit terms as much as in the sales deck.

## FAQ

### What are iGaming solutions?

Software, infrastructure and operational services that support launching and managing online gambling businesses, connecting platforms, games, payments and compliance into one operating environment.

### What is the difference between B2B and B2C iGaming software?

B2B suppliers serve gambling businesses with platforms and content. B2C operators serve players through consumer-facing sites, usually running on technology supplied by those B2B vendors.

### What services are included in B2B iGaming solutions?

Platforms, player account management, games, payments, sportsbooks, CRM, analytics, compliance and hosting, purchased as individual modules or as an assembled end-to-end platform.

### What is the difference between white-label and bespoke iGaming solutions?

White label configures an existing shared platform under the provider's upkeep. Bespoke development gives greater product control but makes the operator responsible for building and maintaining it.

### Are turnkey iGaming solutions suitable for new operators?

Yes, when the bundled technology and integrations fit the launch requirements. Confirm the licensing arrangement, operating duties, supplier support commitments and ongoing charges before signing.

### Can operators combine multiple iGaming solutions?

Yes. APIs connect selected external services, but the operator must coordinate shared account and transaction data, integration testing, monitoring and support responsibilities across suppliers.

### What role does game aggregation play in iGaming?

Aggregation connects many studios through one operator-facing integration, simplifying catalogue access, content updates and market configuration across the participating game providers.

### How do I choose the right iGaming solutions provider?

Match a documented requirements brief to market coverage, references and tested workflows, then compare total charges, technical ownership, support commitments and data-transfer terms on exit.
