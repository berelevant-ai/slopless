# Superficial analysis

Flag an event followed by an appended claim of significance, commitment, progress, or cultural value whose evidence is missing. Common links include highlighting, underscoring, fostering, and which reflects. The defect requires both the appended construction and its unsupported claim.

## Examples

- Hit: The bridge opened in 1998, **highlighting the city's commitment to sustainable growth**. An opening date supplies no evidence of commitment.
- Hit: The festival ran for three days **and reflects the region's enduring cultural significance**. Duration supplies no evidence of cultural importance.
- No hit: The benchmark fell from 240 ms to 180 ms, demonstrating the measured latency reduction. The measurements support the conclusion.
- No hit: The technician rotated the lens, aligning the sensor with marker B. The clause describes a physical operation.

Flag equivalent constructions. Preserve appended clauses that state an identified mechanism or supported result. A standalone importance claim belongs to semantic thinness; this rule requires the appended clause.
