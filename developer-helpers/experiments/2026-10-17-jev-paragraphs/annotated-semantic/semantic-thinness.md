# Semantic-thinness rule

## Reading the marked examples

- In desired hits, bold marks the defective phrase or the related phrases that form the defective construction. Unmarked words supply context.
- The reason explains the marked wording. A short example may have its complete construction marked when the defect spans that construction.
- Desired no-hits identify preserved wording separately. The reason explains its useful literal meaning, supplied detail, or explicit rule exception.
- Apply the same distinction to equivalent wording in the target paragraph.

Judge the constructions below using the desired-hit and desired-no-hit examples. Equivalent wording can have the same defect.

## How to apply the examples

- Identify the candidate claim and what it states. Use the matching pattern's examples to distinguish the empty construction from its concrete counterpart.
- A specific explanation can supply the missing observation, event, result, or physical cause. A concrete sentence elsewhere does not supply it automatically.
- Solution-like nouns praised as boring remain targets when instructions follow a colon. Literal evaluations of films, audits, reports, or an explicitly dull experience are desired no-hits.
- A factual subject can still have an abstract metaphor or unsupported significance claim. Evaluate what is asserted about the subject.
- Pattern examples establish the intended distinctions; exception notes explain the specific cases they preserve.

### Construction

- `{abstractAgent} seemed to {cognitionVerb} that {emptyShift}.`
- `{abstractAgent} was {speechVerb} that {emptyInstitutionalClaim}.`
- `{abstractAgent} was asking for {softAnswer}.`
- `{abstractAgent} knew it needed to {identityChange}.`
- `{abstractAgent} wanted to become {selfEvaluation}.`
- `{abstractAgent} had been waiting for {abstractPayoff} to {becomingVerb}.`
- `{abstractContainer} held {humanGroup} until {abstractPayoff} arrived.`
- `{abstractMeaning} lives in what it {availabilityVerb}.`
- `{abstractSilence} in {technicalSurface} said {emptyComparison}.`
- `{roadmapSubject} stopped being a plan and started becoming {abstractStructure}.`
- `{pathSubject} is obvious once {abstractObstacle} is no longer allowed to lead.`
- `{opportunitySubject} is to let {abstractWork} teach us what {abstractWork} is.`
- `{answerSubject} was always closer than {abstractSearch} made it seem.`
- `{abstractAgent} asks for {identityRequest}.`
- `something opens when {softCondition}.`

### Preserved concrete uses

- sentence names a concrete actor, document, sensor, transcript, trademark, or measurement.
- sentence states an actual causal mechanism.

#### Desired hits

- The **room seemed to understand** that **something important was beginning to shift**.
  - Why this is a hit: The room receives human understanding while the supposedly important change remains unnamed.
- The **strategy wanted to become more honest with itself**.
  - Why this is a hit: The strategy receives a desire for self-improvement without identifying a person's decision or a policy change.
- The **conversation held the team** until the **next layer of clarity arrived**.
  - Why this is a hit: The conversation holds people and clarity arrives without naming what the team discussed or learned.

#### Desired no-hits

- The room sensor reported that occupancy increased after the doors opened.
  - Preserved wording: `room sensor reported that occupancy increased after the doors opened`. A sensor reports an occupancy change after a stated physical event rather than a room understanding anything.
- The strategy document lists Mara as owner and May 31 as the review date.
  - Preserved wording: `document lists Mara as owner and May 31 as the review date`. The document records an identified owner and review date rather than expressing a strategy's intentions.

## abstract-contrast

Catch abstract not-this-but-that contrasts that reframe with abstract nouns instead of adding concrete information.

### Construction

- `{abstractSubject} is not {abstractObject}. It is {abstractObject}.`
- `{abstractSubject} is not about {abstractObject}. It is about {abstractObject}.`
- `{abstractSubject} {metaphorVerb} in {abstractObject}, not in {abstractObject}.`
- `This is not {abstractObject}. This is {abstractObject}.`
- `The point is not {abstractObject}. The point is {abstractObject}.`

### Preserved concrete uses

- either side of contrast contains concrete technical, factual, or measured content.
- contrast names specific actors, dates, places, statutes, code symbols, or physical objects.
- sentence pair is a legitimate correction of a concrete misconception.

#### Desired hits

- **The point is not control. The point is repair.**
  - Why this is a hit: The contrast substitutes repair for control without stating what anyone should do differently.
- **Prevention lives in rehearsal, not in the lecture**.
  - Why this is a hit: The contrast locates prevention in rehearsal rather than a lecture without identifying a preventive practice or outcome.
- **This is not clarity. This is performance.**
  - Why this is a hit: The contrast replaces clarity with performance without identifying the behavior being criticized.
- **Growth is not intensity. It is consistency.**
  - Why this is a hit: The contrast substitutes consistency for intensity without specifying the activity or evidence of growth.
- **The work is not the outcome. The work is the process.**
  - Why this is a hit: The contrast relabels work as process without identifying a task or an operational difference.

#### Desired no-hits

- The bug is not in the parser. It is in the offset mapper.
  - Preserved wording: `not in the parser. It is in the offset mapper`. The correction identifies the offset mapper rather than the parser as the component containing the bug.
- The policy is not a tax credit. It is a direct rebate.
  - Preserved wording: `not a tax credit. It is a direct rebate`. The correction distinguishes two identified financial mechanisms, a tax credit and a direct rebate.
- The fix is not to retry the request. It is to close the socket.
  - Preserved wording: `not to retry the request. It is to close the socket`. The correction replaces a request retry with the concrete operation of closing the socket.
- The sensor is not in the hallway. It is in the stairwell.
  - Preserved wording: `not in the hallway. It is in the stairwell`. The correction supplies the sensor's physical location in the stairwell.

### Decisive distinction

- A page, feed, dashboard, or brand can be a concrete artifact while its claimed role is still an empty metaphor.
- The page wearing a jacket and the dashboard being the decision are targets.
- A physical pipe, identified product, catalog file, or stated technical operation supplies a literal meaning.

### Preserved concrete uses

- Preserve literal spatial actions, physical objects, identified products, and specified technical operations.
- An explanation must identify the claimed operation or relationship. Merely naming a page, dashboard, client, or other artifact does not explain a metaphor.
- Preserve a literary metaphor when quoted for discussion or attribution rather than asserted by the narrator.

#### Desired hits

- **Prevention lives in rehearsal**.
  - Why this is a hit: Prevention is given a metaphorical home without naming the rehearsal activity or the harm it prevents.
- **Problem-solving lands after the storm**.
  - Why this is a hit: Problem-solving is made to land after a metaphorical storm without identifying the problem or the intervening event.
- **Trust sits inside repair**.
  - Why this is a hit: The spatial relation between trust and repair replaces an account of how trust is earned or restored.
- **The answer is a steadier rhythm**.
  - Why this is a hit: A rhythm substitutes for an answer without identifying the action or schedule being recommended.
- **Daily life is the curriculum**.
  - Why this is a hit: The identity between life and curriculum leaves the subject, learning activity, and intended lesson unstated.
- **Safety starts with consistency**.
  - Why this is a hit: Consistency is offered as the basis of safety without naming the practice that must be repeated or the hazard addressed.

#### Desired no-hits

- The backup generator sits inside the repair shop.
  - Preserved wording: `backup generator sits inside the repair shop`. A physical generator occupies a literal location inside a repair shop.
- The answer is to move the server restart before the migration.
  - Preserved wording: `move the server restart before the migration`. The answer specifies the required ordering of two technical operations.
- Prevention starts with installing smoke alarms in every bedroom.
  - Preserved wording: `installing smoke alarms in every bedroom`. The prevention claim names the equipment to install and the rooms where it belongs.
- Trust improved after the team published the missing incident timeline.
  - Preserved wording: `team published the missing incident timeline`. The trust claim is tied to the team's identified act of publishing previously missing incident information.

#### Desired hits from fixtures

- The **page cannot outsource its answer to metadata**.
  - Why this is a hit: Outsourcing personifies the page without specifying which answer content is missing or how metadata would supply it.
- The **page is wearing a nice jacket over an empty shirt**.
  - Why this is a hit: The clothing metaphor criticizes presentation and substance without identifying the page's missing information.
- The **brand vanishes like a contractor after a vague scope call**.
  - Why this is a hit: The contractor simile leaves the brand's disappearance undefined instead of identifying a change in visibility or recognition.
- **Local AI visibility behaves like a trust filter**.
  - Why this is a hit: The filter metaphor supplies neither the trust criteria nor the operation that selects or excludes a business.
- **The dashboard is the decision**.
  - Why this is a hit: The identity equation replaces an explanation of which decision the dashboard informs or performs.
- To the machine doing the shopping, the **feed is the product**.
  - Why this is a hit: The identity equation leaves unexplained which product information the machine receives or uses.

#### Desired no-hits from fixtures

- The pipeline is the steel pipe that carries water to the tank.
  - Preserved wording: `steel pipe that carries water to the tank`. The pipeline is defined as a physical pipe with an identified material, function, and destination.
- The feed is the product catalog uploaded to S3.
  - Preserved wording: `product catalog uploaded to S3`. The feed is identified as a catalog with a stated storage destination.
