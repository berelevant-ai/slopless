# Semantic-thinness rule

Judge the constructions below using the desired-hit and desired-no-hit examples. Equivalent wording can have the same defect.

## How to apply the examples

- Identify the candidate claim and what it states. Use the matching pattern's examples to distinguish the empty construction from its concrete counterpart.
- A specific explanation can supply the missing observation, event, result, or physical cause. A concrete sentence elsewhere does not supply it automatically.
- Solution-like nouns praised as boring remain targets when instructions follow a colon. Literal evaluations of films, audits, reports, or an explicitly dull experience are desired no-hits.
- A factual subject can still have an abstract metaphor or unsupported significance claim. Evaluate what is asserted about the subject.
- Pattern examples establish the intended distinctions; exception notes explain the specific cases they preserve.

Catch abstract systems, rooms, strategies, and conversations treated as intentional actors instead of naming concrete behavior.

### Construction

- `{abstractAgent} seemed to {cognitionVerb} that {emptyShift}.`
- `{abstractAgent} was {speechVerb} that {emptyInstitutionalClaim}.`
- `{abstractAgent} was asking for {softAnswer}.`
- `{abstractAgent} knew it needed to {identityChange}.`
- `{abstractAgent} wanted to become {selfEvaluation}.`
- `{abstractAgent} had been waiting for {abstractPayoff} to {becomingVerb}.`
- `{abstractContainer} held {humanGroup} until {abstractPayoff} arrived.`
- `{abstractMeaning} lives in what it {availabilityVerb}.`
- `{abstractSilence} in {technicalSurface} said {emptyComparison}.`
- `{roadmapSubject} stopped being a plan and started becoming {abstractStructure}.`
- `{pathSubject} is obvious once {abstractObstacle} is no longer allowed to lead.`
- `{opportunitySubject} is to let {abstractWork} teach us what {abstractWork} is.`
- `{answerSubject} was always closer than {abstractSearch} made it seem.`
- `{abstractAgent} asks for {identityRequest}.`
- `something opens when {softCondition}.`

### Preserved concrete uses

- sentence names a concrete actor, document, sensor, transcript, trademark, or measurement.
- sentence states an actual causal mechanism.

#### Desired hits

- The strategy wanted to become more honest with itself.
- The conversation held the team until the next layer of clarity arrived.

#### Desired no-hits

- The room sensor reported that occupancy increased after the doors opened.
- The strategy document lists Mara as owner and May 31 as the review date.

## abstract-contrast

Catch abstract not-this-but-that contrasts that reframe with abstract nouns instead of adding concrete information.

### Construction

- `{abstractSubject} is not {abstractObject}. It is {abstractObject}.`
- `{abstractSubject} is not about {abstractObject}. It is about {abstractObject}.`
- `{abstractSubject} {metaphorVerb} in {abstractObject}, not in {abstractObject}.`
- `This is not {abstractObject}. This is {abstractObject}.`
- `The point is not {abstractObject}. The point is {abstractObject}.`

### Preserved concrete uses

- either side of contrast contains concrete technical, factual, or measured content.
- contrast names specific actors, dates, places, statutes, code symbols, or physical objects.
- sentence pair is a legitimate correction of a concrete misconception.

#### Desired hits

- The point is not control. The point is repair.
- Prevention lives in rehearsal, not in the lecture.
- This is not clarity. This is performance.
- Growth is not intensity. It is consistency.
- The work is not the outcome. The work is the process.

#### Desired no-hits

- The bug is not in the parser. It is in the offset mapper.
- The policy is not a tax credit. It is a direct rebate.
- The fix is not to retry the request. It is to close the socket.
- The sensor is not in the hallway. It is in the stairwell.
