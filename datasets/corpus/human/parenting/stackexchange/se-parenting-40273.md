# Limit access to screens (phone, computer) to my kids, whereas I benefitted from "no limit" during my own childhood

My own upbringing and later career has followed much the same trajectory as you describe, so this is something I too have pondered quite a bit. But as you've already noted, we are indeed no longer in the 90's. I'd argue the technological progress in the intervening decades have rendered that frame of reference obsolete.
Unlimited  access to devices was by the nature of the technology of the time still more limited than it is today. I spent a  lot  of time in front of my computer screen. But I did not use it in the bathroom, at the dinner table, or when I was outside. Parents of the 90's didn't have to impose hard restrictions, because it was easy enough to create an environment where technology wasn't constantly available. When we had unlimited access to our devices growing up, that  still  left time for reading, building legos and playing with friends.
The  applications  of technology was also limited. Internet access was slow and so were graphics. The devices we had didn't give us a portable equivalent of a casino with all visual cues and rewards carefully tuned to be as addictive as possible, and appealing to children of a very young age. Content was also naturally limited since games were sold individually and at a high price, so any given child only had access to so many games. You weren't constantly downloading the next addictive app, so your use was also naturally limited because even Pizza Tycoon could only keep your interest for so long.
Internet access , as you've noted, in most families I knew of  was  limited, because it came with a per-hour fee, and it blocked the phone lines for everybody else. The same goes for  passive use , which you also comment on: while many parents may have had a permissive attitude towards computers, they might still have concerns about too much TV time. It's probably more informative to compare unlimited screen time today with unlimited TV time in the 90's than with unlimited computer time in the 90's, if you consider typical use.
Technology used by very young children I think had no real equivalence in the early days of personal computers, so there I think  WHO's  "no time spent passively watching screens" is unproblematic in the context of this question. Your child is older, and I would move towards qualifying the use of technology over setting a broad limit, and pay more attention to what your child uses technology for than blindly counting hours. Your individual values may obviously vary from mine, but I think things that I would factor in are:
Prefer multi player over single player games
Prefer constructive use of technology such as dabbling with game development over game play
Prefer active participation over passive consumption
Prefer use of technology to pursue a non-technological interest over using techonlogy as an end in itself; eg. using the internet to find information about a topic of interest
Prefer joint use over solitary use; eg. watch a film together with the child rather than using the TV as a babysitter
Monitor your child's use and how it seems to affect their mood, sleep, and interaction with the outside world, and qualify the screen time based on criteria such as the above to try and make an informed decision about whether the child is exhibiting a healthy use of technology or not.
