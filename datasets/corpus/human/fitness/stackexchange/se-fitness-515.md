# Should I stretch after exercise?

After doing some research, Ivo's response is correct in that stretching before and exercise  may not  prevent injuries:
Karl Fields et. all in Current Sports Medicine Reports had this to say:
Typical of the findings was a randomized trial of 421 runners with stretching, warm up, and cool down before and after running.  This trial showed a slightly lower injury rate in the control group vs the group that used stretching as an intervention before running  (67). Another intervention in a military population showed that 1538 army recruits placed on a stretching protocol vs no intervention  showed no reduction in injury risk
However Fields did find findings that may conclude stretching  after  an excercise,  may  prevent injuries:
Many athletes have adopted the strategy of stretching after activity rather than before. One study raises the possibility that this might offer injury protection...For the intervention program, the athletes stretched after practicing and while fatigued... Results favored the intervention in that in preintervention 9 and 11 athletes sustained hamstring injury compared with 2 and 4 after intervention.  In addition, competition days missed were reduced from 31 and 38 to 5 and 16 after intervention.
Also stretching does seem to be of use in relief of an injury (again Fields):
The most positive of these involved a stretching intervention in plantar fasciitis...For phase 2, all patients were given the PF stretch protocol.  Follow-up at 2 yr showed that 92% had a high degree of satisfaction.
Finally, streching does seem to be helpful in the removal of 'tightness' and enabling freer motion of the muscles.  Two studies I found appear to back this up:
ACUTE EFFECT OF STATIC AND DYNAMIC STRETCHING ON HIP DYNAMIC RANGE OF MOTION DURING INSTEP KICKING IN PROFESSIONAL SOCCER PLAYERS by Mohammadtaghi Amiri-Khorasani et. all conducted this study and found these results:
Study:
The purpose of this study was to examine the effects of static and dynamic stretching within a pre-exercise warm-up on hip dynamic range of motion (DROM) during instep kicking in professional soccer players. The kicking motions of dominant legs were
captured from 18 professional adult male soccer players.
Restults:
Within-group analysis showed no signiﬁcant difference in DROM after the dynamic stretching (2.59 6 4.67) compared with the static stretching (0.35 6 2.197) relative to
the no stretching group during the backswing phase.  There was, on the other hand, a signiﬁcant (p , 0.03) difference in DROM after the dynamic stretching (3.35 6 6.36)
compared with the static stretching (21.35 6 7.86) relative to the no-stretching group during the forward phase
The Effects of Two Modes of Static Stretching On Muscle Strength and Stiffness. by Herda TJ, et all found these findings:
Study:
The purpose of the present study was to examine the effects of constant-angle (CA) and constant-torque (CT) stretching of the leg flexors on peak torque (PT), electromyography (EMGRMS) at PT
Results:
PT decreased (mean ± SE 5.63 ± 1.65 Nm) (P = 0.004), and EMGRMS was unchanged (P > 0.05) from pre- to post-stretching for both treatments...In addition, MTS decreased at all three angles (angel 1 0.23 ± 0.29 Nm°; angel 2 0.26 ± 0.35 Nm°; angle 3 0.28 ± 0.44 Nm°) following the CT stretching treatment (P < 0.005), however, MTS was unchanged following CA stretching (P > 0.05).
Conclusion:
Therefore, if the primary goal of the stretching routine is to decrease MTS,  these results suggest CT stretching (constant pressure) may be more appropriate than a stretch held at a constant muscle length (CA stretching).
Finally back to Fields:
Data suggest that too short of a time (less than 10 s) is ineffective and that prolonged stretches longer than 60 s accomplish no more than stretching periods of 10-60 s...The results showed that static stretching resulted in no change in Achilles stiffness, whereas ballistic stretching decreased Achilles stiffness.
In Essence:
Basically injury prevention is something that studies seem to debate over, and have no conclusive findings as to whether or not stretching decreases injury prevention.  However, there does seems to be conclusive evidence from multiple studies to suggest that relief of muscle soreness, and gain in freedom of motion can be improved through use of stretching.
