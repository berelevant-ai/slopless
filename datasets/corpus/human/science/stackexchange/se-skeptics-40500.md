# Are 19.6 pounds of CO2 produced from burning a gallon of gasoline?

This is directly addressed by the US Department of Energy at  FuelEconomy.gov :
It seems impossible that a gallon of gasoline, which weighs about 6.3 pounds [2.9 kg], could produce 20 pounds [9.1 kg] of carbon dioxide (CO2) when burned. However, most of the weight of the CO2 doesn't come from the gasoline itself, but the oxygen in the air.
[...]
Therefore, to calculate the amount of CO2 produced from a gallon of gasoline, the weight of the carbon in the gasoline is multiplied by 44/12 or 3.7.
[...]
We can then multiply the weight of the carbon (5.5 pounds [2.5 kg]) by 3.7, which equals 20 pounds [9.1 kg] of CO2!
So the answer is:  Yes .
An article at  Slate  draws similar conclusions.
