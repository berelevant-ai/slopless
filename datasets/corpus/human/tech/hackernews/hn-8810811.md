# Ask HN: Why are so many companies against remote engineers?

<!-- author: Blackthorn; created: 2014-12-29T17:44:10Z; score: 11 -->

A bit over a year ago, I moved to the Bay Area to take a job at one of the big-name companies here. Over the past year I've discovered that life in the Bay Area just isn't for me and my company is extremely unsupportive of remote work. No problem, I figured: I'd just find a job with a company that allowed for remote engineers. This has unexpectedly become a world of trouble. I see all the time blog posts, comments, etc., from hiring representatives about how it's so difficult to find good talent. There's plenty of great talent out there though: it's scattered throughout the rest of the country, and unwilling to move to the Bay Area! One would think that companies hiring for positions that can be performed remotely, like software engineering, would start hiring remote to obtain the talent they desire, but I've encountered immense resistance to this. I've even had one company bring me in for an interview with the promise that the job would be remote, only to tell me at the end of the day that they were extending me an offer but only for onsite in San Francisco! So, what gives? Why the continued resistance to remote engineers?
