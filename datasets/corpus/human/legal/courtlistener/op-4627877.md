Each of the petitioners alleges the following errors: (a) That respondent has assessed against each petitioner a liability in respect of taxes alleged to be due by the Old Farmers Oil Co., hereafter referred to as Old Farmers Co. for the calendar year 1919, after the period provided by law and the statute of limitations for the making of such assessment against Old Farmers Co. has expired, and when said assessment was prohibited by the statute of limitations applicable to said calendar year; (b) that respondent has assessed against each petitioner a liability in respect of taxes alleged to be due by the Old Farmers Co. for the calendar year 1919, after the period provided by the statute of limitations for the making of such assessment, instituting proceedings in court, or instituting other proceedings in respect of the tax liability, *2621 if any, has expired, and when such assessment, proceeding in court, or other proceeding in respect of said alleged tax liability, were barred by the statute of limitations applicable to said calendar year for the making of said assessment, instituting proceedings in court or instituting other proceedings in respect to such alleged *255 tax liability; (c) respondent has assessed against each petitioner a liability in respect of the alleged taxes of the Old Farmers Co. for the calendar year 1919, after the period for making such assessment under the provision of section 280 of the Revenue Act of 1926 and other provisions of said Act had expired, and has made such assessment contrary to the Revenue Act of 1926, when said assessment was barred by said Act; (d) respondent assessed against each petitioner a liability in respect of alleged taxes of the Old Farmers Co. for the calendar year 1919, as a transferee of said company, before it had been determined by a suit at law or equity or by other appropriate legal proceedings that petitioner was a transferee or assignee of the Old Farmers Co. and before the deficiency had been redetermined or any assessment made against the Old Farmers *2622 Co. and while the appeal of said Old Farmers Co. for a redetermination was pending before the Board; and (e) that the alleged liability as assessed by respondent is not authorized by law or by the Revenue Act of 1926, and, in the alternative, if any law purports to authorize said assessment as made by respondent against petitioner, said purported law is unconstitutional. The facts were stipulated and from such stipulations and from the pleadings, we make the following findings of fact. FINDINGS OF FACT. Petitioner, Woodley Oil Co., is a corporation organized under the laws of the State of Delaware, with its principal place of business at City Bank Building, Shreveport, La. The remaining petitioners are individuals. J. F. Van Cleve, James M. Foster, N. M. Kronenberg, T. J. Woodley, H. G. Freedman and Aaron Freedman reside at Shreveport, La.; C. F. Elmer resides at Walcott, Iowa; E. E. Dabney resides at De Leon, Tex.; J. L. Page and G. B. Trew reside at Vivian, La.; and E. L. Woodley, formerly residing at Shreveport, La., now resides at 3916 Gillon Avenue, Highland Park, Dallas, Tex. The liabilities herein involved arise by reason of a deficiency in tax determined by respondent *2623 against the Old Farmers Co. for the calendar year 1919. The Old Farmers Co. filed its corporation income and profits-tax return on March 10, 1920, and respondent mailed to it a 60-day letter with respect to the year 1919 on February 6, 1925. There was a proceeding for the enforcement of the alleged liability for income and profits taxes alleged to be due the United States by said Old Farmers Co. for the calendar year 1919, pending at the time of the enactment of the Revenue Act of 1926. At no time prior to the filing of the petitions herein had respondent assessed any part of the deficiency against the Old Farmers Co. The original *256 petition of the Woodley Company was filed with the Board on July 6, 1926. All the other petitions were filed on July 30, 1926. The assessment lists assessing the taxes and liabilities herein against the petitioners were signed on April 10, 1926, by the Commissioner of Internal Revenue. The 60-day letters here involved were mailed on June 4, 1926, to all the petitioners except E. E. Dabney, and the 60-day letter to E. E. Dabney was mailed on July 12, 1926. None of the petitioners had received any notice except notices and demands from the *2624 Collector of Internal Revenue and 60-day letters from the Commissioner of Internal Revenue. None of them had an opportunity to be heard before the assessment was made against each of them. No proceeding in court has been instituted by respondent against the petitioners or any one of them to determine their liability for the taxes of the Old Farmers Co. Petitioner, Woodley Company, was not a stockholder in the Old Farmers Co. at any time. The remaining petitioners owned stock in the Old Farmers Co. on April 5, 1922, as follows: C. F. Elmer, 4 shares; T. J. Woodley, 40 shares; N. M. Kronenberg, 13 1/2 shares; E. L. Woodley, 113 shares; H. G. Freedman, 13 1/2 shares; Aaron Freedman, 13 1/2 shares; E. E. Dabney, 1 share; J. F. Van Cleve, 5 shares; J. L. Page, 6 shares; James M. Foster, 3 shares; and G. B. Trew, 1 share. On April 5, 1922, petitioner, Woodley Company, purchased the assets of the Old Farmers Co. for 33,150 shares of no par value of its common capital stock, and this stock was distributed and issued direct to the petitioning stockholders and each received 150 shares of stock in the Woodley Company for each share of stock in the Old Farmers Co.
