# 7 Things You Should Know About Selling Your Home

Selling a home is a major life event, and the decisions you make along the way can have a significant impact on your final sale price and overall experience. Whether this is your first time selling or you have been through it before, there is always something new to learn. Here are 7 crucial things to know before putting your home on the market.

**1. Pricing your home correctly from the start is critical.**
One of the biggest mistakes sellers make is overpricing their home. When a home sits on the market too long, buyers start to wonder what is wrong with it. A well-priced home generates more interest, more showings, and often results in competing offers. Work with your agent to review comparable sales in your area and set a price that reflects the market.

**2. First impressions are everything.**
The way your home looks when buyers first see it - both online and in person - can make or break a sale. High-quality listing photos are essential in today's market, where the majority of buyers start their search online. Curb appeal matters too. Simple upgrades like fresh paint, tidy landscaping, and a clean entryway can dramatically increase buyer interest.

**3. Staging can lead to higher offers.**
Staging your home helps buyers visualize themselves living in the space. Studies have consistently shown that staged homes sell faster and for more money than unstaged homes. Even simple staging steps, such as decluttering, rearranging furniture, and adding fresh flowers, can make a meaningful difference.

**4. Disclosures protect you legally.**
Most states require sellers to disclose known defects or issues with the property. Failing to disclose a material defect can expose you to legal liability even after the sale is complete. It is always better to be upfront about any issues and price the home accordingly, rather than hoping buyers do not notice.

**5. Understand your net proceeds before you accept an offer.**
The offer price is not the amount you walk away with. Closing costs, agent commissions, transfer taxes, and any outstanding mortgage balance all come out of the proceeds. Ask your agent to prepare a seller's net sheet so you have a clear picture of your expected take-home amount.

**6. Timing the market is harder than it sounds.**
Many sellers try to time the market to get the best possible price. While spring and early summer are traditionally strong selling seasons, the best time to sell is ultimately when you are ready and the property is prepared. A motivated seller with a well-presented home can succeed in almost any market.

**7. Negotiations do not end with the offer.**
Once you accept an offer, negotiations can continue through the inspection period, appraisal, and even final walkthrough. Buyers may request repairs or credits based on inspection findings. Knowing your bottom line and being prepared to negotiate calmly and strategically will help you close the deal on favorable terms.

Selling your home does not have to be stressful. With the right preparation, the right team, and realistic expectations, you can achieve a successful sale that sets you up for your next chapter.
