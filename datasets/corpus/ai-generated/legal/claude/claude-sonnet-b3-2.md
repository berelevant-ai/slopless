# 7 Things You Should Know About Intellectual Property Rights

Intellectual property - or IP - is one of the most valuable assets a business or individual creator can have. Yet it is also one of the most misunderstood areas of law. Whether you are an entrepreneur, artist, or inventor, here are seven key things you absolutely need to understand about protecting your creative work.

## 1. There Are Four Main Types of IP Protection

Intellectual property law is divided into four primary categories: patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets. Each one protects a different kind of asset. Patents protect inventions, trademarks protect brand identifiers, copyrights protect creative works, and trade secrets protect confidential business information. Knowing which type applies to your situation is the essential first step.

## 2. Copyright Exists Automatically

Many creators do not realize that copyright protection attaches to your work the moment you create it and fix it in a tangible form. You do not need to register your copyright to own it. However, registering your work with the Copyright Office gives you significant additional benefits, including the ability to sue for statutory damages in infringement cases.

## 3. Trademarks Must Be Used in Commerce

Unlike copyright, trademark protection is tied to actual use of the mark in commerce. You cannot simply reserve a brand name without using it. Trademarks can also be lost if you stop using them or fail to police them adequately against infringers. This is why large companies send cease-and-desist letters so frequently.

## 4. Patents Require Disclosure

In exchange for patent protection, inventors must publicly disclose how their invention works. This is a fundamental trade-off built into the patent system. After your patent expires - typically 20 years from the filing date - anyone can use your disclosed invention freely. Many companies choose trade secret protection instead for this reason.

## 5. IP Can Be Sold and Licensed

Intellectual property is a genuine asset that can be transferred, sold, or licensed to others. Licensing is particularly important because it allows you to generate revenue from your IP while retaining ownership. Understanding licensing agreements is critical for anyone working in creative industries or technology.

## 6. Employee Creations Are Often Owned by Employers

If you create something during the course of your employment, your employer may own the resulting IP. This is especially true for software developers, designers, and researchers. It is incredibly important to review any employment agreement carefully before starting a new job to understand what IP rights you are signing away.

## 7. International Protection Requires Separate Filings

IP rights are territorial. A US patent does not protect your invention in Europe or Asia. If you want international protection, you need to file in each jurisdiction or use international treaty frameworks like the Patent Cooperation Treaty. This can be expensive, so it is important to prioritize where you need protection.

Navigating intellectual property law can feel overwhelming, but taking the time to understand these fundamentals will put you in a much stronger position to protect what you create.
