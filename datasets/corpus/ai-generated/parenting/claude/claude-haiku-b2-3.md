# Rethinking Summer: How Boredom is Actually Your Parenting Secret Weapon

The end of the school year is approaching, and I can feel the pressure mounting. Parents everywhere are scrambling to sign their kids up for camps, activities, classes, and enrichment programs to fill the long summer days.

And I get it. I really do. The idea of eight weeks of "what are we doing today?" is genuinely daunting when you're balancing work, household management, and the constant need to keep everyone engaged and stimulated.

But I want to offer a different perspective - one that might feel counterintuitive at first.

What if boredom is actually one of the greatest gifts we can give our kids?

I know, I know. That sounds wild coming from someone who grew up when the idea was to keep kids constantly entertained. But hear me out.

Boredom is where creativity lives. Boredom is when kids figure out how to entertain themselves - when they build elaborate worlds, invent games, start projects, and discover their own interests. Boredom is when their brains actually get time to rest and consolidate learning, which neuroscience research shows is essential for growth and development.

And yet, we've created a cultural narrative where boredom is something to be prevented at all costs. We fill every moment with structured activities, screen time, and adult-directed learning. We're essentially saying to our kids: "Your own thoughts and imagination aren't enough. You need us to provide your stimulation."

I decided to try something different this summer. Instead of cramming the calendar with activities, we're deliberately keeping it open. Some structure, sure - maybe one or two classes or camps that spark genuine interest. But mostly, we're creating space. Space for play. Space for doing absolutely nothing. Space for our kids to figure out what *they* actually want to do when nobody is telling them what to do.

Early reports suggest it's working. My kids are already talking about projects they want to start, books they want to read, and games they want to invent. And more importantly, there's a quality of presence in our household that we don't usually experience during the school year.

Does this mean summer will be perfectly peaceful and easy? Absolutely not. There will be bored kids. There will be conflict. There will be moments where I want to throw a screen at them just to get some quiet.

But I'm learning to sit with that discomfort, because on the other side of it is growth.
