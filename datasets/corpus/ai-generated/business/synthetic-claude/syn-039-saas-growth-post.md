# How We 10x'd Net Revenue Retention by Fixing the Handoff Nobody Owned

Two years ago our NRR was 94%. Today it is 127%. The fix was embarrassingly simple in retrospect and extremely hard to execute in practice. Here is the full story.

The problem was not our product. Our power users were fanatics - the kind of customers who respond to NPS surveys unsolicited to tell you how much they love what you have built. The problem was that we had too few power users relative to our total customer base. The customers in between onboarding and power-user status were churning, contracting, or sitting at minimal usage levels that made them obvious churn candidates at renewal.

Notably, when we mapped the journey from new customer to power user, the biggest drop-off was not where we expected. It was not in the first two weeks of onboarding, which we had invested heavily in. It was in weeks 6 through 12 - after the dedicated onboarding period ended but before the customer had developed the internal champions and use cases that would make them self-sustaining. We called this the "orphan period," and it was where retention went to die.

The fix required creating a new function that did not fit neatly into sales or customer success - a customer development team whose job was specifically to drive expansion of use cases and internal adoption during weeks 6 through 16. Importantly, this team was not measured on renewal rates. They were measured on the number of new internal champions and new use cases activated per account. The logic was that if you drive those metrics, retention and expansion follow naturally. That logic proved correct.

The secret sauce was pairing this function with product-level instrumentation that could tell us in real time which accounts were in the orphan period and which were trending toward power-user status. The force multiplier was making the invisible visible - surfacing the risk before it became irreversible.

It is worth noting that this took 14 months from problem identification to consistent NRR improvement. There was no shortcut. But the 33-point improvement in NRR has had a larger impact on our financial trajectory than any top-of-funnel initiative we have ever run.
