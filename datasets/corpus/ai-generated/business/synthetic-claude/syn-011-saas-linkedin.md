# Why Your SaaS Pricing Model Is Your Biggest Strategic Mistake

Unpopular opinion: most SaaS companies are leaving 40% of their revenue on the table, and their pricing page is the smoking gun.

I have worked with over 60 SaaS companies on growth and monetization over the last eight years. The pattern I see over and over again is a company with genuinely differentiated technology wrapped in a pricing model that was designed in 2015 and has not been touched since. Seat-based, flat-tier, take-it-or-leave-it pricing is not just suboptimal. In a world of usage-based, outcome-oriented, and AI-amplified product experiences, it actively undermines the relationship between value delivered and value captured.

Notably, the best SaaS companies understand something fundamental: pricing is not a finance exercise. It is a product experience. Every time a customer hits a pricing gate, you are making a product decision. Every time they upgrade or downgrade, you are learning something critical about the shape of your value proposition. Importantly, the companies that treat pricing as a first-class product surface - iterating on it with the same rigor they bring to feature development - consistently outperform the ones that set it and forget it.

Here is the thing that most founders find counterintuitive: moving to value-based or usage-based pricing does not just change revenue. It changes the customer relationship. It aligns incentives. It empowers customers to grow into your product at their own pace rather than forcing them into buckets that do not fit.

The secret sauce is understanding your expansion motion before you design your pricing structure. Growth is built in from day one, or you spend three years retrofitting it.
