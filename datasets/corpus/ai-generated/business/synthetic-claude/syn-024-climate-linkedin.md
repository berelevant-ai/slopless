# The Carbon Market Needs Engineers, Not Idealists

Controversial take from someone who has spent a decade in climate tech: the voluntary carbon market's problems are not political. They are engineering problems. And they will be solved by engineers, not by policy.

I am optimistic about carbon markets in a way that a lot of my colleagues in the space are not. Here is why: every major problem with the market - additionality verification, permanence guarantees, double-counting prevention, transparent registries - has a technical solution. We just have not deployed those solutions at scale yet.

Notably, the satellite monitoring technology that can replace most on-the-ground carbon auditing is already operational. The blockchain-based registry infrastructure that prevents double-counting is already proven. The remote sensing plus machine learning combination that can assess permanence risk in real time already exists in research contexts. Importantly, what is missing is not the technology - it is the deployment infrastructure and the market standards that would make these tools the default rather than the exception.

This is where I see the most important work happening in the next five years. Not in new carbon project categories or new offset types, but in the boring, critical infrastructure that makes the whole market trustworthy at scale. That infrastructure is the force multiplier that turns a $2 billion market with credibility problems into a $100 billion market that can actually move the needle on global emissions.

The companies that are building this infrastructure are not the ones getting the press coverage. They are heads-down solving hard technical problems in satellite data pipelines, distributed ledger systems, and remote sensing AI models. It is worth noting that they are also, in my view, building some of the most important technology companies of the next decade.

If you are an engineer who wants to work on something that genuinely matters: this is your moment.
