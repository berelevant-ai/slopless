# How We Went From 500 to 50,000 Developer Users in Eight Months

Eight months ago, Forge had 500 developers using our CLI. Today we have 50,000. We did not spend our way to that growth. Here is the honest breakdown.

The first thing we did was stop trying to grow. That sounds counterintuitive, but hear me out. We had been so focused on top-of-funnel acquisition that we had almost completely neglected activation. We were spending money to bring developers to the product and then watching them bounce within the first session because the time-to-first-value was too long. Notably, when we mapped the activation funnel and actually measured where developers were dropping off, the problem became obvious: our "hello world" experience required 14 steps and assumed a level of prior context that most new users did not have. That is not an onboarding problem. It is a product problem.

We reduced those 14 steps to 3. The new getting-started experience takes under 90 seconds. That single change improved 7-day retention by 38%. Importantly, better retention made every subsequent acquisition dollar more effective, which then made organic growth from word-of-mouth more compounding. The sequence matters enormously.

The second lever was what we call "first win" design. We had been thinking about our product in terms of its capabilities - all the powerful things Forge can do. We shifted to thinking about it in terms of the first concrete, specific win that a new developer experiences. Our secret sauce turned out to be one feature: automatic environment variable sync across local, staging, and production. Painful, universal, instantly understood. We made that the hero of our onboarding and it became the thing developers told their colleagues about.

It is worth noting that none of this required us to move fast and break things. We moved deliberately, measured rigorously, and iterated on what the data told us. The 100x growth is the output of a product-led discipline, not a growth hack.
