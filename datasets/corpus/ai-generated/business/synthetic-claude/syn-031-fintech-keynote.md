# Keynote Abstract: Open Banking Is Not a Regulation. It Is a Platform Shift.

Most financial institutions are treating open banking as a compliance project. The ones that will win the next decade are treating it as a platform strategy. In this keynote, I will explain why that distinction is the single most important strategic question facing financial services executives today.

Open banking mandates have done something remarkable: they have forced the commoditization of financial data access. Account aggregation, transaction history, balance verification - these are no longer proprietary advantages. They are baseline capabilities. Notably, this makes the financial services business more competitive in the short term, and it creates the conditions for a fundamentally different kind of financial product in the medium term. Importantly, the institutions that understand this - that the commoditization of data access is not a threat but a platform primitive - are moving to build the value-added services that data access enables rather than trying to defend the data access itself.

I will share three frameworks that the most forward-thinking financial institutions are using to navigate this shift. First, the distinction between owning the customer relationship and owning the data - and why the former matters and the latter increasingly does not. Second, how best-of-breed API-first institutions are building the developer ecosystems that pave the way for distribution through products they did not build. Third, the specific organizational changes required to operate as a platform rather than a product - and why this is as much a culture change as a technology change.

It is worth noting that the window for incumbents to define the platform layer is not unlimited. Fintechs and technology companies are actively building alternative platform positions, and the open banking ecosystem rewards speed of integration. The financial institutions that move decisively in the next 18 months will define the competitive structure of the industry for the following decade.

This is a session for executives who are ready to stop asking "how do we comply?" and start asking "how do we compete?"
