# 7 Things You Should Know About Business Credit

Building strong business credit is one of the smartest financial moves any company owner can make. Yet it is also one of the most misunderstood topics in the small business world. Many entrepreneurs do not even realize their business has a separate credit profile until they need financing and find themselves starting from scratch. Here is what you need to know.

**1. Business Credit Is Separate from Personal Credit**

Your personal credit score and your business credit profile are two completely distinct things. Business credit is tied to your company's EIN rather than your Social Security number. Keeping them separate protects your personal finances and opens up a wider range of funding options as your business grows.

**2. You Have to Actively Build It**

Business credit does not build itself the way personal credit might over time. You need to deliberately open accounts, use them responsibly, and make sure they are being reported to the major business credit bureaus - Dun and Bradstreet, Equifax Business, and Experian Business. This takes intentional effort and consistency.

**3. Your DUNS Number Is a Critical Starting Point**

Dun and Bradstreet assigns a unique nine-digit identifier called a DUNS number to every business. Many lenders, suppliers, and even government contracts require one. Registering for a DUNS number is free and is one of the first steps in establishing a credible business credit profile.

**4. Vendor Credit Is a Powerful Entry Point**

Many new businesses start building credit by working with vendors who offer net-30 or net-60 payment terms and report those payments to credit bureaus. These trade lines are often easier to obtain than traditional loans and can quickly add positive history to your business credit file.

**5. Payment History Is the Most Important Factor**

Just as with personal credit, paying your bills on time is the single most important thing you can do for your business credit score. Late payments can significantly damage your profile, while a consistent record of on-time payments builds credibility with lenders and suppliers over time.

**6. Strong Business Credit Unlocks Better Financing**

When your business has a solid credit profile, you gain access to higher credit limits, lower interest rates, and more favorable terms on loans and lines of credit. This can make a meaningful difference in your ability to invest in growth, manage cash flow, and weather unexpected expenses.

**7. Monitor Your Business Credit Regularly**

Errors on credit reports are surprisingly common, and they can have real consequences if left uncorrected. Check your business credit reports regularly across all three major bureaus and dispute any inaccuracies you find promptly. Staying on top of your credit profile is an ongoing responsibility, not a one-time task.

Understanding and actively managing your business credit is a strategic advantage that too many small business owners overlook. Start building it early, and it will pay dividends for years to come.
