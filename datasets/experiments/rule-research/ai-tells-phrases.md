## 1. High-Frequency Single Words (Verbs)

These verbs appear 5-200x more often in AI output than in matched human writing. Most have legitimate uses; the signal is density and context.

- **delve** - the single most flagged AI verb; spiked 9-25x in academic writing after Nov 2022 (Max Planck, NIH studies); rarely used organically at this rate. Low FP risk in normal prose - if it appears, it's usually genuine AI output.
- **underscore** (as a verb, "this underscores...") - used to assert significance without explaining it; rare in natural speech. Low FP risk.
- **leverage** - corporate metonym for "use"; legitimate in finance but overused as generic verb. Medium FP risk in business writing.
- **foster** - implies intentional cultivation; AI uses it for any positive causation. Medium FP risk.
- **harness** - "harness the power of"; legitimate in engineering contexts, AI overextends to abstract nouns. Medium FP risk.
- **navigate** (figurative) - "navigate the complexities/landscape"; rarely means literal navigation in AI text. Medium FP risk.
- **elevate** - used for any improvement regardless of magnitude. Medium FP risk.
- **embark** - almost always precedes "on a journey"; theatrical for mundane actions. Low FP risk.
- **showcase** - neutral word AI uses where "show" or "demonstrate" would suffice. Medium FP risk.
- **underscore** / **highlight** / **emphasize** - three near-synonyms AI rotates through (synonym cycling). Medium FP risk.
- **bolster** - inflated synonym for "support" or "strengthen". Low FP risk.
- **encompass** - AI prefers over "include" or "cover". Medium FP risk.
- **resonate** - applied to any positive audience reaction; vague. Medium FP risk.
- **catalyze** - AI extends chemistry term to any change. Low FP risk in non-science writing.
- **galvanize** - same pattern as catalyze. Low FP risk.
- **elucidate** - formal synonym for "explain"; AI uses over-formally. Low FP risk.
- **illuminate** (figurative) - "illuminates the complexity"; metaphor AI applies mechanically. Medium FP risk.
- **reimagine** - marketing-speak for any change. Medium FP risk.
- **revolutionize** - used for incremental improvements. Low FP risk - human writers use it too but less mechanically.
- **transcend** - AI applies to routine improvements ("transcends the ordinary"). Medium FP risk.
- **unpack** - trendy synonym for "explain" or "analyze"; has spread to human writing. High FP risk.
- **garner** - slightly formal "earn/receive"; AI prefers it over "get" or "earn". Medium FP risk.
- **grapple** - "grapple with complexity"; sounds engaged without specificity. Low FP risk.
- **juxtapose** - AI applies to any comparison. Medium FP risk.
- **pave the way** - compound verb AI uses for any enabling action. Medium FP risk.
- **shed light on** - near-cliche even outside AI; flagged in NIH study. High FP risk.
- **boast** (applied to things/products) - "the platform boasts..." - copula avoidance pattern. Low FP risk.
- **serves as** / **stands as** / **marks** / **represents** / **features** - copula avoidance; AI substitutes these for simple "is/has". Medium FP risk individually, strong signal in clusters.
- **cultivate** - applied to anything grown metaphorically. Medium FP risk.
- **augment** - AI prefers over "add to" or "improve". Low FP risk.
- **commence** - very formal "begin"; AI formality inflation. Low FP risk.
- **ascertain** - formal "find out"; same pattern. Low FP risk.

## 2. High-Frequency Single Words (Adjectives)

- **pivotal** - most flagged adjective; used for any importance regardless of whether things actually pivot. Low FP risk.
- **robust** - used for anything described as strong, reliable, or comprehensive. Low FP risk in non-engineering contexts.
- **seamless** - applied to any integration or process. Low FP risk.
- **intricate** / **intricacies of** - signals complexity without describing it. Low FP risk.
- **multifaceted** - signals complexity; rarely followed by the actual facets. Low FP risk.
- **meticulous** / **meticulously** - applied indiscriminately to any careful process. Low FP risk.
- **comprehensive** - means "complete" but AI uses as filler praise. Medium FP risk.
- **holistic** - buzzword for "considers multiple aspects". Medium FP risk.
- **nuanced** (as empty praise) - applied without specifying the nuance. Medium FP risk. (Note: "nuanced" in academic contexts has higher FP risk.)
- **vibrant** - tourism-brochure adjective AI applies to communities, ecosystems, cultures. Medium FP risk in travel writing (high FP), low elsewhere.
- **groundbreaking** - used for routine developments. Medium FP risk.
- **transformative** - same as groundbreaking. Medium FP risk.
- **cutting-edge** - marketing adjective for anything modern. Low FP risk.
- **ever-evolving** / **ever-changing** - compound that sounds dynamic without saying what changed. Low FP risk.
- **dynamic** - used as filler modifier ("dynamic landscape", "dynamic environment"). High FP risk - legitimate in many human contexts.
- **crucial** / **vital** / **essential** - near-synonyms AI rotates for "important". High FP risk individually (common human words), signal is density/rotation.
- **paramount** - elevated synonym for "most important". Low FP risk.
- **compelling** - applied to any argument, content, or evidence. Medium FP risk.
- **innovative** - used for anything new regardless of actual novelty. Medium FP risk.
- **unprecedented** - used for events that clearly have precedents. Low FP risk.
- **indelible** - "left an indelible mark"; formulaic phrase. Low FP risk.
- **actionable** - business-jargon filler ("actionable insights"). Low FP risk in non-business writing.
- **burgeoning** - slightly formal "growing"; AI prefers it. Low FP risk.
- **nascent** - "nascent field/technology"; semi-formal. Low FP risk.
- **quintessential** - applied to examples that may not actually be quintessential. Low FP risk.
- **overarching** - signals hierarchy without specifying it. Medium FP risk.
- **commendable** - praise word that sounds evaluative rather than analytical. Low FP risk.
- **profound** - applied to routine insights. Medium FP risk.
- **enduring** - AI applies to any lasting thing. Medium FP risk.
- **bespoke** - slightly archaic "custom/tailored"; AI reaches for it for formality. Low FP risk.
- **nestled** - almost exclusively in tourism-brochure contexts AI generates ("nestled in the heart of"). Low FP risk.
- **bustling** - same tourism-brochure pattern. Low FP risk.

## 3. High-Frequency Single Words (Nouns)

- **tapestry** - most flagged AI noun; "a rich tapestry of..." used for any complex collection. Very low FP risk. Confirmed 227x overuse vs humans (IsGPT data).
- **landscape** (figurative) - "the competitive landscape", "digital landscape", "evolving landscape"; AI overextends the geographic metaphor. High FP risk individually, signal is combination with other tells.
- **realm** - "in the realm of"; formal substitute for "area" or "field". Low FP risk.
- **testament** - "a testament to"; applied to any positive outcome. Low FP risk. Confirmed 319x overuse for "indelible mark" phrase (IsGPT data).
- **beacon** - "stands as a beacon of"; applied to any positive example. Low FP risk.
- **treasure trove** - applied indiscriminately as a metaphor for any collection of useful things. Low FP risk.
- **synergy** - corporate buzzword; AI applies beyond business contexts. Low FP risk in non-business writing.
- **interplay** - "the interplay between X and Y"; signals relationship without describing it. Low FP risk.
- **journey** - used for any process or experience, not literal travel. High FP risk in some genres (memoir), low elsewhere.
- **paradigm** - "paradigm shift" applied to incremental changes. Medium FP risk.
- **ecosystem** - extended from biological to any community/system. High FP risk in tech writing, lower elsewhere.
- **mosaic** / **symphony** / **constellation** / **prism** / **fabric** - abstract visual metaphors AI cycles through as tapestry variants. Low FP risk as a cluster.
- **ethos** - reaches for formality where "values" or "culture" would do. Low FP risk.
- **cornerstone** - "a cornerstone of"; used for anything important. Medium FP risk.
- **milestone** - used for routine events. High FP risk.
- **essence** - "the essence of"; vague depth signal. Medium FP risk.
- **understanding** (as a noun object) - "gain a deeper understanding" (739x overuse - IsGPT data). Low FP risk in this phrase form.
- **implications** - "the implications of"; AI uses as section-ender without specifying them. Medium FP risk.
- **nuance** (as noun) - "the nuances of"; same as the adjective pattern. Medium FP risk.
- **complexity** / **complexities** - "navigate the complexities of"; rarely followed by what's actually complex. Medium FP risk.
- **insight** / **insights** - "gain valuable insights", "provide valuable insights" (confirmed 902x and 739x overuse vs humans - IsGPT data). Low FP risk in this phrase form.
- **framework** - applied to anything organized. High FP risk in tech/academic writing.
- **blueprint** - "a blueprint for"; applied to any plan. Medium FP risk.
- **roadmap** - applied to any sequence of steps. High FP risk in product/tech writing.
- **zeitgeist** - anachronistic noun AI reaches for to sound culturally aware. Low FP risk.
- **endeavor** - formal "effort/attempt"; AI over-formalizes. Low FP risk.

## 4. Overused Adverbs

- **seamlessly** - "integrates seamlessly"; always. Low FP risk as adverb.
- **meticulously** - same as adjective pattern. Low FP risk.
- **notably** - sentence opener that asserts notability without justifying it. Medium FP risk.
- **crucially** - same pattern as notably. Medium FP risk.
- **importantly** - same pattern. High FP risk.
- **essentially** - filler hedge. High FP risk.
- **ultimately** - conclusion opener AI uses to signal resolution. Medium FP risk as a standalone sentence opener.
- **undoubtedly** - asserts certainty without evidence. Low FP risk.
- **certainly** - chatbot conversational tic; signals accommodation ("certainly, I can help"). Low FP risk outside chatbot output.
- **arguably** - hedge that sounds balanced but is often followed by an uncontested point. Medium FP risk.
- **predominantly** / **primarily** - both flagged in NIH study; AI overuses as hedges for simple statements. Medium FP risk.
- **profoundly** - inflated adverb for modest effects. Low FP risk.
- **significantly** - overused as emphasis even when effect is small. High FP risk individually, signal is density.

## 5. Transition and Signpost Phrases

These are heavily overused sentence starters and connectors. AI clusters them; human writers use them singly.

**Additive transitions AI overuses:**
- Moreover
- Furthermore
- Additionally
- In addition to
- Also (sentence-start)
- Notably
- Indeed

**Adversative transitions AI overuses:**
- However (sentence-start, overused)
- Nevertheless
- Nonetheless
- Notwithstanding
- On the other hand
- In contrast
- On the contrary
- That said / With that said
- Despite

**Causal transitions:**
- Consequently
- Therefore / Thus / Hence
- As a result
- Due to the fact that (verbose form)
- In light of the fact that (verbose form)

**Conclusion markers:**
- In conclusion
- In summary / To summarize / To sum up
- Ultimately (as conclusion opener)
- Overall (as conclusion opener)
- At the end of the day
- In the final analysis
- In brief / In closing
- All things considered
- To wrap things up
- All in all

Note: Wikipedia's "Signs of AI Writing" flags these specifically because they appear at end of sections in Wikipedia articles, which rarely use section summaries.

**False-context starters (high FP risk individually, strong in clusters):**
- In today's [X] / In today's digital age / In today's fast-paced world
- In the era of / In an era where
- In the ever-evolving landscape of / In the constantly evolving field of
- In the realm of / In the world of / In the context of
- In the age of
- With the advent of
- As we navigate / As we explore / As we examine

## 6. Sentence Templates and Slot-Filler Patterns

These are structural templates where only the slot contents vary. Each is a signal by itself.

**False-contrast template (very strong signal):**
- "It's not just X - it's Y."
- "It's not X, it's Y."
- "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "Not only X, but also Y."
- "Not only does it X, but it also Y."
- "No X. No Y. Just Z." (fragment variant)
Wikipedia specifically flags this as a "Negative Parallelism" pattern.

**"Whether you're X or Y" template:**
- "Whether you're a beginner or an expert..."
- "Whether you're looking for X or Y..."
- "Whether you're X, Y, or Z..."
Signals false inclusiveness; creates artificial breadth without substance.

**"From X to Y" template:**
- "From ancient civilizations to modern startups..."
- "From the Big Bang to dark matter..."
Creates sweeping scope impression without actual analysis.

**Opening frame templates:**
- "In today's fast-paced/rapidly evolving/digital world..."
- "In an era where [X is increasingly/more important than ever]..."
- "In the ever-evolving world of..."
- "As [subject] continues to evolve..."
- "As we look to the future..."

**Throat-clearing preambles:**
- "It's important to note that..."
- "It's worth noting that..."
- "It should be noted that..."
- "It's worth mentioning that..."
- "It goes without saying that..."
- "Needless to say..."
- "One of the most important/significant..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "At its core..."
- "At the end of the day..."

**Invitation-to-explore templates:**
- "Let's delve into..."
- "Let's dive into..."
- "Let's explore..."
- "Let's unpack..."
- "Let's take a closer look..."
- "Let's examine this further..."
- "Let us consider..."
- "Let's break this down..."
- "Embarking on a journey..."
- "Delving into..."
- "Navigating the complexities of..."

**Importance-assertion templates:**
- "plays a crucial/vital/key role in..."
- "plays a pivotal role in shaping..."
- "cannot be overstated"
- "is vital for..."
- "is essential to..."
- "serves as a reminder that..."
- "underscores its importance"
- "stands as a testament to..."
- "stands as a beacon of..."
- "reflects a broader trend toward..."

**Significance-inflation templates:**
- "marking a pivotal moment"
- "a watershed moment"
- "a turning point in..."
- "has revolutionized the way we [verb]"
- "has transformed the landscape of..."
- "left an indelible mark" (confirmed 319x overuse - IsGPT data)

**Data/insight templates (very high frequency overuse):**
- "provide valuable insights" (confirmed 902x overuse vs humans - IsGPT data)
- "gain valuable insights" (confirmed 739x overuse)
- "offers valuable insights" (confirmed 298x overuse)
- "gain a comprehensive understanding" (confirmed 355x overuse)
- "gain a deeper understanding" (confirmed 131x overuse)
- "sheds light on..."
- "study sheds light on" (confirmed 130x overuse)
- "opens new avenues" (confirmed 206x overuse)
- "significant contributions to the field"

**Generic future-closer template:**
- "[Subject] may/could/will/is poised to become the [most X] [Y]."
- "As we move forward..."
- "Moving forward..."
- "In the not-too-distant future..."
- "The future holds..."

**"Challenges and opportunities" closer:**
- "Despite [positive words], [subject] faces challenges..."
- "While X is impressive, Y remains a challenge."
- "Despite challenges, [subject] continues to thrive."
This is flagged by Wikipedia as a rigid structural pattern that closes articles without specificity.

**Confirmational authority templates:**
- "The reality is..."
- "The truth is..."
- "The key is..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Let's be honest..."
- "The fact is..."
These assert certainty without evidence; Bloomberry classifies them as a distinct cadence pattern.

**Rhetorical question then self-answer:**
- "The result? [X]."
- "The goal? [X]."
- "The bottom line? [X]."
- "The catch? [X]."
- "The kicker? [X]."
- "Plot twist: [X]."
These are infomercial hooks; AI uses them to sound punchy.

**Motivational closer:**
- "The question isn't whether [X will happen]. It's whether you're [prepared/ready]."
- "The question isn't X. It's Y."
Bloomberry documents this as a distinct cadence (Modal-framed urgency).

## 7. Hedging Patterns

AI hedges either excessively (epistemic cowardice) or with stacked hedges (double hedges).

**Single hedges (high FP risk individually, signal is density):**
- perhaps, possibly, likely, potentially, seemingly, arguably
- it seems that, it appears that, it feels like, it would seem that
- may, might, could, should (modal overuse)
- often, sometimes, many, some, usually, typically, frequently (hedge adverbs)

**Double/stacked hedges (low FP risk - rare in human writing):**
- "could potentially..."
- "may eventually..."
- "might ultimately..."
- "can often be beneficial..."
- "may potentially help..."

**Epistemic preambles:**
- "it's important to note that..." (also under signposts)
- "it cannot be overstated that..."
- "it is worth considering that..."
- "one could argue that..."
- "to a certain extent..."

**Vague attribution (AI gap-filling):**
- "Experts believe..."
- "Studies show..." / "Research suggests..." (without citation)
- "Industry leaders agree..."
- "Observers have noted..."
- "It is widely believed that..."
- "is believed to have..." / "likely began..." / "appears to have studied..." (speculative gap-filling; Wikipedia flags this specifically)
- "maintains a relatively low public profile" (hedge for missing information)

## 8. Structural and Formatting Patterns

**Macro-structure tells:**
- Sweeping contextual opening (landscape/era framing) before main point
- Rigid "Challenges and Future" section closing with vague positivity
- Section-ending summaries in prose ("In summary, this section has shown...")
- Conclusion that restates the introduction without adding information
- Article lead that opens: "X refers to..." or "X is the..." (list-article pattern)
- "This article explores..." / "This paper aims to..." / "The purpose of this study is to..."

**Formatting tells:**
- Excessive bullet lists where prose is appropriate (Wikipedia: "virtually nonexistent" in human Wikipedia articles)
- Bold headers inside bullets ("**Term**: Definition text") where bold header is just a restatement
- Title Case in section headings (capitalize every noun)
- Em dash overuse: more than 1 per 500 words (legitimate human use is much lower)
- Curly quotation marks (smart quotes; weak signal in plain text)
- 5+ consecutive short noun-phrase bullets with no verbs
- 3+ headings in under 300 words
- Uniform paragraph length throughout (15-25 word sentences consistently)
- Excessive bolding of keywords ("key takeaways" styling)

**Sentence-level uniformity:**
- All sentences 15-25 words (metronomic pacing)
- Absence of fragments, run-ons, or parenthetical asides
- Consistent Oxford comma use
- Minimal contractions
- American English spelling exclusively
- Near-zero spelling errors

**Copula avoidance:**
AI substitutes "is/are/has" with: serves as, stands as, marks, represents, features, boasts, presents, maintains, offers, refers to.
This signals AI even when individual substitutes are individually unremarkable.

**Synonym cycling:**
Rotating near-synonyms to avoid repeating a word: developers/engineers/practitioners/builders in same paragraph; crucial/vital/essential/key across sections. Humans repeat words; AI rotates.

**Rule of Three compulsion:**
Three-item adjective lists, three-part structures, triadic phrases appear in nearly every paragraph. Human writing varies list length; AI defaults to three.

## 10. Creative/Fiction-Specific Tells

Flagged in arXiv papers on AI fiction (StoryScope 2025, AI Fiction Paradox 2025).

**Hedged action patterns:**
- "began to [verb]" instead of the action itself ("began to walk" vs "walked")
- "seemed to [verb/adjective]" ("seemed to understand", "seemed hesitant")
- "started to [verb]"
- "appeared to [verb]"
These are indirect constructions; AI uses them to hedge narrative assertions.

**Character voice clichés:**
- "voice barely above a whisper" (documented formula across AI fiction)
- "eyes filled with tears"
- "heart pounding in her chest"
- Character name defaults: Emily, Sarah, Ethan, Lucas (AI fiction naming patterns)

**Over-explanation of theme:**
AI fiction states themes explicitly rather than embedding them in action. Often appears as a last paragraph moral.

**Tidy single-track plots:**
Human fiction has temporal complexity (flashbacks, nonlinear structure) and moral ambiguity. AI fiction defaults to linear chronology with unambiguous protagonist choices.

**Emotional flatline patterns:**
- "What surprised me most..." (claims surprise without conveying it)
- "I was fascinated to discover..."
- "What struck me was..."
- "hits different" / "hit differently"
These assert emotion rather than create it.

## 11. Empty Emphasis and Inflation Patterns

**Hollow intensifiers:**
- "genuine" / "real" / "actual" (inflation: "genuine utility", "real on-chain tokenomics") - asserts authenticity without demonstrating it
- "truly unique"
- "absolutely essential"
- "extremely helpful"
- "deeply insightful"
- "remarkably important"
- "quite frankly"
- "to be honest" / "let's be honest"
- "let's be clear"

**Self-labeling significance (claiming rather than earning):**
- "That last point is the key one."
- "This is where it gets interesting."
- "Here's where it gets clever."
- "This is the important part."
- "This is the contrarian take."
Human writers earn the reader's attention; AI announces it.

**Novelty inflation:**
- "a concept nobody is naming"
- "a failure mode nobody talks about"
- "a term he introduced"
- "coined the phrase"
Used to create false originality; the concept named is usually well-known.

**False range sweeps:**
- "from the Big Bang to dark matter"
- "from ancient civilizations to modern startups"
- "spanning centuries of..."
Creates scope impression without actual breadth of analysis.

**Promotional language patterns (tourism-brochure tell):**
- "nestled within breathtaking..."
- "vibrant hub of..."
- "thriving ecosystem of..."
- "natural beauty of..."
- "in the heart of..."
Wikipedia flags this as "promotional tone" that appears in AI-generated Wikipedia articles about places and companies.

## 13. False-Positive Risk Summary

Words with HIGH false-positive risk in normal human writing (use density/cluster signals, not presence):
- dynamic, crucial, vital, essential, key, important, innovative, comprehensive, ecosystem, framework, milestone, roadmap, journey (in some genres), however (sentence-start), therefore, notably, essentially, ultimately, often, sometimes, significant/significantly

Words with LOW false-positive risk (rare in uncoached human writing at AI frequency):

- harness, navigate, foster, elevate, unleash, streamline, empower, bolster, spearhead, resonate, revolutionize, facilitate, underpin, nuanced, multifaceted, myriad, plethora, encompass

## 14. Bloomberry Research: Cadence DNA Patterns

Bloomberry (Jan-Mar 2026, 7,400+ entries across ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini) identified an "AI Sentence DNA": a consistent four-part structure appearing regardless of topic.

**The AI four-part structure:**
1. Opening (temporal or landscape frame)
2. Expansion (elaboration with hedges)
3. Contrast ("However..." or "That said...")
4. Resolution (positive or forward-looking close)

This macro-rhythm is independent of word choice and harder to detect with simple word lists.

**17 documented hook patterns:**
- "In today's landscape..."
- "In a world where..."
- "Have you ever wondered..."
- "Let's be honest..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- Contrarian opener: "Most people believe X. They're wrong."
- Statistical opener: "Studies show that X percent of..."
- Empathy opener: "If you've ever struggled with..."
- Definitional opener: "X is not just Y - it's Z."
- Urgency opener
- Question-and-answer opener
- Anecdote opener with named protagonist
- Challenge-first opener
- Future-state opener
- Confession opener
- Provocative claim opener
- Rhetorical reversal opener

**129 documented cadence structures**, including:
- Rhetorical Contrast: Negative frame -> pivot -> positive reframe
- Motivational Cadence: Short claim -> evidence -> imperative
- Generic Opener: Temporal frame -> generalization -> main claim
- Aphorism Pattern: Abstract concept -> simple predicate ("Clarity is speed.")
- Infomercial Hook: "The result? [Answer]."
