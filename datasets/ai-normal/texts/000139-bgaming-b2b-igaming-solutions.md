# iGaming Solutions: a complete guide to B2B services for operators

On launch day, a player's first deposit and first spin depend on suppliers that have never met. The payment gateway, the account system and game content from a studio such as [BGaming](https://bgaming.com/) must all agree on one balance. B2B solutions for iGaming exist to make those connections work, and the deployment model you choose decides who keeps them working after launch.

## What Are iGaming Solutions?

A registered player is only a prospect until money arrives in a wallet and a game loads against that balance. iGaming solutions are the technology and operational solutions that unite registration, deposit and gameplay: software, infrastructure and the services that keep them running. B2B services for iGaming are the parts of an online gambling business a player never sees but relies on every session.

Most operators combine components from several suppliers rather than writing them in-house:

- Platform development and player account management (PAM)
- Game content and game aggregation
- Payment systems and fraud screening
- Regulatory compliance, customer relationship management and business intelligence
- Hosting and host infrastructure

**An end-to-end iGaming platform can coordinate these responsibilities through a single broad supplier, through specialists stitched together by the operator, or through a mix of both.** The term describes a scope of service, not a single product.

That scope matters because whatever the package excludes remains your work. A supplier may deliver accounts, wallets and reporting while leaving licensing, payment contracts and support to you. **Read the package boundary before comparing prices**, because the technology and operational services outside it determine how large a team you need on day one.

## Why operators rely on specialist B2B providers

Every extra player session adds work behind the scenes: another authentication, another wallet update, another record for the regulator. Licensed markets show the scale involved. The [Gambling Commission](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/statistics-and-research/publication/industry-statistics-annual-report-financial-year-april-2024-to-march-2025) reported on 25 November 2025 that Great Britain's licensed remote casino, betting and bingo sector generated £7.8 billion in gross gambling yield between April 2024 and March 2025, up 13.1% on the previous year.

That figure describes one jurisdiction's gambling activity, not the size of the worldwide iGaming B2B software market. It does not count sessions, transactions or compliance records either. It gives operating-scale context for iGaming operators licensed there: if sessions and transactions rise with that activity, compliance records rise with them, and the technology underneath is expected to keep pace.

**Specialist providers absorb the engineering that every operator would otherwise repeat.** A vendor develops the platform once, maintains integrations with payment providers and game studios, and spreads server, maintenance and software-update costs across its clients. Each operator configures capabilities that already exist instead of funding their creation.

Outsourcing brings flexibility as well as savings. An operator can add an integrated service when a market opens, drop one that underperforms, and keep its own staff focused on product, marketing and player relationships. **Building every component internally ties the same budget to maintenance** before a single player signs up.

## B2B vs B2C iGaming Software

Running a casino and supplying its software are different businesses with different customers. B2C iGaming software faces players: the lobby, the registration form, the cashier and the game window on direct-to-consumer gaming sites. B2B software faces operators: the platform, the wallet, the integration layer and the back office that make those sites possible.

The B2B side of the gaming services differs from the consumer side on four points:

- Audience: the supplier's customer is the operator, and the operator's customer is the player.
- Revenue: suppliers earn licence fees, setup charges or revenue share, while operators earn from gambling activity.
- Architecture: supplier platforms usually run many clients on one codebase, with each client's data and configuration isolated.
- Use case: suppliers scale a product across markets, while operators scale a brand within them.

**The two models are connected rather than opposed.** Supplier charges fund the technology, and the operator carries the gambling licence, player relationships, support and marketing. A consumer-facing casino can buy most of its stack on a software-as-a-service basis and keep paying as it launches, operates and scales.

The practical consequence is that your supplier's incentives follow your revenue. **A revenue-share model rewards the vendor when you grow, while a flat licence fee does not.** Knowing which side of the relationship you occupy makes the deployment choices below easier to judge.

## Types of B2B iGaming Solutions

How much of the iGaming stack will your team own? White-label and turnkey solutions, modular composition and bespoke platform development place a different share of the B2B infrastructure in your hands. The right answer follows your business model, budget and technical capability, and shapes the digital gambling experience you can offer. Whichever route you take should stay customisable enough to serve your target markets.

### White Label Solutions

A white-label platform is a finished casino waiting for your brand. The provider already runs accounts, wallets, games and payments, so your implementation work shrinks to branding, configuration and content selection. **Technical setup and business preparation can run in parallel**, which is why the model suits operators who want a standard launch quickly.

The constraint is the shared core. Because many clients run on one platform, deep changes to features or flows are rarely possible, and your roadmap follows the provider's. In exchange, the provider maintains and improves functionality for everyone, and you avoid a development budget before revenue arrives.

Weigh ongoing charges and provider dependency against that saving. Check who holds the licence: white-label arrangements can use the provider's ready-made licensed product, while turnkey arrangements can operate under the operator's own licence, so verify licensing responsibilities in the proposed package. **Configuration is only one of the [online casino launch steps](https://bgaming.com/articles/5-exact-steps-to-create-an-online-casino-website)**, which include licensing, marketing and provider contracts when you start an online casino.

White label fits best when your differentiation lives in marketing and player relationships rather than in product mechanics. If your plan depends on features no other brand has, the next two models apply.

### Bespoke Solutions

Some products cannot be configured into existence. A tournament format no vendor offers, a wallet that behaves unusually across brands, or an interface built around one specific audience may all require ownership of platform development. **Bespoke development buys control over features, interfaces and architecture**, and that control is the only reason to choose it.

Control arrives with delivery responsibility. A custom build moves through stages that each have to be staffed:

1. Requirements gathering and system design
2. Engineering
3. Integration with payment and game suppliers
4. Quality assurance and regulatory preparation

The same team stays responsible for maintenance, security patches and compliance updates after launch. Implementation time follows scope rather than a fixed calendar: a narrow platform with few integrations finishes sooner than a multi-brand, multi-jurisdiction build, so estimate duration from your own requirements list instead of a supplier's generic timeline.

**Custom ownership does not cut you off from external content.** Game studios expect to integrate with operator-built platforms, and BGaming supports custom-platform integration alongside direct and platform-powered setups. Bespoke therefore means owning the core, not building every component yourself.

### Modular Solutions

Imagine swapping one payment provider for another without touching your game lobby. That is the promise of modular architecture: each capability is integrated separately through APIs, so you can add a supplier to an existing stack, expand one area independently or replace a component that underperforms.

**Component choice gives modular operators the most control over user experience short of a full bespoke build.** You pick the PAM, the aggregator, the payment gateway and the CRM that fit your markets, and you keep the option to change any of them. The price is a heavier technical management load than a white-label contract carries.

That load is integration ownership. Games, payments, identity checks, bonuses and reporting all share account and transaction data, so a change in one can break another. Someone has to own coordinated testing, monitoring and support across suppliers, and that someone is usually the operator.

Plan each connection as a project with its own testing and monitoring stages rather than a one-off technical task. **Scalability in a modular stack is only as good as its weakest integration**, so a guide to [casino API integration](https://bgaming.com/articles/api-integration-casino-games-what-do-you-need-to-know) is worth reading before the first component goes live.

## What do B2B iGaming solutions include?

### Casino Platform Development

The platform is where separate services meet one coherent account. A registration lands in player account management (PAM), a deposit moves through payment systems into a wallet, and a game round is fetched through game aggregation and settled against that wallet. **Platform development is the work of making those handoffs reliable** for both the player-facing front end and the back office.

Around that core path, the platform exposes connection points for reporting, customer management, bonuses and security. It also hosts or connects the supporting services most operators add:

- Sportsbook software for betting markets
- Customer relationship management for segmentation and campaigns
- Business intelligence for performance reporting
- B2B compliance tools for regulatory compliance checks

Underneath sits the host infrastructure: gaming servers, databases, load balancers and content delivery networks, plus the payment gateways, security layers and APIs that connect everything. When evaluating a platform, ask how it behaves when one connected service fails. **Graceful handling of a slow payment provider or an unavailable game studio** separates a platform core from a loose collection of integrations.

### Player Account Management (PAM)

A deposit limit set on Monday must still apply on Friday, on a different device, in a different game. PAM is the system that makes one account mean the same thing everywhere. It holds the player's identity, wallet, session history and controls, and it serves every other component that needs them.

Core PAM functions typically cover:

- Registration and authentication
- Wallet balances and transaction history
- KYC and AML workflows
- Multi-brand support from one account database
- Responsible gaming controls such as deposit limits and self-exclusion

**Evaluate PAM on whether staff can enforce controls consistently, not on feature count.** Centralised records and secure back-office access let a support agent see the same balance, limits and verification status the player sees. Configurable identity checks, risk rules and brand management decide how much of that enforcement runs automatically and how much needs a human.

Because PAM holds credentials and money, it is also the main target for account takeover and fraud. Authentication strength, encryption and monitoring belong in the evaluation, and a guide to [player-account security](https://bgaming.com/articles/online-gambling-security-the-best-tips-for-gaming-operators) maps common threats to the measures that counter them. **Treat any gap there as a licensing risk**, not only a technical one.

One integration simplifies expansion, but it does not remove selection work. Which studios and titles you can offer still depends on market availability and on the configurations your aggregator supports, so check the catalogue against your target licences before signing.

### Payment Systems

A player who waits three days for a withdrawal remembers the wait longer than the win. Payment method choices set that waiting time, along with minimum deposits and failure rates, so compare processing conditions rather than payment logos.

[Betfair](https://support.betfair.com/app/answers/detail/10271-instant-bank-transfers--faqs/)'s guidance for GBP and EUR customers shows the gap between two bank options. Standard bank deposits and withdrawals take one to five days, with a £20 or €20 minimum deposit. The first Pay by Bank deposit can take up to 90 minutes, later deposits and withdrawals are immediate, and the minimum falls to £5 or €5.

**A payment gateway is the routing layer that connects several payment service providers and chooses between them.** Routing rules can follow geography, currency and risk score, sending a card payment in one market to a different acquirer than the same card elsewhere. Security sits inside the flow: card authentication, transaction monitoring and reconciliation back to the PAM wallet complete the journey.

### Sportsbook Software

The moment a match kicks off, a sportsbook stops being a catalogue and becomes a live trading desk. A pre-match selection carries a price set hours earlier. In play, that price moves with every event, and your exposure on each outcome moves with it. **Odds management and risk management are continuous operating tasks**, not setup steps.

Sportsbook software covers the pieces behind that activity:

- Odds feeds and pricing across pre-match and in-play markets
- Bet types from singles to multiples and bet builders
- Live trading tools and exposure limits
- Virtual sports for continuous content
- Risk management at player and market level

Operators add a sportsbook solution when their audience already bets on sport elsewhere or when a market's licence covers both verticals. Deployment comes in two forms. API integration gives full front-end control at the cost of implementation work, while an embedded iFrame launches faster with the supplier's interface. **Either way, accounts, wallets and reporting must stay shared with the casino.**

When evaluating suppliers, check regional sports coverage, live odds latency, wallet integration, multi-currency support and how the service scales during major tournaments. A supplier strong in European football may be thin in cricket or basketball, so match coverage to your markets rather than to the brochure.

### CRM, Business Intelligence and Compliance Tools

A reload bonus sent to the wrong segment costs money twice: once in the bonus and again in the players it fails to retain. Customer relationship management tools exist to pick the right segment, using behaviour, preferences and deposit history, and to run the campaign through email, SMS or in-app messages.

**Business intelligence turns that campaign into a measurable decision.** By combining game, payment and marketing data, BI reports gross gaming revenue, net gaming revenue, retention and bonus return on investment per segment, so the next campaign starts from evidence. Player-behaviour analysis also feeds personalised marketing and fraud detection, as this guide to [game analytics](https://bgaming.com/articles/data-driven-era-of-igaming-4-ways-to-use-game-analytics-for-enhanced-player-experience) shows.

Compliance tools decide who is eligible in the first place. Onboarding checks, payment monitoring and campaign eligibility rules connect to target-market regulations and player-protection controls:

- KYC and AML verification
- Sanctions and politically exposed person screening
- Responsible gaming controls and intervention triggers
- Audit-ready records for regulators

Together these tools support long-term growth because they let an operator spend on players it can keep and lawfully serve. **Treat regulatory compliance as a data problem shared with marketing**, not as a separate department that only says no.

## How to choose the right iGaming solutions provider

Every one of the B2B iGaming providers you shortlist promises scalability, security and support. Those promises become useful only when matched to testable operating requirements. Write one requirements brief and judge every candidate iGaming solutions provider against it on two fronts: demonstrated operational fit and enforceable commercial responsibilities.

### Test Market Fit and Technical Ownership

A polished demo on a generic lobby proves little. Shortlist vendors against your target markets, licences, expected volumes, customisation needs and launch scope, and ask for references from operators with a similar profile. **Industry experience counts only when it matches your jurisdictions.**

Then test a representative workflow in a sandbox: register, deposit, play, request a withdrawal and check the back office. Alongside that workflow, inspect the evidence behind each vendor's claims:

- API documentation
- Security certifications and disaster-recovery evidence
- Peak-load results
- Who owns support when a connected service fails

### Compare Charges, Support and Exit Terms

**Minimum monthly charges can begin before your casino goes live.** A 2023 platform agreement between HR Entertainment and Pragmatic Solutions, [filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1947210/000175392623001609/g083517_ex10-13.htm), shows what published terms look like: a €40,000 setup fee, a €20,000 migration fee and monthly royalties of 2% on the first €1 million of GGR, falling to 1.5% above that, where GGR means wagers less winnings.

**Support and exit terms deserve equal scrutiny.** The same contract sets a €15,000 monthly minimum starting at go-live or six months after commencement, whichever comes first, and specifies:

- Priority-one incidents: a 15-minute response and 30-minute updates
- Qualifying service credits: 5% to 15% of monthly revenue share, subject to exclusions and an annual cap
- Termination duties: customer-data transfer planning
- Transition services: available on request for up to six months

Compare total cost, escalation paths, credit exclusions and transition duties across every proposal before you sign any of them.

## FAQ

### What are iGaming solutions?

They are the software, infrastructure and operational services used to launch and run online gambling businesses, connecting platforms, games, payments and compliance into one operating environment.

### What is the difference between B2B and B2C iGaming software?

B2B suppliers serve gambling businesses with platforms and tools. B2C operators serve players through consumer-facing sites, usually built on technology licensed from those B2B vendors.

### What services are included in B2B iGaming solutions?

Typical services cover platforms, PAM, game content, payments, sportsbooks, CRM, analytics, compliance and hosting, bought as individual modules or as an assembled platform.

### What is the difference between white-label and bespoke iGaming solutions?

White label configures an existing shared platform. Bespoke development gives far greater product control but requires the operator to own development, maintenance and continuing upkeep.

### Are turnkey iGaming solutions suitable for new operators?

Yes, when the bundled technology and integrations match the launch requirements. Confirm licensing arrangements, operating duties, supplier support commitments and ongoing charges before signing.

### Can operators combine multiple iGaming solutions?

Yes. APIs connect selected external services, but the operator must coordinate shared account and transaction data, integration testing, monitoring and support responsibilities across suppliers.

### What role does game aggregation play in iGaming?

Aggregation connects many studios through one operator-facing integration, simplifying catalogue access, content updates and market configuration across all participating game providers.

### How do I choose the right iGaming solutions provider?

Match documented requirements to market coverage, references and tested workflows, then compare total charges, technical ownership, support commitments and data-transfer terms across proposals.
