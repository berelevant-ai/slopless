# 7 Things You Should Know About Chocolate

Chocolate is one of the most beloved foods in the world, but there is far more going on beneath the surface than most people appreciate. From how it is made to how it affects your body, the story of chocolate is genuinely fascinating. Here are seven things you should know.

**1. All chocolate starts from the same place**

Whether you are eating a white chocolate bar, a creamy milk chocolate truffle, or a 90-percent dark chocolate square, the journey begins with cacao beans from the Theobroma cacao tree. The huge variety of end products comes from differences in processing, added ingredients, and the ratio of cacao components. Understanding this shared origin helps make sense of the broader chocolate landscape.

**2. The percentage on dark chocolate labels actually means something specific**

The number you see on dark chocolate packaging - 70%, 85%, and so on - refers to the total percentage of ingredients derived from the cacao bean, including cocoa solids and cocoa butter. The higher the percentage, the less room there is for sugar and other additions. Higher percentages generally mean a more bitter, complex flavor and a higher concentration of beneficial plant compounds.

**3. Processing method dramatically affects flavor and nutrition**

Cacao beans undergo fermentation, drying, roasting, and conching before becoming chocolate. Each step transforms the flavor profile in significant ways. Natural and Dutch-processed cocoa powder differ in acidity and taste because of how they are treated after roasting. Single-origin chocolates vary widely in flavor because of differences in growing conditions, bean variety, and processing choices.

**4. Dark chocolate contains compounds that are legitimately good for you**

This is not just marketing spin. High-quality dark chocolate is rich in flavonoids - plant compounds with antioxidant properties that have been associated with cardiovascular benefits in multiple research studies. However, it is worth noting that many of these studies used concentrated cocoa extracts rather than commercial chocolate bars, so the magnitude of real-world benefit from eating chocolate is probably more modest.

**5. Tempering is the secret behind professional chocolate's texture and snap**

If you have ever wondered why high-quality chocolate bars have that satisfying snap and glossy surface, the answer is tempering - a precise process of heating and cooling chocolate to specific temperatures. This encourages the cocoa butter to form stable crystal structures. Untempered chocolate blooms with a dull gray surface and crumbles rather than snapping cleanly.

**6. Storage is simpler than most people think**

Chocolate should be stored at cool room temperature - around 65 to 70 degrees Fahrenheit - away from light, moisture, and strong odors. Refrigerating chocolate is actually not recommended unless necessary, because the temperature fluctuations cause moisture condensation that leads to surface bloom and can pick up refrigerator odors. A cool, dark cabinet is ideal.

**7. The ethical sourcing conversation is genuinely important**

The chocolate industry has significant documented issues with labor practices, including child labor, in parts of West Africa where much of the world's cacao is grown. Certifications like Fairtrade and Direct Trade are imperfect but meaningful signals that producers are working toward better practices. Knowing where your chocolate comes from is becoming increasingly important for conscientious consumers.
