# 7 Things You Should Know About Olive Oil

Olive oil is one of the most beloved and widely used cooking fats in the world, but there is a lot of confusion and misinformation out there about how to choose, store, and use it properly. Whether you are a seasoned home cook or just getting started, here are seven essential things to understand about olive oil.

**1. "Extra virgin" is not just a marketing term**

Extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) is actually a specific grade defined by strict chemical and sensory standards. To qualify, the oil must be produced purely through mechanical means with no chemical processing, and it must meet acidity and flavor requirements. It represents the highest quality category and retains the most beneficial compounds from the olive fruit.

**2. Freshness matters more than most people think**

Unlike wine, olive oil does not improve with age - it degrades. The fresh harvest date on a bottle is far more useful than an expiration date, which can be set up to two years out. Ideally, you want to consume olive oil within 12 to 18 months of harvest and within a few months of opening the bottle.

**3. Light and heat are your biggest enemies**

Storing olive oil properly is crucial to preserving its quality. Light exposure and heat both accelerate oxidation, which causes the oil to go rancid. Store your olive oil in a cool, dark place - never on a sunny countertop next to the stove. Dark-colored glass bottles are better than clear ones for this reason.

**4. You can actually cook with extra virgin olive oil**

A persistent myth holds that extra virgin olive oil should not be used for cooking because of its smoke point. In reality, quality EVOO has a smoke point of around 375 to 405 degrees Fahrenheit, which is perfectly suitable for most home cooking including sauting, roasting, and pan frying. The high polyphenol content also makes it more stable under heat than many refined oils.

**5. Fraud in the olive oil industry is a documented problem**

Studies have found that a significant percentage of oils labeled as extra virgin in supermarkets do not actually meet the standard. Common practices include blending with cheaper refined olive oil or other vegetable oils. Buying from reputable producers with transparent sourcing is the best way to protect yourself.

**6. Flavor profiles vary enormously by region and variety**

Olive oils from different growing regions taste dramatically different from one another. Spanish oils tend toward a milder, buttery profile. Italian oils are often grassy and peppery. Greek oils lean robust and fruity. Exploring these differences is one of the most rewarding aspects of becoming a more knowledgeable olive oil consumer.

**7. The health benefits are well-supported by research**

The Mediterranean diet, which features olive oil as a central fat source, is one of the most studied dietary patterns in nutritional science. Research consistently links regular consumption of olive oil to reduced cardiovascular risk, anti-inflammatory effects, and other positive health outcomes. The polyphenols in high-quality EVOO are believed to play a major role.
