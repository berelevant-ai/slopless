# 7 Things You Should Know About Retirement Planning

Retirement might feel like a distant concern, especially if you are in your twenties or thirties, but the decisions you make today have an enormous impact on the quality of life you will enjoy in your later years. The earlier you start planning, the better off you will be. Here are seven crucial things everyone should understand about retirement planning.

**1. Starting early is the single most powerful thing you can do**

Thanks to the power of compound interest, time is genuinely your most valuable asset when it comes to retirement savings. Someone who starts saving at 25 will end up with significantly more money at retirement than someone who starts at 35, even if the late starter saves more money each month. Every year you wait to start is a year of compounding growth you can never get back.

**2. Employer matching is essentially free money**

If your employer offers a 401(k) match, contributing enough to capture the full match should be your absolute first financial priority. A typical employer might match 50 percent of your contributions up to 6 percent of your salary. If you are not contributing enough to get the full match, you are literally leaving part of your compensation on the table. This is one of the best and most guaranteed returns available in personal finance.

**3. Tax-advantaged accounts are incredibly powerful tools**

The two most commonly used retirement accounts in the United States are the 401(k) and the IRA. Traditional versions of these accounts give you a tax deduction now and you pay taxes on withdrawals in retirement. Roth versions work the opposite way: you contribute after-tax dollars now, but your money grows and can be withdrawn completely tax-free in retirement. Understanding which type is better for your situation can make a substantial difference in your overall wealth.

**4. You will need more money than you probably think**

A common rule of thumb is that you will need roughly 25 times your annual expenses saved by the time you retire. This is based on what is known as the "4 percent rule," which suggests that you can safely withdraw 4 percent of your portfolio each year without running out of money over a 30-year retirement. If you plan to spend $60,000 per year in retirement, that means you need $1.5 million saved. This number can feel daunting, but breaking it down into monthly savings goals makes it much more manageable.

**5. Social Security alone will not be enough**

Many people make the mistake of assuming that Social Security benefits will cover most of their retirement needs. The reality is that Social Security is designed to replace only about 40 percent of the average worker's pre-retirement income. For most people, that is simply not enough to maintain their desired lifestyle. Treating Social Security as a supplement to your own savings, rather than your primary source of retirement income, is the right way to approach planning.

**6. Healthcare costs in retirement are substantial and often underestimated**

Healthcare is one of the biggest and most frequently overlooked expenses in retirement planning. Fidelity estimates that the average couple retiring today will need approximately $315,000 to cover healthcare costs throughout retirement, and that number does not even include long-term care. Factoring healthcare costs explicitly into your retirement savings goals is absolutely essential.

**7. You should adjust your asset allocation as you approach retirement**

When you are young and have decades before retirement, it makes sense to invest more aggressively in stocks for higher growth potential. As you get closer to retirement, gradually shifting more of your portfolio into bonds and other lower-volatility assets helps protect the wealth you have accumulated. Many target-date retirement funds do this automatically, which is one reason they have become so popular among investors who prefer a hands-off approach.

Retirement planning can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into these key principles makes the path forward much clearer. The most important thing is simply to start, and then to keep going consistently over time.
