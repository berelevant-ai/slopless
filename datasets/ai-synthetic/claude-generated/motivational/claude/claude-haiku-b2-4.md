# The Productivity Myth: Why Less is Actually More

I need to be honest about something: we've been sold a lie about productivity, and I think it's time we talked about it. The myth is that more is always better. That productivity means cramming more tasks, more hours, more deliverables into the same amount of time. But what if that's actually the opposite of how productivity really works?

Here's what I've noticed in my own work and in observing people who are actually producing meaningful results: the most productive people aren't the ones with the longest to-do lists. They're the ones who've gotten really selective about what makes the cut.

Think about it this way. You have a finite amount of focus, energy, and time each day. The question isn't "How do I do more?" The question is "Which things deserve my best effort?" When you frame it that way, suddenly productivity becomes about making choices, not about working faster or longer.

This is where intention comes in. Before you add anything to your plate, ask yourself: Does this align with what I'm actually trying to accomplish? Will this get me closer to my biggest goals? Is this something only I can do, or can it be delegated or eliminated? These are the filter questions that separate real productivity from just looking busy.

What's interesting is that companies and individuals who operate this way actually accomplish more. Not more tasks - more meaningful progress. They're willing to say no to good opportunities so they can say yes to great ones. They're not trying to be busy; they're trying to be effective.

The cultural narrative around productivity has created this epidemic of busyness where people feel productive because they're exhausted. They feel like they're making progress because they're working all the time. But most of that activity isn't actually moving the needle on anything that matters.

Real productivity looks like this: you've identified the few things that actually matter for your goals. You've protected your time and focus for those things. You say no to everything else without apologizing. You work with intention rather than just momentum. And because you're not diluted across a hundred different priorities, you actually make progress that's visible and meaningful.

This takes more discipline than just saying yes to everything. It's harder to be selective than to be busy. But it's also how you actually build something worth building.

Your time is your most valuable asset. Spend it accordingly.
