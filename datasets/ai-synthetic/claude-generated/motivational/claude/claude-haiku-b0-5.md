# The Motivation Myth: Why Waiting for Inspiration Is Keeping You Stuck

Here's something that might surprise you: motivation isn't actually what gets things done. We often think that we need to feel motivated before we can take action. We wait for that spark of inspiration, that perfect moment when we feel energized and ready to tackle our goals. And sometimes we wait, and we wait, and nothing happens. Sound familiar?

The truth is that this is backwards. In most cases, action comes first, and motivation follows. This concept might feel counterintuitive, but once you understand it, it can be genuinely life-changing. Instead of waiting for motivation to strike, you can create motivation by actually starting to do the work.

Think about exercise as an example. Most people who go to the gym regularly will tell you that they don't always feel motivated to work out. But they go anyway. And what happens? Usually, about ten or fifteen minutes into their workout, they start to feel more energized. Their mood improves. They feel a sense of accomplishment. That feeling of motivation comes as a result of taking action, not before it.

The same principle applies to creative work, learning, career development, and really anything else worth doing. The resistance you feel before starting is often much stronger than the actual difficulty of the work itself. Once you're in motion, things flow more easily than you expected.

This is why establishing routines and systems is so powerful. When you have a consistent routine around the things that matter to you, you remove the need for motivation entirely. You're not making a decision each day about whether you feel like doing it. You're simply doing what you've decided is important. It becomes automatic. This is incredibly freeing.

There's also something important to recognize about the motivation myth: it gives us permission to do nothing. When we tell ourselves "I'll do it when I feel motivated," we're essentially giving ourselves an out. We're excusing ourselves from action. But motivation is not a prerequisite for doing worthwhile things. Discipline and commitment are.

Now, this doesn't mean you need to push yourself mercilessly or ignore genuine exhaustion. It's about distinguishing between "I don't feel like it" and "I'm genuinely depleted and need rest." Usually, the former is just resistance, and the latter is real. The key is learning to recognize which one you're actually experiencing.

So here's what I'd encourage you to do: stop waiting for motivation. Start making the commitment to take action anyway. Commit to just five minutes if that's all you can manage. Start before you feel ready. Take the messy, imperfect first step. And then notice what happens to your motivation once you're already in motion. You might be surprised at what you're capable of when you stop waiting and start doing.
