# Finding Your Purpose Isn't a Destination - It's a Direction

I think we talk about "finding your purpose" wrong. We talk about it like it's this thing that's out there waiting to be discovered, like you're going to wake up one day and suddenly know exactly what you're meant to do with your life. But I've learned that purpose isn't something you find - it's something you build.

Here's the distinction that changed how I think about this: purpose isn't a fixed destination. It's a direction you move toward. It's something that becomes clearer the more you engage with the world, the more you try things, the more you pay attention to what resonates with you versus what drains you.

I talk to a lot of people who are stuck because they're waiting to be certain before they commit to anything. They want to know they're going the right direction before they start walking. But that's not how it works. Direction becomes clear through movement. Purpose emerges through exploration and experience.

Think about it in practical terms. When you try things - new projects, new roles, new skills, new relationships - you get information. You learn what you enjoy. You discover what you're good at. You start to see patterns in what lights you up versus what feels obligatory. That's how you build a sense of direction.

The people I know who have the clearest sense of purpose aren't the ones who figured it out early and never wavered. They're the ones who've tried a bunch of things, paid attention to what felt meaningful, and gradually refined their direction based on that lived experience.

Here's what I think is actually happening when people say they've "found their purpose": they've accumulated enough experience and self-knowledge that their next steps feel obvious. They're not certain about the entire future - they're just clear about the next direction to walk in. And often, that clarity came from exploring lots of different directions first.

This reframes the whole question. Instead of "What's my purpose?" the better question becomes "What kinds of problems do I want to solve?" or "What do I want to be known for?" or "What would I do even if nobody was paying attention?" These are questions you can start answering right now, through small experiments and careful attention.

The good news is you don't have to have it all figured out. You just have to be willing to try things, notice what resonates, and let your direction emerge from that process.

Your purpose isn't waiting to be found. It's waiting to be built.
