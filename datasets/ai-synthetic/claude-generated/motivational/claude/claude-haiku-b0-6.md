# Comparing Your Progress: How to Stay Focused on Your Own Journey

In today's world, comparison is unavoidable. We're constantly exposed to curated highlights of other people's lives and achievements. Social media, professional networks, and even casual conversations with friends can trigger that familiar feeling of "I'm not doing enough" or "why aren't I as far along as they are?" If you've ever found yourself in this mindset, you're definitely not alone. But here's something important: comparison is one of the biggest motivation killers out there.

The trap of comparison is that it's always unfair, even when you don't realize it. You're comparing your internal experience - your doubts, your struggles, your slow progress - with someone else's external presentation - their highlight reel, their polished achievements, their carefully curated narrative. You're not seeing the full picture. You're not seeing the person working late nights, facing rejection, dealing with self-doubt. You're only seeing the finished product.

What makes this especially damaging is that comparison is almost impossible to win. There will always be someone further ahead than you. There will always be someone who achieved something faster than you did. If you make comparison your metric for success, you've essentially set yourself up for constant disappointment. And constant disappointment is a powerful motivation killer.

The antidote to destructive comparison is what we might call "competitive benchmarking." Instead of comparing yourself to others' outcomes, compare yourself to your own past performance. The only person you should be competing with is who you were yesterday. Did you improve? Did you learn something? Did you show up and do the work? If the answer is yes, then you're winning. That's it. That's the actual measure of success.

This doesn't mean you should never look at what others are doing. Learning from people further along the path is valuable. It can provide inspiration and insight. The key is to look at their work with the intent to understand and improve your own craft, not to judge yourself lacking by comparison.

It's also helpful to recognize that different people have different starting points, different circumstances, different timelines. Someone might have reached a particular milestone faster than you, but that doesn't mean they're better or more capable. It just means they had a different path. And your path is uniquely yours. You get to write it according to your values, your timeline, your definition of success.

Here's the practical thing to do: when you catch yourself comparing, pause. Notice it. Then consciously redirect your attention inward. Ask yourself: Am I moving in the direction I want to go? Am I showing up for the things that matter to me? Am I learning and growing? These are the questions that matter. Because ultimately, the only comparison that counts is the one between who you are today and who you were before. Focus on that. Keep your eyes on your own paper. Your motivation will thank you for it.
