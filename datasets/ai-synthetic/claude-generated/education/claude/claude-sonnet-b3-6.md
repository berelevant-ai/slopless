# 7 Things You Should Know About Homeschooling

Homeschooling has grown dramatically in popularity over the past decade, and even more so in the wake of the global pandemic. More and more families are choosing to take their children's education into their own hands, and the results can be remarkable. If you are curious about homeschooling - whether you are considering it for your own family or simply want to understand it better - here are seven essential things to know.

## 1. Homeschooling Is Legal Across the United States

One of the first questions many people have is whether homeschooling is actually legal. The answer is yes - homeschooling is legal in all 50 states, though the specific regulations vary considerably from state to state. Some states require parents to notify local authorities and submit annual assessments, while others have very minimal oversight. It is important to understand the rules in your specific state before you begin.

## 2. There Is No Single "Right" Way to Homeschool

This is perhaps the most liberating thing to understand about homeschooling: there is no single curriculum, schedule, or approach that everyone uses. Some families follow a structured classical model, others use a more relaxed "unschooling" philosophy where children guide their own learning, and many fall somewhere in between. The flexibility to customize education to your child's individual needs and interests is one of the biggest advantages of homeschooling.

## 3. Socialization Concerns Are Often Overstated

The most common objection to homeschooling is that children will not get enough social interaction. In practice, most homeschooling families are deeply embedded in rich social networks - co-ops, sports teams, arts programs, religious communities, and neighborhood friendships. Many homeschooled children actually have more meaningful social experiences than their traditionally schooled peers, because they interact with people of all ages rather than only with same-age classmates.

## 4. The Time Commitment for Parents Is Real

Homeschooling requires a serious commitment of time and energy from at least one parent. This is not something that can be done casually alongside a full-time career without significant support systems in place. Many homeschooling families make significant financial sacrifices to allow one parent to stay home. Being honest about this reality upfront is essential for making a sustainable long-term choice.

## 5. Curriculum Resources Have Never Been Better

One of the most exciting developments in homeschooling is the explosion of high-quality resources now available. From comprehensive curriculum packages to individual subject programs, from online video courses to interactive apps, the options are genuinely overwhelming in the best possible way. Organizations, co-ops, and online communities also offer tremendous support for new homeschooling families navigating these choices.

## 6. Homeschooled Students Perform Very Well Academically

The research on academic outcomes for homeschooled students is consistently positive. On average, homeschooled students score higher on standardized tests and are well-represented in university admissions. Of course, outcomes vary depending on the quality and consistency of the education provided, but the evidence suggests that homeschooling, when done thoughtfully, can produce excellent academic results.

## 7. College Admissions Is Very Manageable

A common concern is whether homeschooling will close doors to higher education. The reality is that most colleges and universities have well-established processes for evaluating homeschooled applicants, and many actively welcome them. Strong standardized test scores, a well-documented portfolio of coursework, extracurricular involvement, and compelling essays can make a homeschooled student a very attractive candidate.

Homeschooling is not right for every family, but for those who are well-suited to it, it can be an incredibly rewarding and effective approach to education.
