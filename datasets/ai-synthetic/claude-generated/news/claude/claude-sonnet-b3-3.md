# 7 Things You Should Know About the Decline of Local News

Local news is in crisis. Across the United States and many other countries, newspapers have closed, newsrooms have shrunk, and entire communities have been left without reliable local journalism. This is not just a media industry problem - it has real consequences for democracy, civic engagement, and community wellbeing. Here is what you need to understand about this important issue.

**1. Hundreds of local papers have closed in recent years**

The scale of local news decline is staggering. In the United States alone, hundreds of local newspapers have shut down over the past two decades, and thousands of journalism jobs have been lost. Many of the communities left behind are now described as "news deserts," meaning they lack any meaningful source of local reporting.

**2. The internet disrupted the business model**

For most of the twentieth century, local newspapers were sustained by a combination of print advertising and subscription revenue. The rise of the internet fundamentally changed this. Classified advertising, which was once a huge source of revenue, migrated to free platforms like Craigslist. Display advertising shifted to digital platforms like Google and Facebook. Local papers were left scrambling.

**3. Corporate ownership has accelerated the decline**

Many surviving local papers have been acquired by large hedge funds and private equity firms whose primary goal is to extract maximum profit. This has led to dramatic staff reductions and a shift away from the kind of in-depth investigative local journalism that holds local governments and institutions accountable.

**4. The consequences go beyond just less news**

Research has shown that when local news outlets close, voter turnout in local elections tends to fall, government corruption goes unchecked, and communities become more politically polarized. Local journalism serves as a critical connective tissue for communities, and its absence is felt in very tangible ways.

**5. Nonprofit models are emerging as a potential solution**

One of the most encouraging developments in this space is the growth of nonprofit local news organizations. Outlets funded by foundations, individual donors, and reader memberships have found ways to sustain quality local journalism without relying on traditional advertising. Many are thriving in cities and regions across the country.

**6. Public funding for local journalism is a growing conversation**

In some countries, public investment in local journalism is already established. In the United States, there is a growing debate about whether some form of public support for local news could help fill the gap left by commercial outlets. This remains a complex and contested idea, but it is gaining more serious attention.

**7. Readers have a real role to play**

If you value local journalism, supporting it financially is one of the most direct things you can do. Subscribing to your local paper, donating to a nonprofit news outlet, or even just regularly engaging with local reporting sends a signal that this kind of journalism matters and deserves to survive.

The future of local news is not yet determined. But understanding what is at stake is an essential first step toward ensuring that communities have the information they need to thrive.
