# 7 Things You Should Know About Pricing Strategy

Pricing is one of the most powerful levers a business has, and also one of the most underestimated. Get it right and you can grow faster, attract better customers, and protect your margins. Get it wrong and even a great product can struggle to gain traction. Here are seven critical things you should know about pricing strategy.

**1. Price Communicates Value**

Your price is not just a number - it is a signal. Too low, and customers may assume your product is low quality. Too high without clear justification, and you lose people before they even engage. Pricing at the right level shapes how your offering is perceived and positions you within your competitive landscape.

**2. Cost-Plus Pricing Is a Starting Point, Not a Strategy**

Many businesses set prices by adding a margin to their costs. While this ensures profitability, it ignores what customers are actually willing to pay and what competitors are charging. Cost-plus pricing should inform your floor, but your strategy needs to go much further than that.

**3. Value-Based Pricing Is the Gold Standard**

The most effective pricing approach anchors your price to the value your product delivers to the customer, not to what it costs you to produce. If your solution saves a customer ten thousand dollars a year, pricing at two thousand dollars is leaving significant money on the table. Understanding the economic value of your offering is essential.

**4. Pricing Affects Customer Quality**

The price you charge tends to attract a certain type of customer. Lower prices often attract more price-sensitive buyers who churn faster and demand more support. Higher prices tend to attract customers who are more committed, see more value, and are easier to retain. Pricing is effectively a customer selection mechanism.

**5. Discounting Has Serious Long-Term Costs**

It is tempting to offer discounts to close deals faster or win over hesitant buyers. But frequent discounting trains customers to wait for promotions, devalues your offering, and compresses margins in ways that compound over time. When you do discount, do it strategically and with clear conditions.

**6. You Should Test and Iterate on Price**

Many businesses set a price at launch and never revisit it. Pricing should be treated as a dynamic variable that you test, measure, and adjust over time. As your product matures, as you add features, and as your market position strengthens, your pricing should evolve to reflect that.

**7. Pricing Tiers Can Dramatically Increase Revenue**

Offering multiple pricing tiers - for example, a basic, professional, and enterprise plan - allows you to capture more value across different customer segments. The middle tier often becomes the anchor that drives the most conversions, while the premium tier captures high-willingness-to-pay customers who would otherwise be undercharged.

Pricing strategy deserves the same level of attention and rigor as product development or marketing. By thinking carefully about these seven dimensions, you can build a pricing model that supports sustainable growth and reflects the true value you deliver.
