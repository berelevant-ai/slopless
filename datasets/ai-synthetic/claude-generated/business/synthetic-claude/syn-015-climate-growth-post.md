# How We 10x'd Carbon Credit Verification Speed Using Satellite Telemetry

When we launched our carbon verification service in 2023, a single project audit took an average of 14 weeks. Last quarter, our median audit time was 9 days. Here is what changed.

The traditional carbon verification process was designed for a world of on-the-ground auditors, paper documentation, and quarterly reporting cycles. Notably, that world still exists for a lot of the market - and it produces verification outcomes that are slow, expensive, and often questionable in their accuracy. We decided to build something categorically different.

Our hypothesis was simple: if you can observe the same forest or agricultural plot from space, continuously, you should not need to send someone there every year with a clipboard. The challenge was turning that hypothesis into a verification methodology rigorous enough to satisfy international carbon standards.

Importantly, the answer was not just better satellite imagery. It was the combination of satellite telemetry, ground truth calibration sensors, and a machine learning pipeline trained on verified historical data from over 3,000 projects. Those three elements together create a remote verification system that is demonstrably more accurate than traditional audit methods for the specific question of carbon stock measurement.

The speed improvement is the force multiplier, but precision is the actual product. Our verification uncertainty bounds are tighter than traditional methods, which means the credits we verify trade at a premium on the voluntary market. That premium is what makes the unit economics work at scale.

It is worth noting that we did not replace human judgment - we repositioned it. Our verification scientists now spend their time on methodology, edge cases, and quality assurance rather than manual data collection. This paves the way for a verification throughput that can scale with the voluntary carbon market rather than becoming the bottleneck that slows it down.
