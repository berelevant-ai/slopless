# The Agentic Stack: Where the Value Will Accrue in the AI Infrastructure War

The AI infrastructure market is undergoing a rapid and fascinating stratification. Twelve months ago the interesting question was "which LLM will win?" Today that question is mostly settled - or rather, it has revealed itself to be the wrong question. The more interesting and still-open questions are one layer up: which agentic frameworks, orchestration systems, and memory architectures will define how AI actually gets deployed in production?

At our firm, we have spent the last six months with deep conviction that the agentic layer is the most important infrastructure bet of the current cycle. Here is our thesis in detail.

Notably, running a single LLM prompt is a solved problem. The hard problems - and therefore the durable value - are in orchestrating sequences of AI actions across tools, managing state and memory across sessions, ensuring reliability when individual steps fail, and providing the governance and observability that enterprise deployment requires. Importantly, none of these problems are glamorous. They are the engineering equivalent of drainage systems: invisible when working perfectly, catastrophic when not.

The companies that are winning at the agentic layer share two characteristics. First, they are obsessively reliability-focused. Agentic workflows that complete 95% of the time are not production-grade; they are experiments. The companies that understand this and build for 99.9% completion rates in well-defined task domains are the ones that will win enterprise contracts. Second, they are integration-deep rather than integration-wide. A best-of-breed integration with the 20 tools that cover 80% of enterprise workflows is more valuable than surface-level support for 200 tools.

It is worth noting that the agentic infrastructure market is still early enough that no clear winners have emerged. The next 24 months will likely see significant consolidation. The force multiplier for the companies that get there first is the workflow data they accumulate - task completion patterns, failure modes, edge cases - that becomes a durable moat.
