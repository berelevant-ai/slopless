# Built by Developers, for Developers

At Northlight, we have a simple belief: the best developer tools are the ones that disappear. They are so well-designed, so predictable, and so well-integrated into the way engineers already think and work that using them feels like the absence of friction rather than the addition of a new thing to learn.

We build developer infrastructure - specifically, the observability, deployment, and reliability tooling that engineering teams need to ship with confidence at any scale. We have been building this tooling for six years, and we are still learning every day from the engineers who use it.

**Developer empathy is our competitive advantage.** Every person on our product and engineering team has written production code in the last 12 months. That is not a rule - it is a culture. It is what keeps our intuitions sharp and our design decisions grounded in the reality of how software actually gets built. Notably, the best developer tools are not designed by people who have read about developer workflows. They are designed by people who are living them.

**We optimize for the 3 AM incident.** It is easy to design a dashboard that looks impressive in a product demo. It is hard to design a product that empowers an on-call engineer to diagnose and resolve an incident at 3 AM when they are half-awake and everything is on fire. We design for the latter, every time. That means fewer features, better defaults, and ruthless prioritization of the workflows that matter most under pressure.

**Speed compounds.** We believe in moving fast because in developer tooling, every hour of friction has a cost - a delayed deploy, a delayed diagnosis, a delayed decision. But we move fast with careful architecture and strong contracts, not by cutting corners that create fragility. The secret sauce of a durable developer tools business is that every customer who loves your product becomes a distribution channel. Quality is the growth strategy.

It is worth noting that we are a team of 40 people and intend to stay small relative to our ambitions for as long as possible. Small teams move fast and make coherent product decisions. We are not in a hurry to grow for growth's sake.
