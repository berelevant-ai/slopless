# Keynote Abstract: From Reactive to Predictive - How AI Is Rewriting the Rules of Supply Chain Management

For the last twenty years, supply chain excellence was defined by efficiency: lean inventories, optimized routes, and cost-per-unit metrics that drove out every possible dollar of waste. That model served us well - right up until it didn't. The disruptions of the last five years revealed something uncomfortable: we had optimized for a world that no longer exists.

The supply chain of the next decade will not be defined by efficiency alone. It will be defined by resilience, visibility, and the kind of predictive intelligence that lets organizations see disruptions coming before they happen and respond before they cascade. Notably, that is not an aspiration - it is a business requirement. And AI is what makes it achievable at scale.

In this keynote, I will share what we have learned from working with some of the world's largest and most complex supply chains through the transition from reactive to predictive operations. Importantly, the shift is not primarily technological - it is organizational. The technology exists. The limiting factor is the decision-making architecture that surrounds it.

I will cover three ideas that matter for the next 18 months. First, the specific data investments that drive the highest predictive value and how to prioritize them when resources are constrained. Second, how the best logistics organizations are structuring human-AI collaboration to preserve judgment where it matters while automating where speed is the primary advantage. Third, what the supply chain control tower of 2027 actually looks like - and why it is less about dashboards and more about autonomous action within defined guardrails.

It is worth noting that this is not a theoretical session. Every framework I share comes directly from live deployments, with real numbers. The era of supply chain as a cost center is ending. The era of supply chain as a competitive advantage is here.
