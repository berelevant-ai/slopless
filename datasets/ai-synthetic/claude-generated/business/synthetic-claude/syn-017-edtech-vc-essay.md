# The Learning Revolution We Have Been Waiting For Is Actually Happening

Education technology has been "about to transform learning" for as long as I can remember. MOOCs were going to democratize university education. Adaptive platforms were going to personalize learning for every student. Gamification was going to make studying feel like play. And yet, for most students in most schools, the experience of learning in 2020 looked remarkably similar to 1990.

Something is different now. And it is not hype - it is compounding capability.

Notably, the convergence of three distinct forces is creating conditions for genuine transformation for the first time. First, the quality of AI tutoring has crossed a threshold where sustained Socratic dialogue with a model is educationally valuable, not just superficially impressive. Second, real-time assessment has finally separated itself from standardized testing, enabling continuous feedback loops that actually change how students study. Third - and this is the force multiplier that most market observers are underrating - AI tools are rapidly expanding the supply of high-quality instructional content in ways that were economically impossible even three years ago.

Importantly, the startups we are most excited about are not building AI tutors or AI content platforms. They are building learning infrastructure - the data layers, assessment frameworks, and adaptive pathway engines that power whatever the next generation of learning products turns out to be. Infrastructure bets are harder to evaluate but create more durable competitive positions, and in a market that has seen many consumer-layer companies flame out, we have conviction in the infrastructure layer.

It is worth noting that the international opportunity here is staggering and underappreciated. The US is a large and well-funded market for edtech, but the real scale is in markets where quality educational infrastructure is genuinely scarce. The companies that crack those markets will build something remarkable.

We are actively deploying into this space and looking for founders who understand both the technology and the deeply human dynamics of how learning actually works.
