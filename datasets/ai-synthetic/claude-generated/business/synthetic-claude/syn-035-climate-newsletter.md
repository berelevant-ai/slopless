# Canopy Letter: The Industrial Decarbonization Decade Begins

Welcome back to Canopy Letter.

If you follow climate tech closely, you are probably aware of a growing frustration in the space: the easy wins are behind us. Solar and wind have achieved cost parity. EV adoption is mainstream. The remaining emissions - heavy industry, shipping, aviation, agriculture, cement, steel - are technically harder, more capital-intensive, and slower to decarbonize than the sectors we have already addressed. Welcome to the hard part.

Notably, this is actually where it gets interesting. The easy decarbonization was driven primarily by technology cost curves. Panels got cheaper. Batteries got cheaper. The economics did the work without requiring policy heroics or behavioral change at scale. The hard decarbonization is different. It requires technology innovation in areas like green hydrogen, direct air capture, sustainable fuels, and industrial process heat where the cost curves are still steep. Importantly, it also requires new financing structures, new market mechanisms, and new industrial partnerships that do not fit neatly into the playbooks we used for the energy transition.

The force multiplier in this phase is industrial anchor customers - the large manufacturers, shipping companies, and utilities that can commit to long-term offtake agreements, provide the demand certainty that de-risks capital formation, and serve as proving grounds for technologies that need to demonstrate at scale before they can compete on cost. We are seeing the early emergence of exactly these partnerships, and they pave the way for a decade of industrial transformation that would have seemed implausible five years ago.

It is worth noting that the investment opportunity is proportionally large. Industrial decarbonization is a many-trillion-dollar market transformation over the next 20 years. The companies that establish durable positions in the enabling technology and infrastructure layer over the next five years will compound extraordinary value.

Inside this issue: our analysis of the green hydrogen market inflection, a deep dive on sustainable aviation fuel supply chains, and a conversation with the head of sustainability at a major global manufacturer about what they are actually buying and why.
