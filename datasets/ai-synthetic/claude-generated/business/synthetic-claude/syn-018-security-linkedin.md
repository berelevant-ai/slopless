# The CISO Role Is Being Reinvented. Most CISOs Don't Know It Yet.

Three years ago a CISO's job was to say no. No to the shadow IT. No to the unsanctioned SaaS. No to the developer who wanted to bypass the proxy. Security was a gate, and the CISO was the gatekeeper.

That model is not just outdated. It is actively dangerous in 2026.

Here is the uncomfortable truth that I share with every CISO I mentor: if your security function is primarily defined by what it prevents rather than what it enables, you have already lost the battle for relevance - and probably for security outcomes too. The most sophisticated attacks today are not stopped by no. They are stopped by deep visibility, rapid response capability, and a security culture that is embedded in the engineering workflow rather than bolted on at the end.

Notably, the CISOs who are winning right now have repositioned their function from compliance sheriff to strategic enabler. They are in the room when architecture decisions get made. They are building security tooling that empowers developers to ship safely, fast - not tooling that slows them down. They are using AI to handle the scale of threat detection that no human team could manage alone. Importantly, they are measured on business outcomes, not checkbox compliance.

The secret sauce? Earning trust from the engineering organization. When developers see security as a partner rather than an adversary, the cultural dynamics shift in ways that have more impact on your security posture than any tool you can buy.

It is worth noting that this transition is hard. It requires CISOs to give up a certain kind of control they have historically wielded. But the alternative - a security function that is increasingly bypassed, ignored, and irrelevant as the business moves faster than the process - is far worse.

The best security leaders are force multipliers for the business. Are you?
