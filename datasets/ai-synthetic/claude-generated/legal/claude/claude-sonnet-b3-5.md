# 7 Things You Should Know About Estate Planning

Estate planning is one of those topics that most people know they should deal with but consistently put off. The reality is that having a solid estate plan in place is one of the most important things you can do for yourself and your loved ones. Here is a straightforward breakdown of what you need to know to get started.

## 1. A Will Is Just the Beginning

Many people think that having a will means their estate planning is complete. In reality, a will is only one piece of a comprehensive plan. Depending on your situation, you may also need trusts, powers of attorney, healthcare directives, and beneficiary designations. Each of these tools serves a different purpose and they all work together.

## 2. Beneficiary Designations Override Your Will

Here is something that genuinely surprises people: assets like retirement accounts, life insurance policies, and certain bank accounts pass directly to the named beneficiary, regardless of what your will says. If you have an outdated beneficiary designation - like an ex-spouse listed on a 401(k) - that person will receive the asset even if your will says otherwise. Reviewing and updating beneficiary designations regularly is absolutely critical.

## 3. Dying Without a Will Means the State Decides

If you pass away without a valid will - a situation called dying "intestate" - your state's default laws determine how your assets are distributed. These rules may not align with your wishes at all. For example, a long-term partner who you are not married to may receive nothing under intestate succession laws. Having a will ensures your intentions are honored.

## 4. Trusts Are Not Just for the Wealthy

A common misconception is that trusts are only for the ultra-rich. In reality, trusts are useful for people at many different asset levels. A revocable living trust, for instance, can help your estate avoid probate - the often slow and expensive court process for distributing assets - and can provide significant privacy benefits since trusts are not public record.

## 5. Powers of Attorney Are Essential While You Are Alive

Estate planning is not only about what happens after you die. A durable power of attorney designates someone to make financial decisions on your behalf if you become incapacitated. A healthcare proxy or medical power of attorney does the same for medical decisions. Without these documents, your family may need to go to court to get legal authority to act on your behalf.

## 6. Estate Plans Need Regular Updates

An estate plan is not a one-and-done exercise. Major life events - marriage, divorce, having children, significant changes in assets, or the death of a beneficiary - should all trigger a review of your plan. Many people create an estate plan and then never look at it again, which can cause serious problems down the line.

## 7. Estate Taxes May or May Not Apply to You

Federal estate taxes only apply to estates above a very high threshold (currently over $13 million for individuals), so most people do not need to worry about them. However, some states have their own estate or inheritance taxes with much lower thresholds. Understanding whether state-level taxes apply in your situation can meaningfully affect your planning strategy.

Taking the time to put a solid estate plan in place is one of the most meaningful things you can do for the people you care about. It does not have to be complicated, and getting started is easier than most people think.
