# 7 Things You Should Know About Tenant Rights

Understanding your rights as a tenant is incredibly important, and yet so many renters go through their entire lease without knowing the basic protections available to them. Whether you are renting for the first time or you have been a tenant for years, this guide will walk you through the essential things you need to know.

## 1. You Have the Right to a Habitable Home

One of the most fundamental tenant rights is the right to a livable space. This means your landlord is legally required to maintain heating, plumbing, and structural integrity. If your unit has serious problems like mold, broken heat, or pest infestations, your landlord must address them in a reasonable timeframe.

## 2. Security Deposits Are Regulated

Most states have strict rules about how much a landlord can charge for a security deposit and how quickly it must be returned after you move out. Typically, landlords have 14 to 30 days to return your deposit, along with an itemized list of any deductions. Knowing these timelines can make a real difference when it comes to getting your money back.

## 3. Landlords Cannot Enter Without Notice

In virtually every jurisdiction, landlords are required to give advance notice before entering your unit - usually 24 to 48 hours. There are limited exceptions for genuine emergencies, but routine inspections and repairs require proper notification. This protects your privacy and gives you time to prepare.

## 4. You Are Protected Against Retaliation

If you report a housing code violation or exercise any legal right, your landlord cannot legally retaliate against you by raising your rent, cutting services, or attempting to evict you. Retaliation is illegal in most states and can give you grounds for legal action.

## 5. Eviction Has a Formal Process

Landlords cannot simply remove your belongings or change the locks. Eviction requires a formal legal process that includes written notice, a court hearing, and a judgment. If your landlord tries to remove you without going through this process, you may have a strong legal claim against them.

## 6. Fair Housing Laws Apply to You

Federal law prohibits landlords from discriminating based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability. Many states add additional protected categories. If you believe you have been discriminated against in housing, you have the right to file a complaint.

## 7. You Can Negotiate Your Lease Terms

Many tenants do not realize that a lease is a negotiable document before you sign it. Things like pet policies, subletting rights, and renewal terms can often be discussed and modified. Taking the time to read and potentially negotiate your lease can save you significant trouble down the road.

Understanding these rights is the first step toward being a confident and empowered tenant. When in doubt, it is always a good idea to consult with a local tenant advocacy organization or attorney.
