## 000037-152

Students selected for extra tuition because of one unusually low test result will often score higher on a second test. An unusually low result can combine a student's usual performance with a bad day. On the next test, that temporary disadvantage may be absent, so the result can rise even if the student's underlying proficiency has not changed.

The timing of the tests matters for other reasons too. If the second assessment covers material taught during the intervening month, ordinary classroom instruction could contribute to an increase. If it reuses the same questions, remembering particular answers could help. A report should describe both the interval and whether the forms were reused.

A later improvement can reflect the disappearance of the temporary factors that helped produce the low initial score, rather than any increase in underlying proficiency. This tendency is called regression toward the mean.

To estimate the tuition programme's contribution, eligible students could be allocated by lottery to an immediate start or a delayed start. Both groups would take the same assessment at the same time. The comparison would then address a difference in improvement between groups facing the same calendar and testing conditions. Attendance should also be recorded, since an offer of tuition and actual attendance answer different evaluation questions.

The assessment report needs to give the selection rule, the number of eligible students, and the number with results at both time points. It should describe any departures from the lottery and explain how missing follow-up results were handled. The initial result may have been depressed by an unusually bad day, and the following result can improve simply because that temporary influence is gone. A table of group sizes beside the estimated difference will help readers judge the scale of the comparison.

## 000037-114

An optical zoom changes the image formed by the lens, while a digital zoom enlarges a smaller part of the image the camera has captured. Enlarging that cropped area can make individual detail look softer. The two controls may share one slider on a compact camera, which makes the transition easy to miss.

Look for a marker on the zoom display. On this camera the white part represents the lens range and the blue part begins the digital enlargement. You can turn the blue range off in the menu. That leaves the full lens range available while preventing the slider from continuing into the crop.

Holding the camera steady matters at either setting. A longer focal length magnifies movement as well as the subject, so support the camera when trying to read small lettering in a photograph. Stabilisation can help with movement of the camera; a person walking across the frame can still blur during a long exposure.

The digital part of the range takes a portion of the captured picture and expands it. Optical zoom instead changes the picture projected through the lens, so cropping and enlargement account for the softer detail associated with digital zoom. The menu also lets you choose whether the camera saves a date stamp in the image itself. Leave that off if you may later want an unmarked print.

To compare results, take two pictures of the same stationary subject in good light. Inspect the saved files rather than just the rear screen, whose small size can hide differences. Keep the original files so the comparison is not affected by a messaging service resizing one image.

When ordering prints, check the lab's suggested resolution for the size you want. A file that looks acceptable in a small album may reveal the loss of detail in a much larger print.

## 000037-163

# Following the divisibility argument

An even integer can be written as twice an integer. To show that the sum of two even integers is even, write the first as 2a and the second as 2b. Their sum is 2a + 2b, which factors as 2(a + b). Since a + b is an integer, the sum has the required form.

The letters stand for arbitrary integers. Choosing a = 3 and b = 5 gives a numerical illustration, but the proof works for any permitted choices. This is the difference between checking a particular sum and showing why every sum in the stated class has the property.

Students often pause at the final step because the factorization appears to settle everything. The definition requires twice an integer, however, so the status of the expression inside the brackets matters. The closure of integers under addition supplies that link. In a first proof course, saying it explicitly makes the dependence on the number system visible.

Before trying the next question, trace the completed argument in this order:

- Replace each even integer using the definition.
- Factor the common 2 out of the sum.
- Check that the expression left inside the brackets is an integer.
- Return to the definition of even.

Now attempt the same sequence for two odd integers, written as 2a + 1 and 2b + 1. The extra constants alter the bracket after factorization. Write that bracket out before deciding the parity of the result. Submit both the symbolic calculation and the sentence connecting its final form to the definition; together they show where the conclusion comes from.

## 000037-015

Voluntary reading diaries can provide detailed evidence about how students use their time, but the group returning them may differ from the group invited to take part. Students who already read regularly may be more willing to record their reading. If they are more likely to return a diary, the average calculated from returned diaries will overstate reading time across the invited group.

Consider a study in which a department invites every first-year student to keep a diary for one week. Each entry records the start and end of a reading session, the assigned text, and whether the student was reading alone. This format can show the distribution of sessions across the week, including short periods that a single end-of-week question might miss. It also asks participants to do more work than a brief survey.

The researcher needs a consistent rule for interrupted sessions. A student who answers messages for ten minutes during a half-hour session may record either twenty or thirty minutes unless the instructions specify what counts. Providing an example before the diary period helps standardize this decision. A reminder halfway through the week can reduce entries completed entirely from memory at the end.

Submission rates should be reported by groups for which invitation counts are known, such as course or study mode. Differences can identify parts of the invited population that are poorly represented. Small groups need care: a large percentage difference may involve only a few students, and combining categories may conceal the difference the researcher wants to examine.

The estimate from the diaries can be too high because regular readers may be more inclined to take part and send their records back. Their greater representation among respondents would make the returned diaries describe more reading than occurs, on average, among all the students who were invited.

A useful follow-up is a short question to a sample of nonrespondents about reading during the same week. That provides evidence about the direction of the participation difference. It should be reported separately from the diary estimate because the shorter question uses a different method and may produce less detailed recall.

## Why a queue can persist beside an empty desk

## 000037-151

# Comparing two admission rates

A department's admission rate divides its offers by its applications. The university rate combines the offers and applications from every department. These rates answer different questions because departments receive different numbers of applications.

Consider a small illustration. Department A offers places to 80 of 100 applicants in one group and 9 of 10 in another. Department B offers places to 1 of 10 in the first group and 20 of 100 in the second. Within each department the second group has the higher admission rate. Across the university, however, the first group receives 81 offers from 110 applications, while the second receives 29 from 110.

The reversal occurs because the groups applied in different proportions to the two departments. Most applicants in the first group selected the department with the higher overall acceptance rate. Combining the departments changes the comparison by giving each group's departmental rates different weights. The university totals remain arithmetically correct.

For an admissions inquiry, the next question is substantive. Did applicants have similar preparation? Were they steered towards particular departments? Did a department change its available places? Department-level comparisons remove one source of aggregation, but those questions require additional records.

Use this order when presenting the figures:

- Show offers and applications together for each department and group.
- Compare rates within departments.
- Show how applications are distributed across departments before giving the combined rates.

This sequence lets readers see both the local comparisons and the weights behind the university total. A university-wide rate describes the outcome of the whole application process; a departmental rate describes outcomes conditional on applying there. Choosing between them depends on which part of that process the inquiry addresses.

## 000037-099

# What the two parcel weights mean

A courier can price a parcel using the space it occupies rather than its weight on your scales. A box of cushions takes up room in the van even though it is light. The booking form calls the space-based figure volumetric weight and compares it with the actual weight. The larger figure becomes the chargeable weight.

For this service, the form calculates volumetric weight from the external length, width, and height. Measure the closed box, including any bulge. The dimensions printed on a packaging supplier's invoice may describe the inside of the box, so transferring those numbers can give a different result from measuring the finished parcel.

Suppose your packed lamp weighs three kilograms and its dimensions produce a volumetric weight of five kilograms. You would pay at the five-kilogram rate. Putting it in a smaller box could change that comparison, provided the lamp still has enough protective packing. Removing padding just to reach a cheaper band can leave the item exposed to damage.

The reverse case matters when sending books. A compact box might produce a volumetric weight of two kilograms while weighing seven kilograms on the scales. The actual seven kilograms then determines the rate. A smaller outer box would not reduce that charge unless its removal also reduced the actual packed weight enough to enter another band.

Before paying, compare the booking confirmation with the parcel itself. Check the destination, the measured dimensions, and the packed weight. If you alter the box after booking, update the booking before printing the label. Keeping a photograph of the sealed parcel beside a tape measure can also help you answer a later measurement query without having to reconstruct the packaging from memory.

## 000037-157

## Choosing a measure of typical rent

For a description of what a household near the middle of the local rental market pays, the median is the more useful starting point. It places half the observed rents on either side of a central position. The mean instead divides total rent by the number of properties.

Consider five monthly rents: 600, 650, 700, 750, and 2,300. The median is 700, while the mean is 1,000. The expensive property affects the mean strongly because its full value enters the total. The median depends on its position above the middle rather than how far above the middle it lies.

That feature also limits the median. If the most expensive property rises from 2,300 to 3,300, the median stays at 700 although aggregate rent increases. A housing office estimating total rent payments would lose relevant information by looking only at the median. For that purpose, the mean multiplied by the property count reproduces the total.

Both measures also depend on the sample. A list of newly advertised properties describes asking rents for available homes, while existing tenants may be paying different amounts. Using the median cannot repair a mismatch between the population sampled and the population named in the report.

For the neighbourhood profile, report the median to describe the household near the centre, and give the source and observation month beside it. For a budget estimating all rent payments, use the total or its equivalent mean-and-count calculation. This is why the median remains the better headline for the proposed profile: the profile asks about a central household, whereas the alternative measure serves the distinct total-payment question.

## 000037-155

# What 'some' contributes

In ordinary conversation, saying that some students passed often suggests that others failed. In a simple logical analysis, however, 'some students passed' requires at least one student to have passed and is compatible with everyone passing. The suggested limit and the minimum asserted content need to be distinguished.

Imagine a tutor who knows all the results and is asked whether the whole class passed. If she replies, 'Some students passed,' a listener may reason that she would have given the stronger answer if she could. The impression that some failed comes partly from the question and the tutor's apparent knowledge.

Change the situation. A visiting lecturer has marked only five papers from a class of thirty and says, 'Some students passed; I haven't seen the remaining papers.' The second clause makes the limit on her knowledge explicit. Her first clause now offers the result she can support while leaving the unmarked papers open.

A useful classroom exercise is to compare that sentence with 'Some students passed, in fact everyone did.' The continuation may sound like a correction of an expectation, but it need not contradict the first clause's minimum claim. Students should identify which expectation changes and which assertion remains satisfied.

## Testing the interpretation

Work in pairs with one person supplying the question and the other reading the answer. On the first round, ask whether anyone passed. On the second, ask whether everyone passed. Keep the answer itself unchanged and write down whether it feels sufficient in each exchange. A difference in how complete the answer feels gives us evidence about the role of the question, even before we decide how to describe the inference formally.

Then add a brief account of the speaker's access to the marks. One version can make the speaker the examination officer; another can make the speaker a student who has spoken with three friends. Compare the interpretations again. Record the exact dialogue rather than only your final judgment, since another pair needs the context to assess your reasoning.

For analysis, write down three things separately: the words used, the question being answered, and what the speaker is presented as knowing. Then test a continuation that withdraws the suggested limit. This helps locate the source of the interpretation. In a report of examination outcomes, an exact count usually avoids the ambiguity altogether; in a dialogue exercise, keeping the same word across changed contexts lets us examine how the inference is produced.

## 000037-115

A microwave's defrost programme gives frozen food time between bursts of heating. Microwaves heat different parts unevenly, and the pauses allow some heat to spread. The food can still have cold areas, so the programme's prompts to turn or separate pieces matter.

For a frozen block of bread slices, separation changes the problem. Once the outer slices loosen, remove them and continue with the part that remains frozen, following the programme instructions. Leaving every loosened slice beside the block can warm the outside much more than necessary while the centre is still solid.

A meal in a container behaves differently. The container's shape affects the depth of the food, and the dish may need stirring rather than separating. Use cookware suitable for the appliance and follow the food's defrosting and cooking instructions. A defrost setting is a preparation stage, so any specified cooking step still needs to follow it.

The prompts return during longer programmes because the condition of the food changes. Turning a solid block early redistributes exposure. The repeated prompt therefore accompanies a new opportunity to change the food's position.

For future portions, consider freezing food in smaller, flatter packs when the recipe and storage guidance allow it. A pack that suits the container you will use later is easier to turn or stir than a large irregular block. Label it with the contents and freezing date before putting it away. That helps you choose the correct instructions at the next meal and avoids having to identify an unlabelled portion after it has begun to soften.

## 000037-154

A stock is measured at a point in time; a flow is measured over an interval. The number of books held by a library on 31 December is a stock. The number acquired during the year is a flow. Both can be expressed in books, which makes their different time references easy to overlook.

Suppose the library begins January with 12,000 books, acquires 900, and withdraws 400. Its closing stock is 12,500 if those are the only changes. The acquisition flow describes incoming books; the net change in stock also takes withdrawals into account. A statement that the collection grew by 900 would confuse the incoming flow with that net change.

Now consider loans. A book can be borrowed several times while remaining part of the collection. Annual loans may exceed the number of books held without creating an accounting inconsistency. The loan count measures transactions, while the collection count measures items. Counting renewals as additional loans would change the transaction measure again, so the report needs a consistent renewal rule.

The same distinction affects staffing. The number of employees on the final payroll date is a stock. The number hired during the year is a flow. A year with many hires can still end with fewer employees if departures are greater. To compare workload, neither figure substitutes directly for hours worked.

When checking a report, attach a date or an interval to each count, then identify what enters and leaves the measured population. For collections, reconcile opening books, acquisitions, withdrawals, and closing books. For borrowing activity, inspect the transaction definition instead. This separates two different checks that the shared word 'books' can otherwise conceal.

## 000037-112

# Why the umbrella still feels wet inside

A wet umbrella folded tightly can transfer rainwater from its outer surface to the fabric inside. The sleeve then holds damp layers together and slows drying. Leaving it packed overnight can produce a musty smell even when no rain reached you beneath the open canopy.

For an ordinary drying routine, open it where drips will not create a slippery floor. Follow any manufacturer's guidance about leaving the mechanism under tension. A stand that keeps the tips upright may drain differently from hanging the umbrella by its handle, so check beneath it before placing it over a wooden floor.

A genuinely leaking canopy has a different sign: water reaches the underside while the umbrella is open in rain. Look for a torn seam or a small hole against a light background once the fabric is dry. A wet inner surface discovered only after unpacking does not distinguish a leak from water transferred during storage.

If you pack it wet for a train journey, take it out again when you reach your destination. This is the point at which the storage explanation becomes a practical decision. You can prevent several more hours of damp contact by removing the sleeve, even if there is no convenient place to open the whole canopy immediately. Let the sleeve dry separately too.

Before putting the umbrella away for a season, inspect the ribs and the fabric around their ends. A bent rib can rub the same spot every time the canopy moves. Photograph any damage before asking a repairer about it. Store the umbrella dry and loosely enough that the handle and frame are not pressing into the fabric; that addresses a different source of damage from the moisture trapped after a journey.

## 000037-017

A chair with sound joints and a worn painted finish can often be refreshed without stripping it to bare wood. Start by checking that the existing coating is suitable for preparation at home; if its age or composition is uncertain, get that assessed before sanding. This guide assumes a modern coating that is firmly attached.

Wash the chair with a cleaner suitable for the existing finish, rinse as directed, and let it dry. Grease around the top rail and arms can interfere with the new coating even when the surface looks clean. Remove the seat pad if it is held by accessible screws, keeping the screws together in a small container.

Lightly sand the glossy areas until they look dull. The aim is to give the primer a surface it can grip, rather than remove all the old paint. A smooth, glossy coating gives the next coat less purchase, so dulling it helps the new finish stay attached.

Work around curved spindles with a flexible sanding pad rather than forcing a flat block against them. Raised edges lose their coating quickly, so use lighter pressure there. After sanding, remove the dust using the method specified by the coating manufacturer. Check the underside of the rails as well as the surfaces visible from above.

Apply a primer compatible with both the old finish and your chosen paint. A small brush is easier to control around joints; on broad surfaces, finish each area with light strokes in the same direction. Keep an eye on the underside of rails, where excess primer can gather into drips. Let it dry for the time stated on the container before applying the topcoat.

Use thin coats of paint and check them in good light. Turning the chair between coats makes missed edges easier to see. The surface may feel dry before it is ready for another coat or for ordinary use, so follow the separate recoat and curing times. Refit the seat only when handling it will not mark the finish.

The preparation works because reducing the gloss gives the primer something to hold onto. There is no need to take away every layer of sound paint; making the slippery, shiny surface dull improves the grip of the coating that goes over it.

## 000037-103

# Why a bathroom mirror fogs

A mirror fogs when moist room air meets glass cold enough for some of that moisture to become liquid on its surface. The tiny droplets scatter light, giving the glass its cloudy appearance. Heating the glass or reducing the moisture reaching it changes the conditions that produce the droplets.

An extractor fan removes humid air from the bathroom. Its performance depends partly on replacement air reaching the room, so check the installation guidance before sealing the gap beneath the door. A fan with a run-on timer continues after the light goes out. The timer setting is usually separate from any humidity control.

Heated mirror pads work on the glass rather than the room air. Their clear area can be smaller than the full mirror, especially where the heating pad covers only the centre. Check the pad dimensions as well as the advertised mirror dimensions if seeing the whole surface matters to you.

If two people use the mirror together, a narrow clear area may put one person outside it. A rectangular pad running up the centre can suit someone standing close for shaving but be inconvenient when checking hair from farther back. Ask the showroom to identify the heated area on the model you are considering, rather than judging it from a photograph taken when the whole display is dry.

The cloudy coating forms because water vapour in the room turns into small liquid drops on the cooler mirror. Those drops spread the reflected light, which is why the reflection looks misty. A microfibre cloth can remove the water already present, although a damp cloth may leave streaks that become noticeable once the glass clears.

A surface treatment is another purchase to compare. Read whether it is intended for glass with an existing coating and how often it needs renewal. Some products require the surface to be completely dry before application, which affects when you can apply them in a shared bathroom.

Whatever product you choose, the basic cause is moisture becoming droplets on colder glass. The droplets interrupt the clear reflection. For cleaning costs, include replacement cloths or repeat applications rather than comparing only the first bottle with the price of a permanent fitting.

# Reading a metered water bill

## 000037-206

# When an electricity tariff changes

An electricity bill usually separates the unit rate from the standing charge. The unit rate applies to each kilowatt-hour used; the standing charge applies to each day covered by the bill. A change in either can alter the total even if your household uses the same amount of electricity.

When a tariff changes halfway through a billing period, look for two sets of dates. The supplier may use a meter reading on the change date to divide consumption between the old and new prices. Without a reading, it may estimate that division and mark the relevant figures accordingly.

Check the total number of days across both parts. Those dates should cover the billing period without a gap or an extra day. A tariff beginning on 16 May should not also be charged for 15 May, and the old tariff should finish before the new one begins.

For the consumption calculation, multiply each part's kilowatt-hours by its own unit rate. For the fixed part, multiply the days in each part by that part's standing charge. This is why the two prices introduced at the start of the bill must be checked separately: one follows the split in consumption and the other follows the split in days. Add the results before comparing them with the charges shown.

If the supplier revises an estimate, compare both the reading and the price allocation on the replacement bill. The overall volume may be unchanged while more of it moves into the higher-priced period. Keep the earlier statement until you understand which figures changed and why the revised total follows from them.

# Weighting the town travel survey

A survey weight describes how much a respondent contributes to an estimate. If younger residents respond less often than older residents, giving each response the same weight can leave younger residents underrepresented. We can increase the contribution of their responses so the age distribution of the weighted sample matches the town's population figures.

The adjustment requires reliable population counts and compatible age groups. A survey category ending at age twenty-four cannot be matched directly to a population table ending at twenty-nine. The analyst should document how the groups were reconciled and retain the original response counts beside the adjusted totals.

Weights can also be large when a small group supplies very few responses. That gives each of those respondents substantial influence over the estimate. The report should show the range of weights and explain any trimming, including which population totals were allowed to differ after trimming.

The spread of weights affects uncertainty as well as the estimate itself. A few heavily weighted responses can make a result less stable than the raw sample size suggests. Weighting lets a response count more or less towards the result; increasing the contribution from an underrepresented age group brings its weighted share into line with the population. The uncertainty calculation should use the survey design and supplied weights rather than treating the adjusted records as independent extra observations.

Matching age totals also leaves other response differences to investigate. Within the same age group, commuters who drive might be more willing to answer a travel survey than people who walk. Compare the weighted results with available independent measures and explain which characteristics were used for adjustment, so readers can judge the remaining limitations.

# Comparing response rates across wards

The response rate is the number of usable questionnaires divided by the number of eligible households invited. Record both counts beside the rate. Two wards can have the same percentage while supplying very different numbers of responses, which affects the precision available for later comparisons.

Eligibility needs a rule fixed before the counts are calculated. A letter returned because a property is empty can be excluded if the survey concerns occupied households.
