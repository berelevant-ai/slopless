Step 1: Audit Your Current Business English Level (Week 1) You cannot fix what you have not measured. Spend 30 minutes on a free online Business English assessment. Try the Cambridge Business English test or the EF SET quiz. Write down your score. Be honest. Most people in Asia overestimate their writing skills and underestimate their speaking gaps. Identify your 3 weakest areas from this list: email writing, presentations, meetings, negotiations, or small talk with colleagues. Step 2: Build Your Core Vocabulary System (Weeks 1 to 3) Focus on 500 High-Value Business Words Forget general English. You need business-specific language. Learn words like "stakeholder," "deliverable," "escalate," "bandwidth," and "align." Use Anki flashcard app. Study 20 new words per day. Review old cards every morning. This takes 15 minutes daily. After 3 weeks, you will have 420 words actively in your memory. That is enough to handle 80% of business conversations. Use Real Business Materials Only Stop using textbooks from 2015. Read the Harvard Business Review app daily. Listen to the "HBR IdeaCast" podcast during your commute. Watch TED Talks with subtitles. Every piece of content must be business-focused. No entertainment content counts for this phase. Step 3: Master Professional Email Writing in 14 Days (Weeks 2 to 4) Email is where most Asian professionals lose credibility without realizing it. A vague subject line, an overly casual tone, or a wall of text can kill your professional image instantly. Follow this system every day for 14 days. Use the BLUF method: Bottom Line Up Front. State your main point in the first sentence. Keep every email under 150 words unless absolutely necessary. Use bullet points for any list of 3 or more items. Never start with "I hope this email finds you well." It adds zero value. End with one clear action item. Tell the reader exactly what you need from them. Write 3 practice emails every day. Use ChatGPT or Grammarly to check your tone. Ask it: "Does this sound professional and direct?" Rewrite until it does. Step 4: Speak Up in Meetings Using the 3-Part Formula (Weeks 3 to 5) The Point-Reason-Example Structure Every time you speak in a meeting, use this formula. State your point first. Give one reason. Then give one example. Practice this out loud every morning for 10 minutes. Record yourself on your phone. Listen back. It feels painful at first. That discomfort means you are growing. After 3 weeks of daily practice, your meeting contributions will sound structured and confident. Join a Speaking Practice Group Find a Toastmasters chapter in your city. There are active chapters in Bangalore, Manila, Jakarta, and Ho Chi Minh City. Attend twice per month minimum. Alternatively, join a free English conversation group on Meetup or Discord. Speaking with other non-native professionals removes the fear faster than any app can. Step 5: Learn Negotiation and Persuasion Language (Weeks 4 to 6) This is the skill that directly converts to salary increases. You need phrases that sound assertive without being aggressive. Practice these exact phrases until they feel natural. "Based on the data, I would recommend we consider..." "I understand your concern. Here is what I propose..." "Can we align on the key priorities before moving forward?" "I would like to revisit this point after we review the numbers." "What would need to be true for you to support this approach?" Role-play these with a study partner twice per week. Use actual work scenarios you are currently facing. Real practice beats fake exercises every time.
