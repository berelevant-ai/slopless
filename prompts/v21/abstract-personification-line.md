# Abstract Personification Line

Read the complete passage, including connected paragraphs. Resolve the referent and the abstraction's role from context rather than a noun's usual meaning. Cover unfamiliar wording, inflections and positions, including passive or static spatial relationships.

First distinguish adopted narration or dialogue from wording handled as evidence and literal entities established by the events. A phrase's identity or form can be the evidence. Quotation marks and genre alone decide neither status.

Then identify what the abstraction explains about a particular behavior, thought, choice or perception. Preserve an intelligible motive, constraint or consequence, including retrospective explanations. Ordinary wording could express the same relation without making its figurative version defective.

Observation, orientation, characterization, compression and pacing can themselves supply a particular function; they need no later action. Distinguish that contribution from merely naming an emotion with an actor or spatial role. A concrete occasion for a feeling or useful activity nearby does not by itself make that role informative.

Assess each candidate separately. One interchangeable payoff is sufficient even beside meaningful writing. Another check's applicability supplies no exemption.

## Development Examples

### Hit

Nearly midnight: Daniel watches the woman at the coffee stand pack unsold sandwiches into a crate, then sees his daughter's flight number appear on the arrivals board. He buys two sandwiches, with **hope settling into a small corner of the terminal**, and waits beside the doors for the yellow coat he mailed her in September.

### Hit

My sister wanted the drill because she had a house. I wanted the socket set, including the pieces I knew Dad had lost. We laid everything on the garage floor and discussed batteries, storage and which attachments fitted which machine, while **resentment ran the meeting beneath our reasonable voices**. By four we had divided even the loose screws.

### Hit

We skipped the drinks, which left enough for two meals from the place with the paper lanterns. For **an hour over which worry had no jurisdiction**, we ate beside the open window and watched people coming home from work. In the morning I found the rice cartons still on the sill.

### No Hit

He said, 'My pride won't let me wear the hearing aid at bowls because they'll think I'm old.' Then he asked me to repeat the fixture date for the third time. I wrote it on his calendar. The following week, he wore the aid beneath his club cap.

### No Hit

The blind woman's description included the words 'silence sat on the stairs.' When I asked what she meant, she explained that the usual hum from the stairlift had stopped. I kept both the phrase and her explanation in the statement. They established when she first noticed the power cut.

### No Hit

We left the bucket by the fire exit again. During rehearsal Regret walked through the closed dressing-room door, took the actor's chair and dragged it beneath the leak. Our ghost had been doing this since the roof failed. I fetched the bucket and put it beneath the drip, then carried the wet chair back to the dressing room.
