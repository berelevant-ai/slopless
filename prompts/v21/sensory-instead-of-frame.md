# Sensory Instead of Frame

Input: passage.

Read the complete connected sequence. Generalize across sensory modalities, wording, inflections and positions, including contrasts between alternatives, changed conditions and remembered conditions.

Judge the inventory or exchange's own contribution beyond the informative observation. The defect is interchangeable contrast, rather than comparison itself or paired sensory nouns. Ordinary sensory nouns, familiarity, figurative language and repeated context alone are insufficient.

An informative change protects its sensory information. Flag that same contrast only when additional independently generic decoration remains; this can occur within the same sentence. Nearby facts and causal wording supply no blanket exemption.

Preserve useful physical, perceptual, social and narrative functions, including observation, orientation, characterization, compression and pacing. Require neither a named sensory source, explicit physical contact nor later action. Merely naming a feeling or event can still leave generic decoration.

Assess adopted narration and ordinary dialogue by function; preserve wording examined or documented as wording. Stock scenic metaphor and other checks may overlap.

## Development Examples

### Hit

The garage waiting room smelled of lemon cleaner rather than the fuel we'd noticed earlier; someone had removed the leaking can and washed the floor. **The afternoon itself seemed to have exchanged the scent of steel and danger for soap and trust.** The mechanic handed back the keys and asked us to check the mileage on his form.

### Hit

On the farm, breakfast was at half past five. **The mornings rang with buckets and birds instead of keyboards and urgency.** I tried to help by laying the table the night before, but the cat slept on the clean plates. My aunt washed them without mentioning it and assigned me the mugs instead.

### Hit

In the guest room, an exercise bicycle served as a clothes rack. **The air held lace and yesterday rather than the chrome and tomorrow of her own flat.** Carol found a clean towel on the saddle and put it beside her bag. A blue power light glowed on the bicycle's console. Her host knocked to ask whether she should unplug it for the night.

The pale board felt waxy rather than silky when I ran my finger across it, though they looked identical from where I stood. He turned it over and showed me the finish written on the back. I chose the other board for the handrail and kept the sample until installation.
