# Sensory Instead of Frame

Input: passage.

Read the complete connected sequence. Generalize across sensory modalities, wording, inflections and positions, including contrasts between alternatives, changed conditions and remembered conditions. Judge each eligible cue independently.

Separate the inventory or exchange's contribution from the scene's other information. Ask what particular perceptual distinction it supplies, and whether the contrast instead packages whole settings or periods into opposing stock sensory bundles. Their ingredients can be physically plausible while the exchange serves only interchangeable atmosphere. Particularity comes from what this perception distinguishes here.

An informative change protects its sensory information. Before flagging that same contrast, identify additional generic decoration beyond the informative observation; it can remain within the same clause. A change of setting or time alone supplies no such distinction. The defect is the interchangeable contrast, rather than comparison itself or paired sensory nouns. Ordinary nouns, familiarity, figurative language and repeated context alone establish no defect.

Preserve useful physical, perceptual, social and narrative functions supported by the passage, including observation, orientation, characterization, compression and pacing. Require neither a named sensory source, explicit physical contact nor later action. Missing sensory grounding can be the defect in an abstract stock inventory. Merely naming a feeling or event can leave generic decoration.

Other defects can coexist with this one. Useful detail protects the meaning it supplies; assess any remaining ornamental exchange independently. Nearby facts and causal wording supply no blanket exemption.

Determine the role of quoted or recorded language before judging its style. When its particular formulation is itself the object of the passage's attention, preserve that wording. Apply the check normally when the narrator or speaker adopts it to describe the scene. Quotation marks or genre alone establish no exemption.

## Development Examples

### Hit

The garage waiting room smelled of lemon cleaner rather than the fuel we'd noticed earlier; someone had removed the leaking can and washed the floor. **The afternoon itself seemed to have exchanged the scent of steel and danger for soap and trust.** The mechanic handed back the keys and asked us to check the mileage on his form.

### Hit

On the farm, breakfast was at half past five. **The mornings rang with buckets and birds instead of keyboards and urgency.** I tried to help by laying the table the night before, but the cat slept on the clean plates. My aunt washed them without mentioning it and assigned me the mugs instead.

### Hit

In the guest room, an exercise bicycle served as a clothes rack. **The air held lace and yesterday rather than the chrome and tomorrow of her own flat.** Carol found a clean towel on the saddle and put it beside her bag. A blue power light glowed on the bicycle's console. Her host knocked to ask whether she should unplug it for the night.

### No Hit

"Is this the smoother one?" I asked. The carpenter put both samples in my hands. The pale board felt waxy rather than silky when I ran my finger across it, though they looked identical from where I stood. He turned it over and showed me the finish written on the back. I chose the other board for the handrail and kept the sample until installation.

### No Hit

His "all right" sounded different on the telephone, clipped rather than drawn out. It was the way he spoke when his supervisor was standing beside him. I stopped explaining why I needed the van and asked whether I should call back. "About twenty minutes," he said. When he rang from the car park, he let me finish the whole story before asking what I had broken.

### No Hit

By the fourth day, the apartment smelled of soap instead of my father's cigarettes. His coat had gone to the charity shop; the curtains were hanging wet above the bath. My sister opened every cupboard once more before we returned the keys. In the smallest one, behind the fuse box, she found a packet with two cigarettes left. She put it in her handbag.
