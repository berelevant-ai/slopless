# Summary Fog Transition

Input: passage.

Read the complete connected sequence and generalize across wording, inflections and positions. Assess the summary's contribution rather than demanding an exhaustive account of events.

Preserve identifiable physical, perceptual, social and narrative functions, including observation, orientation, characterization, compression and pacing, without requiring later action. A specific subjective experience or memory gap can itself supply the function.

A cause word, nearby dramatic event or another useful detail does not automatically make the candidate transition informative. Merely naming a feeling or event, redundant annotation and generic dramatic payoffs can remain weak. Establish the function from the supplied writing rather than invented missing story requirements or reader preferences.

Assess adopted narration and ordinary dialogue by function; preserve wording examined or documented as wording. Conventional time-passage cliches may overlap. An unspecified external change without a summary of experience falls outside this check.

## Development Examples

### Hit

"Try it now." The plumber crouched beside the open cupboard while I ran the kitchen tap. The new coupling stayed dry. As he packed away, **the rest of the visit passed in an indistinct rush**, including the card payment and carrying the towels outside. His receipt was still on the porch when I went to lock up.

### Hit

From the end of the brass row, I could finally see the conductor's hand above the music stands. We repeated the difficult ending until every instrument stopped together. **Rehearsal vanished in a rush of sound and movement** before we packed up. The percussionist asked me to hold the stage door while she rolled the timpani into the corridor.

### Hit

The optician tightened my glasses and asked me to read the bottom line again. I could make out every letter except the last. **The rest of the visit became a thin mist of events.** She ordered the lenses and printed a receipt, which I folded into the case.

### No Hit

Those first three days with the baby blur into a conversation conducted in pieces. "Bottle washed?" "Top shelf." "Your mother called." An answer might come an hour after its question, from the other side of a sleeping child. On Friday, he said, "About the visitors," and I put down the towel I was folding.

### No Hit

"The interview was a blur," I told Jane, the careers adviser who had helped me prepare. She asked which questions I remembered. All six came back, along with my answers, but I could recall none of the panel's faces. I had stared at the water jug throughout. Jane opened the employer's staff page and turned her screen toward me. Three portraits sat above the names on my interview invitation.

### No Hit

Snow crossed the security light in a racing montage of white slashes. Each flake existed for an instant inside the beam, then vanished into darkness. I watched from the porch while waiting for the taxi. Behind me, my sister stood with her suitcase closed and her coat still on its hook.
