# Gaze choreography

Input: passage.

Cover visual movements across or between targets, including noun-based eye-camera constructions without a perception verb. Generalize across unseen wording, inflections and positions rather than relying on a verb list.

Judge the movement's own contribution. Eye color, a named target, elaborate movement, a cause word or a generic dramatic payoff alone does not make choreography informative. A useful detail elsewhere protects its own role while a separate weak visual sweep remains eligible.

Preserve sightlines that discover something, establish a physical viewpoint, direct attention to a meaningful clue, communicate consequentially or serve another identifiable narrative function. Observation, orientation, characterization, compression and pacing can supply that function without later action.

Assess adopted narration and ordinary dialogue by function. Preserve wording examined or documented as wording. Ordinary looking or watching may also meet a perception check; another defect does not erase useful observation.

## Development Examples

### Hit

I watched the indicator blink twice without the barrier rising. The attendant followed my glance and pressed the manual release. As I thanked him, **his gaze meandered over the booth's inner walls**. He told me the ticket sensor often failed in wet weather and advised keeping the receipt flat.

### Hit

In winter the dog walker arrived in darkness; in summer she brought grass seeds into the stairwell on her socks. Her price stayed the same. One June she explained the holiday arrangements to me while **looking up the stairs and back down their length**. Two days' notice, she said. I saved her number under the dog's name and used it more often than most of my family's.

### Hit

The ferry was delayed, so we went upstairs for coffee. "We could have driven," Lena said, **her brown eyes roving about the lounge**. I reminded her that taking the ferry had been her idea. She laughed and paid for both coffees. On the television above the counter, a weather presenter pointed at the patch of sea we were waiting to cross.

### No Hit

We were discussing the rota when Denise announced that she was leaving. Her last shift would be Sunday, which meant someone had to take the early deliveries. The manager's eyes made a slow circuit of the people around the table. When he reached me, I nodded, and he put my name beside the deliveries. "Well," he said, "we wish you all the best." Then he asked me to print the revised rota.
