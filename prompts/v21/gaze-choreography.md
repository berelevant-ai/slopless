# Gaze choreography

Input: passage.

Judge the relation established by the movement's direction, target or timing.

Cover eyes, gaze, sightlines and equivalent visual movements expressed through verbs or nouns. One weak movement suffices.

A relevant visual field can establish inspection or search without a stated result. Attention offered or withheld can communicate a particular social relation. Preserve physical viewpoints and meaningful clues; infer their function across the connected passage without requiring later action.

Nearby work, speech or emotion cannot by itself make an unrelated visual path informative. Named targets, eye color and elaborate movement alone supply no function. Useful observation protects its own contribution; judge any independent choreography beside it, including when another defect occurs.

Observation, orientation, characterization, compression and pacing qualify through the particular content supplied. Preserve wording whose form or identity is examined or documented, including evidence about events or people. Assess adopted narration and ordinary dialogue normally.

## Development Examples

### Hit

I watched the indicator blink twice without the barrier rising. The attendant followed my glance and pressed the manual release. As I thanked him, **his gaze meandered over the booth's inner walls**. He told me the ticket sensor often failed in wet weather and advised keeping the receipt flat.

### Hit

In winter the dog walker arrived in darkness; in summer she brought grass seeds into the stairwell on her socks. Her price stayed the same. One June she explained the holiday arrangements to me while **looking up the stairs and back down their length**. Two days' notice, she said. I saved her number under the dog's name and used it more often than most of my family's.

### Hit

The ferry was delayed, so we went upstairs for coffee. "We could have driven," Lena said, **her brown eyes roving about the lounge**. I reminded her that taking the ferry had been her idea. She laughed and paid for both coffees. On the television above the counter, a weather presenter pointed at the patch of sea we were waiting to cross.

### No Hit

We were discussing the rota when Denise announced that she was leaving. Her last shift would be Sunday, which meant someone had to take the early deliveries. The manager's eyes made a slow circuit of the people around the table. When he reached me, I nodded, and he put my name beside the deliveries. "Well," he said, "we wish you all the best." Then he asked me to print the revised rota.

### No Hit

Under the streetlamp, I let my gaze move between the two paint samples. The gray turned green in that light, while the cream stayed cream. This was how customers would see the sign after sunset. I sent the painter a photograph and confirmed the warmer color.

### No Hit

My father's gaze followed the road around the headland until it vanished behind the quarry. He had walked that bend every morning before buses served the village. The quarry had closed years ago, but he could still name the families who had lived in each cottage along the route.
