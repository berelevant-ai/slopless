# Body-Emotion Shorthand

Find stock bodily emotion tags: heartbeat, breath, chest, stomach, throat, skin, limbs, bodily facial reactions and objects felt as an emotional burden. The words can be unfamiliar; judge whether the bodily reaction adds anything beyond generic alarm, relief, excitement or another feeling.

Preserve particular bodily information: a symptom or exertion, a perceived physical condition, a constraint on an action, or a signal that changes an exchange. The reaction itself must supply that information. An action merely following the tag, or useful facts elsewhere, leaves an independent stock tag eligible. An informative bodily observation needs no later practical consequence.

Cause words, numbers, named objects and quotation marks supply no automatic answer. Judge adopted dialogue and narration normally; preserve wording documented or examined as wording. Named emotion moving as a substance and a generic vocal description alone do not establish this bodily pattern.

Identify actual bodily reactions, not every bad emotional description. A feeling flowing through a scene, regret crossing a face, a brittle voice and a generic glance are different checks. A localized physical symptom is informative as a symptom. Follow a bodily reaction through the passage when another person recognizes it or when it changes what someone can see or do. Evaluate separate cues independently when only one has that useful role. An object felt as bodily weight can still be an emotional tag when the weight represents its emotional significance.

## Hits

I opened the payroll sheet to check the overtime totals. **My stomach dropped.** Someone had replaced all six Saturday shifts with unpaid leave, including the weekend we stayed to finish the hospital order. I still had the delivery receipt on my phone, so I sent it to the manager with a request to correct the hours.

You know the number before the screen finishes lighting up. Your mother calls every Sunday after lunch, never at three in the morning. **A chill threads down your spine.** When you answer, it is her neighbor speaking. She has found your mother outside in her nightdress and wants to know where the spare key is kept.

Clara's husband has crossed out the bookcase on their list and written that she can keep it. Such a small concession after six weeks of arguing. **Her wedding ring feels impossibly heavy on her finger.** She adds the dishwasher to his column, photographs the page, and sends it to him before she can change her mind.

His reply arrives while she is clearing the breakfast things. He wants the cutlery basket too. She hadn't thought of it as a separate item. She writes it under the dishwasher and sends another photograph.

## No-Hits

'Who left these on my desk?' Leah asked, holding up the little knitted socks. Ben's cheeks reddened. She had been about to thank the whole shift, but when she saw his face she put the socks away and waited until the others had gone. 'They're beautiful,' she told him then. 'Your mum taught you, didn't she?'

My hands were still trembling after an hour with the hedge trimmer. Every time I brought the little screw toward the hinge, it rattled against the hole. I put the screwdriver on the bench and went inside for tea. By the time I came back, I could hold the screw steady enough to start the thread.

'Her heart leapt' was the wording the newspaper printed, though the athlete had said only that she was pleased to qualify. We played the interview twice during the correction meeting. The sports editor agreed to replace the phrase in the online version and add the missing detail that she had qualified on her third and final attempt.
