# Low-Information Physical Blocking

Input: passage.

Read the complete connected sequence and generalize across wording, inflections and positions. A named place, object or actor, or an action's chronological occurrence, does not itself establish useful blocking.

Judge each movement's contribution and the presentation of the whole sequence separately: useful individual movements can coexist with a flat arrangement of actions. Empty hesitation, gaze choreography and body-action cues remain eligible when they meet this definition.

An identifiable physical, perceptual, social or narrative function needs no later action. Another useful detail or nearby event protects only its own contribution. Merely naming a feeling or event can leave the candidate interchangeable.

Assess adopted narration and ordinary dialogue by function; preserve wording examined or documented as wording. Multiple checks may apply to the same passage.
