# Borrowed Jargon

Identify both what ordinary activity happens and the imported technical label decorating it. The defect is a technical-sounding label used for prestige around an understandable activity. Generalize across technical domains, word forms and subjects.

Preserve literal technical work, established domain terms and ordinary business uses that name an operation or constraint. Technical work can happen outside software. Ordinary causative "engineer" and implementation uses of "operationalize" can be meaningful. A word's technical origin or an available simpler synonym alone establishes no defect.

Preserve analogies that specify corresponding operations and explain their use. Merely calling worries bugs does not explain a debugging procedure. A recoverable activity with a decorative analogy is a hit; an unresolved figurative claim belongs to unexplained metaphor. Familiar decorative technical expressions stay here even when conventional; generic nontechnical sayings belong to cliche. Judge adopted quotations normally and examined wording as examined wording.

## Development Examples

### Hit

Could we use Friday's staff meeting to **refactor our office etiquette**? I'd like us to agree that everyone washes their own mugs and wipes the kitchen counter after lunch. Please send me any other shared-space requests by Thursday so I can put them on the agenda before the meeting.

### Hit

Rowan's workshop focuses on **recalibrating the leadership mindset**. Its trainer asks each participant to describe a difficult conversation and rehearse a calmer response with a partner. Elm Training offers a lecture on the same subject, so I prefer Rowan for the chance to practice before returning to work.

### No-Hit

Please refactor the welcome-email renderer so the two account types use the same address-formatting function. The current copies disagree about where to put the postcode. Keep the customer-facing wording unchanged, and include examples for both account types in the review so we can check that the shared function handles each format.

### No-Hit

The editorial team has bandwidth for two more reports this month. Each editor has four working days free after the annual review, and a report normally takes four days to check. Please send the shorter transport study first and hold the other requests until we confirm next month's allocation.
