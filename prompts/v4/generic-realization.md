# Generic Realization

Find every claim that understanding, discovery, recognition, or revelation has occurred while its content remains unspecified. This includes indirect announcements such as a clue settling a question, a mystery yielding its secret, or a document making something intelligible. The defect is the missing discovery, regardless of wording.

First distinguish the writing's own discovery claim from commentary about writing. A reviewer criticizing an empty realization, a plot summary describing withheld knowledge, or a chapter titled "Revelation" is NO. Judge the passage's assertion, rather than treating a phrase it critiques or names as an independent assertion.

Read the whole passage and identify what was learned. Return NO when the discovery is communicated before or after its announcement, even without an explicit "realized that" sentence. Facts, a correction of an earlier belief, or an action can make the answer clear. Ordinary recognition can identify a person, voice, or handwriting without explaining every association.

An explicit conclusion such as "they returned and left again" supplies the discovery. Preserve a stated finding even when another check could criticize its precision: "the research revealed an enormous cost" states the finding; its missing amount belongs to significance or measurement.

- NO: The cable was unplugged. That explained the blank screen. The explanation is already supplied.
- NO: He thought the lid was jammed until she unscrewed it clockwise. His puzzled expression disappeared. The action supplies the discovery.
- NO: "Then I understood everything" is a vague ending; the editor asked what the character learned. This discusses wording as an example.
- NO: The investigation might reveal the cause. This anticipates a discovery rather than claiming one occurred.
- NO: He saw the cake and candles. "Now I know why you invited me." The occasion is inferable.
- NO: Her father's suitcase was gone. She understood and set one fewer place. His departure is inferable.

Return YES when the passage supplies only the discovery's subject, source, trigger, or incidental details. Merely naming a letter, chart, or meeting leaves what it revealed unspecified.

- YES: **The third voicemail supplied the missing insight.** Fran filed it under Tuesday.
- YES: **The reason for the delay became apparent during our call.** We spoke for twelve minutes.
- YES: **The documentary revealed what the city had overlooked.** It lasted seventy-eight minutes.
- YES: Reading the archive, **she finally saw why the mayor had resigned.** She put the folder away. The archive is the source; the reason remains unspecified.

Preserve editorial discussion of withheld information, openly unanswered questions, empathy, and acknowledgment of requests. Generic lessons, circular explanations, and mood changes belong to other checks unless an unspecified discovery is also claimed. Quoted dialogue is judged by the same discovery-content test.
