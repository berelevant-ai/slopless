# Inflated Evaluation

Does the writer or narrator make or endorse an evaluation whose strength exceeds the basis the passage supplies? Catch exaggerated praise or condemnation, unsupported comparative judgments, inflated importance or impact, and confidence in future success that the described circumstances cannot justify.

First establish whose assessment the passage advances. Rejecting an inflated quotation, proposing its replacement, or reporting someone else's assessment without adopting it is a no-hit. A fictional character's limited belief remains that character's belief. Judge the writer's operative recommendation or endorsement, including an assessment first attributed to someone else and then adopted.

Then compare the evaluation with the basis supplied for it. An ordinary adjective can inflate a claim: a more or less sensible, practical, honest, thoughtful, or useful option still asserts a comparative judgment. Catch these unsupported evaluations even when the wording is restrained. Describing an object or naming two alternatives leaves their relative merits unestablished. An incidental change also needs a relevant connection to any claimed improvement in prospects or performance.

Preserve modest personal preferences and proportionate judgments with a relevant basis. A concrete change can support a comparison when it explains the quality being judged; numbers and formal tests are optional. Preserve scoped awards, explained conditional forecasts, conventional technical meanings, and literal nonevaluative facts. Read the complete passage for support; unrelated facts leave a separate claim unsupported. A figurative phrase or an unspecified takeaway alone belongs to another check. Sweeping rankings of the lessons gained from adversity or ordinary moments remain eligible.

## Hits

- "For **a more honest account of the strike**, read Doyle's newsletter." The recommendation gives no basis for comparative honesty.
- "Missing that sales meeting has **permanently destroyed his prospects in this industry**." One setback becomes a certain lifetime outcome.
- "**Defeat is sport's finest instructor, teaching preparation at a depth that even a hard-earned victory cannot reach.**" The sweeping ranking exceeds the account given.

## No-Hits

- "I prefer this strike diary; the writer admits when he gets a date wrong." A modest preference has a relevant basis.
- "The courier's late arrival spoiled the launch photos because the samples were still in the van. Calling it a disaster for that morning's shoot seems fair to me." The judgment fits the stated event and scope.
