# Vague Attribution

Does the writer invoke other people's expertise, research, evidence, consensus, or judgment as support while leaving the relevant source or observed account unspecified? Find all instances of this defect, including indirect appeals and unfamiliar wording.

First distinguish the writer's operative appeal from documentation of another's argument. Reporting how a brochure uses an authority claim, examining that wording, or rejecting it is a no-hit unless the writer adopts the appeal. Criticism is optional for documentation. Descriptions of a document's reasoning, its attributed recommendation, or a response asking for support can remain entirely documentary. Conversely, an ordinary assertion such as "Experts recommend this approach" uses the experts as support even without explicit agreement. A reference to a document elsewhere does not automatically turn the writer's own appeal into documentation.

For an appeal the writer advances, identify the claim and the relevant source or account supplied for it. Anonymous specialists, professional opinion, received wisdom, and unspecified studies remain eligible even when the wording is cautious. A profession, broad setting, or mention of an interview or publication alone leaves an unnamed expert unidentified. Singular experts need relevant identification just as groups do. Numbers, names, technical detail, and links elsewhere protect only the appeal they resolve.

A contextual description can identify a particular source without a proper name or formal citation. Preserve relevant identifiable advice, bounded direct observations, and explained methods or results. Information supplied later in the passage can resolve an initially vague reference. Ordinary factual explanations that invoke no unidentified authority are no-hits.

Judge source identification rather than exaggerated confidence, the truth of the claim, or whether the source can be verified externally. An identified person's irrelevant status belongs to prestige padding.

## Hits

- "**Studies appear to suggest that standing meetings reduce mistakes in shift handovers.** On that basis, the depot should remove the chairs from its morning briefing room." Hedging leaves the research unspecified.
- "For the museum extension, **the professional consensus prefers the stone facade**." The judgment relies on unidentified specialists.
- "**Transport research puts the reduction in near misses at eighteen percent when cyclists wear reflective ankle bands.** Our club will include a pair in every new member's welcome pack." A numerical result still needs an identifiable source or account.

## No-Hits

- "The technician who installed our ventilation unit recommends cleaning its intake mesh every month." The described relationship identifies a relevant source.
- "We sent five identical bookings with the same reservation token. The old service allocated five seats; the patched service allocated one. That comparison supports enabling the retry fix." The passage supplies the observed account.
- `The fundraising leaflet quotes a specialist saying, "Music lessons improve children's concentration." It presents that claim as the reason to give every pupil an instrument.` The writer documents the leaflet's argument.
