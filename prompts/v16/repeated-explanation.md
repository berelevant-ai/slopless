# Repeated Explanation

Compare the earlier explanation with the later occurrence's role. Flag repeated explanations, conclusions or recommendations adding neither meaning nor useful function, including those beside useful new material.

Require intervening substantive content or a distinct later section function. Paragraph breaks alone are insufficient. Adjacent duplication belongs to redundancy.

Preserve explanations applied to later items, precautions renewed at another procedural step, and recommendations synthesizing intervening comparisons. Their application can change while facts repeat. Preserve useful recaps, checklists, contrasts and purposeful narrative callbacks. Require their function in the complete writing; headings or closing positions alone establish none.

## Hits

### Example 1

> Hi Tomas,
>
> **Please keep the current camera rather than exchange it. The replacement uses the same mounting plate, so swapping units would leave the loose fixing that lets the picture move.**
>
> I have arranged a technician for Thursday between nine and eleven. She will bring the correct wall plugs and check the cable where it passes through the bracket. Reception has your mobile number for access; please tell them if someone else will be there.
>
> The recording you sent is clear enough for her to use. I have attached it to the job, along with the photograph of the wall. You can delete the public sharing link now.
>
> **An exchange is therefore the wrong option here: keep this camera.**
>
> The technician will send the visit report to this address. I will leave the ticket open until you have checked the picture after the work.
>
> Regards,
> Beth

### Example 2

> Hello Mei,
>
> The missing puzzle piece is being replaced free of charge. **We use the production code on the box to match the cut, because pieces from another printing can have the same picture but different shapes.** Please send a clear photograph of that code, which is printed beside the barcode.
>
> You can keep the rest of the puzzle assembled. There is no need to return it or separate the finished sections. The replacement team asks for the piece's approximate position; your description of the upper-left window is enough, and I have added it to the request.
>
> The team dispatches replacement pieces on Fridays in a small rigid envelope. If the envelope will not fit through your letterbox, the post office may leave a collection card. We can use your neighbour's address if that is easier, as long as you confirm the recipient's name.
>
> **Matching the box's production code lets us send the correct shape, since a different print run can show the identical image with a different piece cut.** Once the photograph arrives I will release the request to the team.
>
> Kind regards,
> Paula

## No-Hits

### Example 1

> # Friday workshop handover
>
> The morning session ends at twelve. Put the repaired items on the collection bench with their numbered tickets attached. Anything still awaiting a part goes on the rear shelf, with the owner's phone number inside the folded ticket.
>
> The afternoon volunteers arrive at one. Leave the key with library reception during the gap. The front door must remain locked while the room is unattended.
>
> Tea money goes in the small tin, and receipts go underneath it. The cash total belongs on the handover sheet beside the attendance count.
>
> ## Before you leave
>
> - Put completed repairs on the collection bench.
> - Move unfinished jobs to the rear shelf.
> - Record attendance and cash on the handover sheet.
> - Lock the door and leave the key at reception.
>
> That gives the afternoon shift a single departure checklist to check against the room.
