# Wordiness

Find an unnecessarily elaborate expression of a single point. Work out a shorter wording that preserves its meaning, qualifications, actor, timing and useful emphasis. Require an identifiable padded construction, rather than treating every sentence with a shorter paraphrase as defective.

Cover verbose cause, condition and purpose connectors, layered requests, generic nominalized actions, and nested identity, capability or role constructions. Judge their function across unfamiliar wording and settings. A genuine defect remains a hit beside informative details.

Preserve ordinary useful formal language, technical terms, meaningful capability or uncertainty, actual named operations or outputs, and decisions whose deliberation matters. Preserve unspecified actors when shortening. Long words, passive voice and nominalizations alone establish no defect.

Duplicated information belongs to redundancy. A disposable announcement around a supplied point belongs to unnecessary framing. This check needs additional unnecessary elaboration of the expression itself. Quotation marks protect wording being documented or examined; wording adopted by the writer still requires judgment.

## Hits

### Example 1

> Suppose twenty classes are assigned by lottery to use one of two textbooks. We compare their average test scores at the end of term. The difference between the two group averages **is what constitutes** our estimate of the textbook's effect.
>
> The lottery assigns whole classes rather than individual pupils, so pupils in the same class share both a teacher and a treatment. The uncertainty calculation must account for that grouping; treating every pupil as an independent observation would make the estimate appear more precise than it is.

### Example 2

> **Due to the fact that** the loading bay will be resurfaced, deliveries need to use the front entrance next week. Small parcels can come through reception. Pallets will need a timed slot so the driver can unload without blocking the pavement.
>
> I've put the temporary unloading schedule in the shared folder. Please add any expected pallets by Thursday lunchtime.

### Example 3

> The analysis **gives consideration to** household size by reporting spending per person as well as spending per household. Both measures are useful here: a larger household may spend more in total while spending less for each member. The appendix shows how shared purchases were allocated.

## No-Hits

### Example 1

> I've checked the meter readings on your account. The reading submitted on 6 September was entered against the upstairs meter, which explains the higher estimate on this bill.
>
> I can make an adjustment to the balance once you send a photo showing the downstairs meter's serial number and current reading. Please include both in the same photo if you can. Your payment date will stay the same while we sort this out.

### Example 2

> Northbank's scanner has the capability to read labels through the clear sleeves we use on returned boxes. The less expensive model needed the sleeve removed in four of our twenty trials. Both connected to the stock system without an adapter.
>
> For the returns bench, I'd choose Northbank. Removing sleeves slowed the test down, and we would need a bin beside the desk for the discarded plastic. The cheaper scanner would still suit the dispatch station, where the labels are exposed.

### Example 3

> The last bus had gone by the time we reached the stop. We stood under the shelter while Len checked the timetable on his phone. There was another route from the hospital, but it meant a walk uphill with the suitcase. After a few minutes we made the decision to call a taxi. It arrived before Len had finished putting the broken wheel back in his bag.
