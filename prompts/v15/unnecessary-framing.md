# Unnecessary Framing

Find a substantive point wrapped in a disposable announcement about that point, its value, the writer's candor, or the act of presenting it. The point is supplied; the surrounding announcement does no useful work for its interpretation or organization. A single instance qualifies, including one within otherwise informative writing.

Cover scene-setting prefaces, honesty announcements, graded answer or solution labels, importance announcements, instructions to contemplate the point, self-answering rhetorical questions, redundant previews, academic contribution announcements, compression announcements, and stock endings. Generalize the function across wording and punctuation.

Judge what the suspected frame contributes in this target. Removing it must preserve the point and useful context. Preserve informative topic labels, scope, priorities, qualifications, methodological explanations, navigation, dependencies, and actual instructions. A label identifying a relevant subject or role can organize information. An adjective describing a meaningful property can contribute information.

Distinguish an instruction to perform a needed observation from an announcement telling readers to appreciate the writer's point. Preserve a real question, clarification, exchange, or discovery whose answer matters to the participants. Judge staged questions used merely to introduce a point as framing.

Assess wording the passage adopts as its own communication. Preserve quotation used to record, criticize, or investigate someone else's wording. Technical detail, names, numbers, and quotation marks alone supply no exemption. An unspecified takeaway, exaggerated claim, unnecessary opposition, or repeated substantive explanation alone belongs to its own check; this check requires an additional disposable frame around supplied content.
