# Unspecified Scope

Find a claim that some work, permission, service, inquiry, offer, or responsibility has a restricted scope while leaving its actual boundary unstated. The reader is assured that limits exist but cannot tell what is included, excluded, allowed, or where the commitment stops. Find all instances, including unfamiliar paraphrases.

## Recognize The Claim

- Restriction can be expressed as a narrow question, contained offering, selective coverage, modest remit, reduced territory, constrained authority, limited involvement, or definite endpoint.
- A promise of clear limits or an agreed boundary can be empty even without words such as narrow or scope.
- A product name, meeting date, address, price, or unrelated fact supplies no boundary unless it identifies what the restriction covers.

## Read The Whole Passage

- A stated inclusion, exclusion, condition, subject, or division of responsibility supplies a useful boundary. Ordinary qualitative language suffices.
- Adjacent sentences can explain the first sentence's restriction. Read references to settled decisions and remaining choices together.
- Acknowledging that coverage is undecided and describing how it will be established is useful planning. Flag an assertion that the scope is already limited or defined while its contents remain missing.
- A contrast between different jobs belongs to unspecified relationship unless it also asserts an unspecified restriction.
- Preserve literal physical width and established technical meanings explained through behavior or a definition.
- Preserve wording quoted for criticism, editing, or discussion as an expression. A report of someone actually making an empty scope claim remains eligible.
- When a critic identifies evasive scope wording and asks for its missing contents, judge that critical observation as useful. A stated privacy boundary or division of work already tells the reader what is reserved.

## Examples

- YES: Our involvement **has a firm stopping point**. The office opens at nine.
- YES: The revised investigation **covers a smaller field**. The report will arrive tomorrow.
- NO: Our involvement ends when the tenant receives the keys; later maintenance goes to the landlord.
- NO: The review is narrower now. The budget is settled, leaving only the choice between the two proposed sites.
- NO: We still need to decide which villages qualify. The survey will establish the service area.
- NO: The editor crossed out 'a selective offering' and asked which repairs the shop accepts.
- NO: The correspondent calls the council's 'contained role' evasive and asks which decisions it can make.
- NO: I discuss my public lectures. Questions about my patients stay private.
