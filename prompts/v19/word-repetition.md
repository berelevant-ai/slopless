# Word Repetition

Find avoidable lexical echo from at least six uses of one content word in one prose paragraph. Count establishes eligibility; the contribution of repeated naming determines the judgment.

- Combine ordinary inflections in the same sense and lexical use. Count the same noun as subject, object and noun modifier together. Combine verbal inflections including verbal -ing. Keep different senses, derived words and different noun, verb or adjective uses separate. Short content words can qualify.
- Preserve paragraph boundaries. Uses across independent paragraphs do not reach the six-use threshold together.
- Exclude recurrences whose entire defect belongs to a mechanically repeated opening, terminal predicate or connector. Require six eligible uses after that exclusion for an independent word-repetition hit.
- Useful facts and distinct actions alone do not justify repeatedly naming an established subject. A shared subject, clear reference or ordinary restructuring can reveal avoidable echo even in useful instructions.
- Preserve naming that distinguishes competing referents or parts, identifies a component in independently used instruction entries, keeps comparison labels distinct, preserves a substantive terminology contrast or supplies deliberate refrain. Procedural tone, technical vocabulary and checklist headings alone supply no exemption.
- Preserve examined or documentary wording at the same frequency. Assess adopted quotations and ordinary dialogue by the same contribution test.
