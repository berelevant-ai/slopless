# Sentence Frame Repetition

Find mechanically repeated lexical sentence frames, including unfamiliar wording and internal phrases. Read the complete connected sequence.

- Require three instances of an ordinary multiword opening, distinctive recurring subject or internal frame. An internal modal-plus-still construction can repeat despite changed subjects and verbs.
- Two adjacent sentences qualify only when both repeat the same explicit answer, fix, goal, lesson, point, result, trick or equivalent takeaway wrapper. Other frames require three.
- Count lexical framing, rather than merely the grammatical shape subject-verb-object. A common pronoun, necessary name or grammatical word alone establishes no defect.
- Then judge what the recurring form contributes. Preserve parallel comparisons, useful progression, independently meaningful instruction organization and deliberate refrain. Useful facts elsewhere leave an independently mechanical wrapper eligible.
- Connect adjacent paragraphs when the construction continues. Separate sections, records or dialogue turns establish separate sequences unless explicitly connected.
- Connector-only recurrence belongs to the transition check; a weak repeated terminal predicate belongs to the ending check. Identify a separately defective opening or internal frame on its own merits.
- Preserve wording reproduced for examination or documentation. Judge adopted quotations and ordinary dialogue normally.

Test actual matching WORDS, rather than similar sentence structure or a generally repetitive idea. An inner phrase has further words after it in every participating statement; a generic final verb phrase belongs to the ending check. Ordinary grammatical prefixes alone do not qualify. Repeated modal-plus-still wording can qualify internally, but distinct capabilities or conditions can justify that parallelism. Preserve a comparison in which each repeated frame identifies a different concrete case, duty or outcome, even when another part of the passage contains genuine slop.
