# Empty Takeaway

Find every lesson, answer, recommendation, or concluding point that is announced or praised while its content remains empty. Generic virtues, favorable judgments, and instructions merely to be better leave the reader without a useful point.

First identify the passage's own advice. Editorial discussion criticizing, quoting, cutting, or replacing empty advice is NO. Judge the discussion rather than lifting its criticized phrase out as a separate recommendation. This remains NO when the discussion only asks whether the phrase has content and supplies no replacement. A script or scene discussed as an example of an evasive answer is also NO. Ordinary quoted advice still receives the same content test as narration.

Read the complete passage for what the reader should learn or do. A substantive point can be a short instruction, diagnosis, factual answer, or conclusion. It needs neither an extended explanation nor a polished introduction. Preserve a virtue word whose specific operational meaning is supplied before or after it.

- NO: Tip: disconnect the battery. A concrete action is specified.
- NO: The answer is A flat. A factual answer is supplied.
- NO: Be patient: let the glue set overnight before removing the clamps. The instruction explains patience.
- NO: The editor rejects "seek balance" and replaces it with an instruction to close the valve before removing the cover. This critiques and revises advice.
- NO: We are checking whether "live boldly" makes any concrete recommendation. This asks about wording rather than advising boldness.
- NO: The script uses "stay positive" as the bureaucrat's non-answer, then shows the questioner repeating her request. This analyzes an evasion.

A directly described failure or contrast can supply the lesson implicitly. Preserve the takeaway when the passage makes a concrete behavior to repeat or avoid clear, even without stating the instruction. Several implementations of that lesson may be possible; an exact repair procedure is optional.

- NO: The labelled crates reached the right customers; the unlabelled ones were delivered to the wrong addresses. That guides our next packing run. The implied instruction is to label the crates.
- NO: Boiling water shattered the cold glass. There is a lesson for the next wash. The described failure supplies a concrete action to avoid.

Return YES when the passage only names a topic, praises the answer, or advocates a virtue while leaving what to learn or do unspecified. Dates, objects, venues, and other incidental details cannot give an unrelated slogan practical content.

- YES: **The answer is integrity.**
- YES: **Our recommendation is excellent and deserves to guide future work.**
- YES: We attended the conference on Tuesday. **The lesson is to show up fully.**
- YES: The coach's advice was **"pursue greatness."** The passage adopts the quoted advice.

Judge this missing takeaway rather than an unspecified discovery, change, significance, or literal use of words such as point or key. Find all instances with equivalent unseen wording.
