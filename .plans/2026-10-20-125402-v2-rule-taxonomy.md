# Accepted Slopless V2 Taxonomy

Accepted on 2026-10-20: the twelve families and the consolidated empty-takeaway and vague-attribution definitions. The coverage audit below records additional issue definitions needed within these families. Detector performance is measured separately.

## Product Scope

- Slopless V2 targets standardized writing defects with fixed definitions and tested defaults; flags select checks.
- Readability measurement and business terminology stay outside this iteration. Textlint support and retained deterministic checks depend on comparisons with Jev.
- The existing Jev pilot covers three check types. The revised taxonomy and migration comparisons still require evaluation.

## Type Decisions

- **Empty takeaways** (`empty-takeaway`) belong under semantic thinness.
  - "That is the real promise." and "The lesson was simple." express the same defect: labeling a takeaway without adding useful content.
  - Consolidate this behavior from `deictic-summary`, `generic-lesson-extraction`, `lesson-framing`, and the relevant parts of `generic-signposting` and `affirmation-closers` into one evaluation type.
- **Vague attribution** (`vague-attribution`) belongs under unsupported authority.
  - "Experts agree ..." and "Research shows ..." imply support while leaving the relevant source unspecified.
  - Consolidate that behavior from `authority-padding`, `uncited-authority`, and `weasel-attribution`. Preserve relevant named sources and meaningful credentials as no-hits.
- **Empty content and unnecessary introductions have different tests.**
  - "The lesson was simple." supplies an empty takeaway.
  - "The lesson was simple: stop retries after three failures." supplies a concrete instruction inside an unnecessary introductory frame.
  - The first belongs to semantic thinness; the second can trigger boilerplate while remaining a no-hit for empty takeaways.
- **Puffery judges inflated evaluation; unsupported authority judges how support is presented.**
  - "A world-class breakthrough" is puffery.
  - "Leading experts agree" invokes unspecified authority. A passage can contain both defects and receive both labels.
- **Use wordiness instead of concision.** The question is whether words can be removed or shortened while preserving the intended meaning.
- Generate development and holdout sets per consolidated issue type. Treat equivalent wordings as variants; use separate labels when a passage has different defects.

## Required Input

- Each finalized issue definition declares `input: passage` or `input: text` alongside its family and type. This describes the context needed to judge the issue.
  - `input: passage` uses a self-contained excerpt with enough surrounding sentences, paragraphs, or headings to judge the construction.
  - `input: text` uses the complete article, story, or reply in its original order. A short complete reply still counts as a text.
- Assign input per issue type. Different types in the same family can require different inputs; define separate types when local and whole-text judgments assess different defects.
- Record additional required context in that same definition. Reader-aware checks need the preceding conversation or reader brief; source-assisted checks need the relevant sources.
- Apply these assignments when defining the identified types:
  - Empty takeaway: `input: passage`.
  - Negation reframe, including cross-sentence constructions: `input: passage`.
  - Heading/body restatement: `input: passage`, including the heading and opening together.
  - Buried answer: `input: text`, with the question or purpose the text should address.
  - Unnecessary repeated background or reader-irrelevant explanation: `input: text`, with the conversation or reader brief.
  - Repeated closers across sections: `input: text`.
- Density reporting can count passage-level detections across a text. Keep that aggregation separate from the detector's required input.
- Keep complete evaluation examples for text-level checks in `datasets/*/texts/`; use `datasets/*/passages/` for independent excerpts. Preserve their original context when creating development and holdout splits.
- These are V2 definition requirements. The legacy coverage list below remains an inventory while consolidated types and their input declarations are completed.

## Accepted Families And Existing Coverage

The twelve families classify the defect. The 106 identifiers below retain the existing coverage inventory; final labels use the consolidated issue definitions above. Broad old rules can supply behavior to several families. Review inherited examples against these definitions before adopting them as V2 labels.

- **Semantic thinness** (`semantic-thinness`): The passage announces a lesson, change, consequence, or insight while leaving the substantive point unstated. Empty takeaways belong here regardless of their sentence shape.
  - **abstract-agency-personification**: "The room seemed to understand that something important was beginning to shift." / "The strategy wanted to become more honest with itself."
  - **abstract-metaphor-claim**: "Prevention lives in rehearsal." / "Problem-solving lands after the storm."
  - **affirmation-closers**: "That is the whole point." / "That's the key."
  - **body-knows**: "The body knows what it knows." / "The body learns the pattern."
  - **contrastive-aphorism**: "The lesson is the part that sticks." / "Small habit is the part that sticks."
  - **deictic-summary**: "That is the real promise." / "That is the lesson, and it applies more widely than people think."
  - **demonstrative-emphasis**: "That is where progress lives." / "The lesson is the part that sticks."
  - **different-job-summary**: "That is a different job." / "This is a different question."
  - **empty-emphasis**: "So this part matters." / "That part matters."
  - **empty-quantification**: "The report just put a number on it." / "The dashboard simply put a number on this."
  - **empty-scene-transition**: "Everything shifted." / "It changed everything."
  - **empty-significance-occasion**: "That matters when marketing leaders choose what to do next." / "The difference matters for anyone who reads the brief."
  - **generic-lesson-extraction**: "The lesson was simple." / "The answer is consistency."
  - **generic-pressure-or-stakes**: "The stakes felt real." / "The pressure sat in the room."
  - **generic-realization**: "She realized the truth." / "They understood what mattered."
  - **generic-signposting**: "The useful conclusion is simple." / "The clear answer is consistency."
  - **hollow-significance**: "That mattered more than it seemed." / "It changed everything."
  - **lesson-framing**: "The lesson was simple." / "The takeaway here is clear."
  - **missing-conversation-plan**: "The content plan is missing the conversation after the query." / "The page plan lacks the follow-up path after the search."
  - **observer-guidance**: "That is where progress lives." / "And that is where the work begins."
  - **point-is-frame**: "The point is not performance." / "The point is to be honest enough to see what is there."
  - **real-work-begins**: "The real work begins." / "The real work begins when we stop hiding behind noise."
  - **recursive-meaning-frame**: "This moment matters because it invites us to rethink what matters." / "The work ahead opens space for more meaningful work."
  - **something-shifted**: "Something had shifted." / "Everything changed."
  - **superficial-analysis**: ", highlighting the city's commitment to sustainable growth." / ", underscoring the importance of simplicity."
  - **truth-answer-moves**: "Eventually the truth surfaces." / "The answer arrives as a body sensation."
  - **vague-connective-payoff**: "And that is where the work begins." / "That is why it matters."
  - **vague-summary-cost**: "All of that gets expensive when nobody knows which problem they are solving." / "All this gets wasteful when no one knows what question they are answering."
  - **vague-threshold-change**: "Things got harder." / "Everything became clearer."
- **Puffery and overstatement** (`puffery-and-overstatement`): The prose praises, dramatizes, or generalizes beyond what its concrete description supports.
  - **formulaic-challenges**: "Despite these challenges, it remains well positioned for continued growth." / "Even with these hurdles, ongoing innovation will help it realize its full potential."
  - **puffery-evaluative-claim**: "The renowned architect changed the city forever." / "The product represents an unprecedented breakthrough."
  - **universalizing-claims**: "Everyone wants the checklist to feel simple." / "Most people expect the process to explain itself."
- **Unsupported authority** (`unsupported-authority`): The prose substitutes unnamed experts, unspecified research, or gratuitous credentials for an explanation readers can assess.
  - **authority-padding**: "The evidence is strongest when the team records decisions before launch." / "The broader research backs the simpler retention plan."
  - **humble-bragger**: "As someone who has spent years in this field ..." / "As someone who has worked with leading teams ..."
  - **uncited-authority**: "Research suggests many parents change the routine slowly." / "The evidence suggests that collaboration drives meaningful change."
  - **weasel-attribution**: "a growing body of evidence suggests" / "according to medical experts"
- **Staged contrasts** (`staged-contrasts`): A rejection, reversal, or opposition adds rhetorical drama while contributing little useful distinction.
  - **abstract-contrast**: "The point is not control. The point is repair." / "Prevention lives in rehearsal, not in the lecture."
  - **blame-reframe**: "The gap comes from practice, not shame." / "Treat the review as learning instead of blame."
  - **negation-reframe**: "The plan is not complicated. The plan is unfinished." / "We do not sell software. We sell outcomes."
- **Boilerplate and signposting** (`boilerplate-and-signposting`): A stock preface, announcement, or ending delays or decorates a substantive point the passage supplies.
  - **academic-formula-frames**: "The paper contributes a fresh perspective to the ongoing dialogue about pedagogical innovation." / "The findings indicate that mentorship can unlock new forms of academic belonging."
  - **boilerplate-conclusion**: "Ultimately, the core fact is this: the fee doubles after six months." / "Ultimately, the whole trick is to assign one owner to each open ticket."
  - **boilerplate-framing**: "When it comes to onboarding, the first hour matters." / "Some common reasons include missing owners and unclear deadlines."
  - **colon-dramatic**: "And then it hit me: everything changed." / "The verdict was simple: ship it."
  - **false-question**: "Isn't that the point?" / "Is that not the whole goal?" as section endings.
  - **llm-openers**: "In today's fast-paced world ..." / "In the ever-evolving landscape ..."
  - **significance-density**: Repeated "Notably ... Importantly ... Crucially ..." / Repeated "It's worth noting ... It's important to remember ... It bears mentioning ..."
  - **solution-boring-frame**: "The solution is boring: move copy into raw HTML." / "The answer is boring: render the price before hydration."
  - **summative-closer**: "At the end of the day, the customer needs the signed copy before Monday." / "The bottom line is simple: cap retries at three."
- **Stock wording and jargon** (`stock-wording-and-jargon`): Cliches, habitual filler, borrowed technical metaphors, or unnecessarily elaborate vocabulary replace more fitting language.
  - **academic-boilerplate**: "bridge theory and practice" / "fertile ground"
  - **actually-overuse**: Repeated "actually" in commentary / Repeated "Actually, ..." sentence openings.
  - **cliches**: "back to square one" / "navigate the complexities of"
  - **corporate-speak**: "low-hanging fruit" / "drill-down"
  - **genre-cliches**: "a culinary journey" / "a feast for the senses"
  - **jargon-faker**: "debug your mindset" / "optimizing for authenticity"
  - **llm-vocabulary**: Clusters such as "vibrant landscape ... delve ... intricate tapestry" / "Additionally ... highlights ... enduring commitment ... valuable ..."
  - **llm-vocabulary-density**: Clusters such as "scalable approach unlocks nuanced insight across the ecosystem" / "transformative strategy elevates impact across every workflow"
  - **prohibited-phrases**: "In today's fast-paced world" / "Let's dive in"
  - **prohibited-words**: Configured stock words such as "delve" / "elevate".
  - **quietly-filler**: "The change quietly reshaped the strategy." / "Quietly, the rules changed."
  - **quietly-overuse**: Repeated "quietly" across an article / Repeated "quietly" across a chapter.
  - **self-help-cliches**: "abundance mindset" / "be authentically you"
  - **seo-filler**: "Let's delve into" / "In today's fast-paced ..."
  - **silently-filler**: "The change silently reshaped the strategy." / "Silently, the rules changed."
  - **simplicity**: Clusters such as "utilize numerous tools to facilitate" / Repeated complex substitutes where plain words would work.
  - **skunked-terms**: "fulsome" / "decimate" from the disputed-word list.
  - **tortured-phrases**: "counterfeit consciousness" for artificial intelligence / "bosom peril" for breast cancer
- **Wordiness and redundancy** (`wordiness-and-redundancy`): Words or modifiers can be removed or shortened while preserving the relevant meaning.
  - **redundancy**: "repeated again" / "basic fundamentals"
  - **uncomparables**: "very unique" / "most complete"
  - **wordiness**: "due to the fact that" / "because of the fact that"
- **Excessive hedging** (`excessive-hedging`): Unnecessary or stacked qualifications obscure the claim. Preserve qualifications that express meaningful uncertainty.
  - **hedge-stacking**: "Maybe, just maybe, the alley would stay quiet." / "The migration might perhaps finish after the review."
  - **softening-language**: Stacked uncertainty such as "It could potentially possibly help." Preserve qualifications that express necessary limits or uncertainty.
- **Rhythm and repetition** (`rhythm-and-repetition`): Mechanical repetition in sentence shape, transitions, lists, or nearby wording.
  - **flat-action-cadence**: "We go. We rest. We look. We wait." / "Mira ran. Eli froze. The teacher pointed. Children searched."
  - **formal-transition-density**: Repeated transitions such as "However ... Therefore ... Ultimately ..." / "Moreover ... Furthermore ... Nevertheless ..."
  - **fragment-stacking**: "Too broad. Deeply vague. More like a label than a plan." / "No implant. No brain surgery."
  - **repeated-predicate-end**: "Content can help. Technical fixes can help. Consulting can help." / Three nearby sentences ending "is important."
  - **repeated-sentence-starts**: "The report says ... The report says ... The report says ..." / Several nearby sentences starting "This means ..."
  - **triple-sentence-repeat**: "Home starts. Home waits. Home moves." / "Home holds. Home turns. Home closes."
  - **triple-word-repeat**: "The platform is fast, reliable, and secure." / "The product creates a flywheel of trust, engagement, and measurable impact."
  - **word-repetition**: Six uses of "home" in one paragraph / Seven uses of "platform" in one paragraph, against the default maximum of five.
- **Narrative craft** (`narrative-craft`): Stock emotion, atmosphere, and physical staging that substitute for useful scene information. Applies to factual storytelling as well as fiction.
  - **abstract-personification-line**: "Fear had no place in her schedule." / "Doubt entered the conversation."
  - **body-action-density**: Repeated movement such as "climbed ... pulled ... walked ... crouched" / Repeated body cues such as "ears twitched ... tail angled ... paws shifted"
  - **body-emotion-shorthand**: "Her chest felt a sudden heavy thump." / "Cassia's chest felt a heavy thump."
  - **emotion-as-substance**: "The anger finally melted out of her posture." / "Relief washed over Cassia."
  - **emotion-telling**: "She was still very angry." / "She was afraid of the audit."
  - **empty-atmosphere-shift**: "The yard felt suddenly cold." / "The air felt different now."
  - **empty-beat**: "Liska hesitated for a second." / "Cassia kept pace."
  - **empty-emotional-weather**: "Silence sat between them." / "A quiet tension settled over the room."
  - **empty-scene-state**: "The yard was empty." / "The room was quiet."
  - **fake-timestamps**: Legacy clock-detail heuristic, such as "5:47 PM." Evaluate gratuitous narrative precision while preserving useful times.
  - **fixed-metaphor-frame**: "The south pier was a graveyard of shadows and salt spray." / "The courtyard was a maze of dust and smoke."
  - **gaze-choreography**: "Her gaze fell to the ground." / "His gold eyes measured the line of small chests."
  - **low-information-beat-density**: "She turned her head. Her eyes looked tired. She crossed her arms and waited." / "Cassia glanced at Arden. Her throat tightened. She stepped back."
  - **low-information-physical-blocking**: "They just stood there." / "She looked around."
  - **narrative-cliches**: "Her heart pounded in her chest." / "A shiver ran down her spine."
  - **perception-verb-density**: "She watched him leave. She looked at Arden. He was looking back." / "He glanced at the gate, stared at the yard, then gazed down the lane."
  - **sensory-instead-of-frame**: "The courtyard smelled of disturbed dust and old ash instead of hot metal." / "The yard smelled of dry tea and old hemp instead of oil."
  - **silence-as-actor**: "The silence was not empty." / "The silence felt arranged."
  - **summary-fog-transition**: "The rest of the drill was a blur." / "Everything after that was a haze."
- **Generated residue** (`generated-residue`): Assistant wrappers, model self-reference, and unfinished generated markers left in publishable text.
  - **artifact-placeholders**: `:contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}` / `sandbox:/mnt/data/report.md`
  - **llm-disclaimer**: "As a language model, I cannot verify that." / "I don't have access to real-time data."
  - **response-wrapper**: "Please let me know if you need a deeper summary." / "I can provide general information about queue backfills."
- **Typography and formatting** (`typography-and-formatting`): Standardized presentation checks whose usefulness and execution method need separate evaluation.
  - **em-dashes**: An em dash joining words without spaces / A sentence repeatedly interrupted by unspaced em dashes.
  - **exclamation-density**: "Amazing! Incredible!" in one paragraph / "Great! Perfect! Wonderful!" in one paragraph.
  - **hidden-unicode-controls**: `U+200B` zero-width space / `U+202E` right-to-left override.
  - **sentence-case**: "Draft Review Notes For Editors" / "The Complete Guide To Better Writing" as headings.
  - **smart-quotes**: `U+201C` and `U+201D` curly double quotes / `U+2019` curly apostrophes.

## Splitting Broad Legacy Checks

- Classify the particular construction rather than assigning an entire old module by its name.
- `generic-signposting` combines empty takeaways, reader instructions, introductory frames, and other evaluative statements.
  - Its empty-takeaway behavior contributes to semantic thinness.
  - Its introductions and reader instructions contribute to boilerplate and signposting.
- `contrastive-aphorism` combines empty standalone aphorisms and staged comparisons.
  - "The lesson is the part that sticks" contributes to semantic thinness.
  - Its less/more, paired-comparison, and evidence-limitation constructions contribute to staged contrasts.
- The inventory places each old identifier once for coverage accounting. Its listed family is not an exclusive execution boundary for every branch of that implementation.
- Apply the same split to legacy conclusion and simplicity frames: "The solution is boring" and "The bottom line is simple" contribute to empty takeaways when the passage supplies no substantive point; wrappers around a supplied instruction contribute to boilerplate.
- `empty-scene-transition`, `something-shifted`, and the vague-change part of `hollow-significance` need a shared unspecified-change definition before their datasets are rebuilt.

## Evaluation Boundaries

- Judge puffery, unsupported authority, and staged contrasts separately from whether a sentence communicates a substantive point.
- Treat source-specific scientific language, concrete comparisons, meaningful uncertainty, and purposeful repetition as relevant no-hits.
- Narrative craft applies to fiction and factual storytelling. A sentence such as "The yard was empty" requires enough context to distinguish useful observation from padding.
- Density controls reporting for the underlying issue. It does not create a separate family.
- The old `fake-timestamps` detector recognizes clock expressions, not fabrication. Review its usefulness as a narrative-detail check; preserve genuine timing information.
- Typography and formatting remains a candidate set of standardized checks. Evaluate whether each convention belongs in the product and whether code or Jev should execute it.
- Keep production rules, prompts, labels, and accepted fixtures unchanged during this taxonomy audit. Implement and evaluate the accepted organization through the development workflow in `AGENTS.md`.

## Outside This Iteration

- **Readability:** `coleman-liau`, `flesch-kincaid`, `gunning-fog`, and `paragraph-length`.
- **Business terminology:** `forbidden-terms`, `recommended-terms`, and `required-terms`.
- **Decorative bold:** Excluded by decision. Useful emphasis and bold list labels remain permitted; judge redundant label wording under wordiness and redundancy.
- `word-repetition` belongs under rhythm and repetition because its purpose is to detect repeated wording.

## Coverage Audit

### Humanizer Checklist

Compared all 26 numbered sections in [Humanizer 3.1.0](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/d4b81278e6604e237bf32f0f7ce5c217aee94269/SKILL.md). Each section below records its family assignment or exclusion and identifies behavior that needs an explicit issue definition beyond the existing inventory.

1. **Not X but Y:** Staged contrasts; covered by the negation-reframe and abstract-contrast definitions, including cross-sentence variants.
2. **One-line closers and dramatic fragments:** Semantic thinness for empty takeaways; rhythm and repetition for stacked fragments.
   - Add explicit definitions for a closer that repeats earlier content, under wordiness and redundancy, and theatrical capitalization or punctuated words, under typography and formatting.
3. **Sayings that sound deep:** Semantic thinness for hollow aphorisms and metaphors; boilerplate and signposting for a preface around a substantive claim.
4. **Staged run-up before the point:** Boilerplate and signposting; covered by the introductory-frame and reader-instruction behavior already identified.
5. **Arguing with no one:** Staged contrasts for imagined objections and rejected alternatives; boilerplate and signposting for empty defensive prefaces.
   - Existing contrast rules and "to be clear" wording provide partial coverage. Define the broader imagined-objection behavior and preserve relevant objections and genuine alternatives.
6. **Forced triads:** Rhythm and repetition; existing three-part lists and repeated sentence structures provide the coverage to generalize at passage scale.
7. **Repeated sentence openings:** Rhythm and repetition; existing repeated-start and cadence checks provide partial behavior coverage.
   - Define repeated subjects with changing predicates explicitly. The repeated-start detector needs three occurrences of a multiword opening; the older triple-opener branch excludes pronoun-only openings.
8. **Dashes as the universal connector:** Typography and formatting; the family fits, while the legacy detector checks only unspaced em dashes.
   - Define repeated dash use separately from spacing. Humanizer also includes spaced dashes, en dashes, and double hyphens; retain the existing decision to evaluate typography separately.
9. **Stacked qualifiers:** Excessive hedging; covered by hedge-stacking and the relevant softening-language behavior.
10. **Hyphenated pairs everywhere:** Typography and formatting; add the missing compound-hyphenation candidate definition and preserve valid spellings.
11. **Passive voice and missing subjects:** Semantic thinness when a sentence hides a relevant actor or action; add this missing definition.
    - Judge the information lost. Useful passive sentences and recoverable subjects remain no-hits; "No configuration file needed" alone does not establish a defect.
12. **Overused AI words:** Stock wording and jargon; covered by the vocabulary, filler, and density checks.
13. **Inflated significance:** Puffery and overstatement for exaggerated importance; semantic thinness for empty significance; boilerplate and signposting for stock outlook sections.
14. **Vague connection or association:** Semantic thinness; add the missing definition for an unspecified relationship, such as a person's role in an organization.
    - Use the supplied context to judge whether the relationship is informative. Preserve warranted uncertainty rather than inventing a role.
15. **Shallow -ing riders:** Semantic thinness through superficial-analysis; puffery and overstatement where the added clause inflates the fact's importance.
    - Source-assisted judgments need the relevant source text to determine whether an interpretation is supported, including claims attributed to a named source. Surface wording alone supports only the stylistic part of this check.
16. **Sales language:** Puffery and overstatement; stock wording and jargon supplies the relevant promotional cliches.
17. **Borrowed authority:** Unsupported authority; vague-attribution covers unspecified experts and research.
    - Add an explicit prestige-list definition for outlet names, follower counts, or credentials used to imply support beyond their relevance. Preserve credentials establishing relevant expertise or eligibility and follower counts establishing reach or niche audience size.
18. **Avoiding is, are, and has:** Wordiness and redundancy; add an explicit definition for unnecessarily elaborate verb phrases. Preserve verbs that specify a useful role or operation.
19. **Bold as decoration:** Excluded by decision. Bold emphasis and bold-label formatting are permitted.
    - Label/body repetition remains under wordiness and redundancy when the label contributes no information; judge the wording with either formatting.
20. **Decorative headings:** Typography and formatting; sentence-case covers capitalization, while decorative symbols, repeated separators, and duplicated titles need definitions.
    - Boilerplate and signposting covers empty rhetorical heading text. Judge meaning and presentation separately.
21. **Curly quotation marks:** Typography and formatting; covered by smart-quotes, with the product convention still subject to separate evaluation.
22. **Chatbot residue:** Generated residue; covered by response-wrapper and related assistant-language checks.
23. **Knowledge-limit disclaimers and guesses:** Generated residue covers model-limit disclaimers; add explicit definitions for narrating a source gap and then filling it with a plausible guess.
    - Place this source-gap completion under generated residue, including hedged guesses. Determining whether the added claim has support requires the supplied source text; the definition and source-assisted input still need implementation and evaluation.
24. **A heading repeated in the first sentence:** Wordiness and redundancy; add heading/body restatement as an explicit issue definition requiring the heading and opening together.
25. **Writing about the document instead of its subject:** Boilerplate and signposting for redundant layout or method narration; generated residue for leaked drafting instructions.
    - Add both behaviors explicitly. Preserve useful methodology, source credits, limitations, and change descriptions where the document's purpose requires them.
26. **Re-explaining what the reader knows:** Wordiness and redundancy for repeated background or reader-irrelevant diagnosis and proof; boilerplate and signposting for unnecessary run-up that buries the answer.
    - Mark these types `input: text` and supply the preceding conversation or an explicit reader brief. Preserve reasoning that affects the reader's decision.
    - A standalone article cannot establish what a particular reader already knows. Keep this context requirement separate from checks that use the text alone.

### Audit Conclusion

- All Slopless inventory entries are accounted for. All 26 Humanizer sections have a recorded assignment or exclusion; decorative bold is excluded, while the additional issue definitions and context-dependent checks remain development requirements.
- Broaden the shared family descriptions when defining those additions: semantic thinness includes missing substantive information; wordiness and redundancy includes repeated information across passages and headings.
- Humanizer's rewrite workflow and voice matching describe editing behavior. Its quotation, proper-name, meaningful-uncertainty, and purposeful-repetition exceptions inform no-hit cases.
- Source checking and reader-context checking require their stated inputs. Establish those inputs before generating labels or claiming coverage for those behaviors.
