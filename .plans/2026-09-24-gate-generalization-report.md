# Gate generalization report: what changed, what it caught, what it cost

How to read the numbers. Every item was run over the same article corpus twice: once with the released 0.2.39 and once with the branch. "Human" is 13,680 documents written before AI assistants (21.3 million words: Wikipedia, Project Gutenberg, RFCs, PEPs, MDN, Stack Exchange, Paul Graham, a16z, government health pages). "AI" is 1,900 documents (1.76 million words) that are either generated by us or scraped and suspected AI. "+N" is the number of sentences the branch flags that the release did not. "-N" is the reverse. Human is 12 times larger than AI, so equal raw counts mean the rule fires 12 times more often on AI text per word. True positives are sentences the rule is meant to catch. False positives are sentences a careful editor would leave alone. The lists below are random samples from the added findings, not the best or worst.

## 1. lesson-framing

Before: five exact phrases ("the lesson here is clear", "the takeaway is clear", ...). After: any "the [modifier] {lesson, takeaway, fix, answer, rule, point, truth, ...} [here | for me] {is, was, remains} [adverb] {clear, simple, boring, obvious, ...}". Human +4, AI +11.

True positives: "The fix is mundane." / "The message is simple: the AI race is becoming an infrastructure race." / "The practical takeaway here is simple but transformative: focus on progress over perfection." / "But the truth is very simple:" / "The fix is obvious but I still see this in senior developers' code:" / "The answer is straightforward, you are making a severe logical error." (human forum).

False positives: "The strategy was straightforward: 'Buy the site, win the franchise, build the building.'" (Wikipedia) / "The message was quite clear." (Wikipedia biography). Only four human additions exist; those are two of them. Two more were fixed before commit: "The answer was always the same: 'Almost eighteen years.'" (Dickens) and "The rule is boring because each clause repeats the same sentence." (fixture).

## 2. summative-closer

Before: a phrase list matched only at the very start of the sentence, and any "when", "if", "with", "by" or digit anywhere silenced it. After: a leading connective is stripped first ("Ultimately, in conclusion, ..."), only a colon, "because", "since" or a digit silence it, a why-closer with a pronoun subject counts ("that's why we", "this is why it", "which is why they"), and more closers (at its core, at the end of the day, simply put, the upshot is, ...). "In the end", "in short", "in essence" were tried and removed after the corpus showed 259 human hits against 6 AI. Final: human +154, AI +63.

True positives: "That's why I built RWU." / "This is why we default to Next.js for any SEO-dependent project." / "At its core, a contract is simply a legally binding agreement between two or more parties." / "That is why these bugs matter." / "The bottom line is that online learning is a powerful tool when used intentionally." / "In conclusion, mastering the art of conversation with ChatGPT requires a combination of creativity, experimentation, and practice."

False positives: "That's why they do it." (human forum, a direct answer) / "That's why I come." / "Put simply, RTSP acts as a 'network remote control' for multimedia servers." (technical explanation) / "So that is why I am trying to learn more about where I was born." / "Long story short, I don't believe that you can 'guarantee' that you're burning only fat..." (forum answer that then explains).

## 3. generic-signposting, question and answer frames

Before: "the {adjective} {noun} is" with a fixed four-token shape and two literal lists. After: any determiner (a, the, our, my, this), a wider adjective list (right, wrong, actual, deeper, harder, uncomfortable, underlying, ...), plural nouns, an optional "here | for me | now", and is/are/was/were/isn't/becomes/remains. Human +15, AI +35.

True positives: "The uncomfortable truth is that air travel remains carbon-intensive." / "The real problem isn't the builder." / "The actual rule is:" / "But the underlying idea is still:" / "The correct frame is agents as a force multiplier on existing competence, not as a replacement for the learning curve." / "Your first priority is detecting death fast, not preventing it."

False positives: "The basic facts are not in dispute." (court opinion) / "The core problem here is that, by default, parameters in Python can either be called positionally or as a keyword argument." (PEP) / "The underlying question is why Euclid did not use this proof, but invented another." (Wikipedia) / "The interesting point here is that code transformers are not needed to execute the transformed code..." (PEP) / "The main points are: Really, really naive C++ code." (forum).

## 4. emotion-telling (now inside the opt-in narrative family)

Before: "{he, I, she, they, we, Name} {felt, was, were} [intensifier] {emotion}" with the emotion word ending the sentence and 15 emotion words. After: also "you" and contractions (I'm, she's), present tense and feel/seem/look/get, an "of/about/by/with/at/for" complement, 50 emotion words; skipped when a digit, a "to" or "that" complement, a later clause, a non-person subject, or an opening quotation mark is present. Human +121, AI +7. The rates are equal, which is why the family is now off by default.

True positives: "They're frustrated." / "I was overwhelmed." / "They're happy." / "We are excited about what comes next." / "Nobody was surprised." / "I got frustrated with insurance sites that make you hand over your phone number before showing any numbers."

False positives: "GamePro was pleased with the game's fidelity to its predecessors..." (a magazine as subject) / "Spanner was overwhelmed by the simultaneous burst." (a database) / "Cryptographers are excited too." / "Gilly is angry at Steph for keeping this from him." (plot summary) / "I was shocked at my ignorance of its spelling." (memoir) / "She was disappointed by the absence of dark-haired Hollywood actresses whom she could emulate then." (Wikipedia).

## 5. false-question

Before: five literals, all with "isn't". After: {isn't, aren't, wasn't, is ... not, are ... not} {that, this, it, those, these} [the] [whole, real, entire, ...] {point, goal, question, lesson, job, ask, ...} or {what, why, how} clause, anywhere in a section-final question. Human 0, AI 0. The construction does not occur in the corpus outside the fixtures; nothing gained, nothing lost.

## 6. Shared implementation-summary guard

Before: one of body, key, page, source, table, numbers, contract, commands, returned, slide, physics, audit, checklist anywhere in the sentence silenced seven rules. After: only technical tokens (api, cache, database, parser, token, invoice, ...) plus a passive location ("documented in the rollback checklist"). In demonstrative-emphasis, a definite copular sentence ("The X is the Y ...") is now capped at five predicate words unless the subject is a discourse noun (bottom line, fact, truth, point), and a proper noun inside the subject vetoes. Measured on demonstrative-emphasis alone: human -192 / +44, AI -62 / +16.

Removed (wanted removals): "The event loop is the place where most interoperability occurs." / "This chunk is used to pad an SCTP packet." / "The Designated Router is the endpoint of many adjacencies." / "The nucleus is the largest cellular organelle in animal cells." / "The slouch hat was the preferred choice of many soldiers, including officers."

Added, true positives (AI): "The owner matters." / "The key is this:" / "This matters for agentic coding too." / "The compiler feedback loop is the key architectural primitive." / "That is runtime-mode contract drift."

Added, false positives (human, technical docs): "This table is read-only." / "This error is returned independent of the client-supplied 'ipp-attribute-fidelity'." / "These are called the virtual link's corresponding routing table entries." / "This is specified by the loopback source and reflected by the mirror." / "This is typically referred to as 'active monitoring' of services." These were hidden before by the words table, key, source and commands; they only report when a document has three such lines.

## 7. observer-guidance

Before: the sentence had to equal one of a few literals, and "this is where X get stuck" accepted five subject nouns. After: prefix match with any tail, quantified plural human or team subjects ("most beginners", "many developers", "teams"), stall verbs, and "this is where the {abstract noun} {starts, happens, gets lost, ...}". Human +0, AI +13.

True positives: "This is where many beginners get into trouble." / "This is where most beginners get trapped." / "This is where many developers get confused." / "This is where most people fail." / "This is where the magic happens." (four different posts) / "This is where the real work begins."

False positives: none on the corpus.

## 8. empty-emphasis

Before: exact token counts (3, 4, 5, 6) and referents "part", "bit". After: "{that, this} [last, first, one, small, ...] {part, bit, detail, piece, moment, step, distinction, difference} [really, still, ...] {matters, counts, sticks, lands}." and a wider virtue-label list ("This is patience."). Human +0, AI +7.

True positives: "That distinction matters." (six different posts) / "That is growth."

False positives: none on the corpus.

## 9. universalizing-claims

Before: 24 fixed subject phrases (most people, many parents, ...) and a four-token verb window. After: {everyone, nobody, we all, all of us, ...} or {most, many, every, all, no, any} [of] {33 human-group nouns}, an optional "for", a six-token window, verbs including believe, think, need, assume, struggle, underestimate. A digit vetoes. Human +105, AI +86.

True positives: "Most developers think about SEO the wrong way." / "Many parents do not realize just how many rights they have within the special education system." / "Every developer knows the feeling — you finish a project, push it to GitHub..." / "Many people think Python was named after the snake..." / "Most people in Asia overestimate their writing skills and underestimate their speaking gaps." / "Many employees assume they are entitled to severance pay..."

False positives: "Most people feel better within a week." (health page) / "We all need clean water." / "Everyone loves her." / "Most people will understand that in this context 'cowries' means some form of currency." / "But most people find ways to feel more positive over time." (health page) / "Anyone knows the cause of my problem, or even better a solution?" (forum question).

## 10. uncited-authority

Before: the authority phrase had to open the sentence, the sentence needed eight tokens, and any parenthesis or digit silenced it. After: up to four lead-in words ("Recent studies show", "A growing body of research suggests", "Most experts agree"), five tokens, a parenthesis silences only when it holds a digit, and more phrases (studies have shown, it's no secret that, the science is clear, doctors recommend). The digit veto stays because a fixture no-hit depends on it. Human +54, AI +16.

True positives: "Multiple studies indicate that exercise can be as effective as medication for some people dealing with mild to moderate depression." / "It's no secret that how you start your morning often determines how the rest of your day unfolds." / "Research has shown that gut bacteria produce neurotransmitters like serotonin and dopamine..." / "Studies have shown that well-rested athletes demonstrate improved strength..." / "Multiple studies show that remote workers often accomplish more in less time..." / "Most doctors recommend having it checked every few years..."

False positives: "Studies have shown that the slower the spreading rate, the rougher the new oceanic crust will be" (Wikipedia geology, cited at page level) / "Research indicates a correlation between the mass of a central supermassive black hole and the extent of the globular cluster system" (Wikipedia) / "Some argue that /s, z, sʼ/ were affricated" (linguistics) / "Several studies have shown that static stretching before activity actually decreases performance." (fitness forum) / "Some experts say that over the centuries it has killed more people than all other infectious diseases combined." (Wikipedia).

## 11. demonstrative-emphasis document threshold

Tried: report at two emphasis lines per document instead of three. Result: human +749, AI +208, mostly ordinary statements ("This patent is filed in China.", "This is measured by downloads and usage."). Reverted. No change ships.

## 12. Correction-token guard for negation-reframe

Before: one of 21 words (code, red, closed, exit, audit, package, patient, api, server, ...) anywhere silenced five negation matchers. After: two of them, or one with a digit. Human +52, AI +17.

True positives: "Programming is not just about writing code—it is about understanding logic, patterns, and mathematical thinking." / "Your roadmap focuses on core value, not API plumbing." / "I realized that the problem was not just about land claiming, but about service boundaries..." / "...good work gets delayed or rejected not on merit, but on timeline." / "It's not about the code. It's about the craft." (probe).

False positives: "This value is not an error code. Instead, it is a numeric value that indicates the current status of the request." (RFC, a real correction) / "However, the start_response callable must not actually transmit the response headers. Instead, it must store them..." (PEP) / "Then she gave Mr. Darling such a look, not an angry look: she showed him the great red tear..." (Peter Pan) / "Often he is not patient nor interested in learning, he is only interested in a confirmation of what he believes" (forum) / "Donated blood is not usually transfused into a patient as whole blood. Instead, it's broken down..." (medical explanation).

## 13. boilerplate-framing openers

Before: 33 literal openers. After: "to be [perfectly, brutally, quite, ...] {clear, honest, blunt, frank, fair}" as a grammar, new openers (make no mistake, the reality is, the fact is, needless to say, let's face it, as we all know), and the reflective-opener place check now only vetoes a capitalized word after to/in/at/from/near ("back to Lisbon" vetoed, "what Slack taught us" caught). Human +75, AI +27.

True positives: "The reality is that unexpected expenses happen to everyone." / "Here's the reality: your mobility directly determines the quality of every movement you perform." / "The reality is that green tests in one browser can hide a long list of compatibility gaps." / "The reality is straightforward: traditional healthcare models are being challenged..." / "But the reality is far more welcoming."

False positives: "The fact is, Lady Bracknell, I said I had lost my parents." (Wilde dialogue) / "The fact is, I was a trifle beside myself" (Jane Eyre) / "To be fair, there were a couple of laugh-out-loud moments..." (review) / "The fact is that many of the toadstools are edible..." (1900s field guide) / "Needless to say, my imitations didn't say anything either." (Paul Graham).

## 14. superficial-analysis

Before: only "-ing" verbs after a comma ("..., reflecting the region's enduring significance"). After: also finite verbs after "and", "which", "thereby", "thus" ("and reflects", ", which underscores"). Human +20, AI +4.

True positives: "and highlighted the importance of resilience." / "and demonstrates genuine commitment to stakeholders." / ", which emphasized the importance of setting optimal values for batch size, concurrency, and timeout thresholds." / ", which illustrates the importance of the site to the Sidonian monarchy." (Wikipedia) / "and reinforced the importance of learning from his – and Simpson's – mistakes." (Wikipedia).

False positives: "and creates its own signature, using its own Token Binding private key..." (RFC, "creates" plus "key") / ", and enhances compromise resilience by mitigating key risk..." (PEP) / "and created the Expo 67 Foundation ... to commemorate the event for future generations." / "and showcases the culture and practices of the diverse aboriginal tribes of the region." (borderline: that is the construction) / ", which demonstrated the parallel influence of the cinematic newsreel." (borderline).

## 15. quietly-overuse

Before: needed four "quietly" before the per-1,000-word rate was even checked. After: two. Human +7 documents, AI +16 documents. The findings are the word itself, so there are no sentence examples; the rate gate is unchanged.

## Net picture

Narrow grammar items (1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 14, 15) add almost nothing on human text and fire on AI text. Broad items (2, 9, 10, 12, 13) add 50 to 150 human findings each across 21 million words, and the human samples above are the cost. Item 4 fires equally on both and is now opt-in. Item 6 is a net reduction of 148 human findings. Item 11 was rejected.
